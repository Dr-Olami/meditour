## Overview

Being told you need hernia surgery can feel worrying, especially if you have been living with the bulge for some time and are unsure what the operation involves. That is completely understandable. But hernia repair is one of the most common and safest surgical procedures performed worldwide, and Bangalore has become a trusted destination for patients who need this procedure done expertly at a fraction of the cost they would pay at home.

A hernia occurs when an internal organ or tissue pushes through a weakness in the muscle wall that normally holds it in place. The most common types are **inguinal hernias** (in the groin), **umbilical hernias** (around the belly button), **incisional hernias** (at the site of a previous operation), and **hiatal hernias** (where part of the stomach pushes up into the chest). You may have noticed a soft lump that appears when you stand, cough, or lift something heavy, and disappears when you lie down. Over time, that lump can become painful and the weakness can enlarge.

Without treatment, a hernia will not heal on its own — the muscle wall does not repair itself, and the gap only tends to widen with time. The real danger is **strangulation**, where the trapped tissue loses its blood supply and becomes a surgical emergency. Hernia repair surgery closes the weakness, usually with a piece of surgical mesh that reinforces the muscle wall and dramatically lowers the chance of the hernia coming back.

For most patients, the surgery is done laparoscopically — through a few tiny keyhole incisions — which means minimal scarring, less pain, and a remarkably quick recovery. Many people walk the same evening and go home within a day or two. Bangalore's accredited hospitals perform thousands of these procedures each year with outcomes that match the best international benchmarks, at prices 80 to 85% lower than in the US or UK.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

You may be reading this because you have noticed a lump in your groin or abdomen that aches after a long day, or because your doctor has examined you and confirmed a hernia. That discomfort — often worse when lifting, coughing, or standing for long periods — is the classic sign that the muscle wall has weakened and needs surgical reinforcement.

Hernia repair is recommended if you have a **symptomatic hernia** causing pain, a visible bulge, or a feeling of heaviness or pressure. Even if the discomfort is mild and intermittent, hernias tend to enlarge over time, and the risk of complications increases as the gap widens. The most serious complication is strangulation, where a loop of intestine becomes trapped and its blood supply is cut off — a life-threatening emergency that requires immediate surgery.

Your doctor may also advise surgery if you have a **large hernia** (where the gap is wide and the risk of recurrence with non-surgical management is high), a **recurrent hernia** (one that has come back after a previous repair), or an **incisional hernia** following a past abdominal operation. Hiatal hernias, which cause acid reflux and heartburn, may be repaired surgically if medication no longer controls your symptoms.

Occasionally, patients with a small, painless hernia may be offered a period of watchful waiting. However, most surgeons recommend timely repair because the hernia will not improve on its own, and operating earlier — when the defect is smaller — generally means a simpler procedure and a lower recurrence rate. Your surgeon in Bangalore will review your examination, scans, and symptoms to confirm that surgery is the right step for you.

## Pre-operative Preparation

Before you travel to Bangalore, share your ultrasound or CT report, blood test results, and a list of all medications you take with your surgical team. If you have had any previous abdominal surgeries, bring those operative notes too, as they help your surgeon plan the repair and anticipate any scarring. This allows your surgeon to assess the size and type of hernia and decide whether a laparoscopic or open approach is best.

Your doctor will likely ask you to stop blood-thinning medications — such as warfarin, clopidogrel, or aspirin — several days before surgery to reduce bleeding risk. Be honest about everything you take, including herbal supplements, because some can interfere with anaesthesia. If you smoke, try to stop at least a week before surgery, as smoking increases the risk of wound infections and delays healing. If you have a chronic cough or constipation, mention it, because both increase pressure inside the abdomen and can strain the repair.

Once you arrive in Bangalore, you will have a pre-operative assessment including blood tests, an ultrasound or repeat imaging, and an anaesthesia consultation. You will be asked to fast for 6 to 8 hours before the procedure. Most patients are scheduled for surgery within 2 to 3 days of arrival.

## Procedure Details

It is natural to feel anxious about what will happen during the surgery. Hernia repair is done under general anaesthesia, meaning you will be completely asleep and will not feel anything. For some groin hernias, a spinal or local anaesthetic with sedation may be an option. The procedure typically takes 45 minutes to 2 hours, depending on the type and complexity of the hernia.

The standard approach for most hernias is **laparoscopic (keyhole) repair**. Your surgeon makes three or four small incisions — each about 5 to 10 millimetres — in your abdomen. Carbon dioxide gas is gently introduced to create space, and a tiny camera (laparoscope) is inserted to give the surgeon a clear, magnified view of the hernia on a high-definition monitor. The hernia contents are gently pushed back into the abdomen, and a piece of **surgical mesh** is placed over the weakness from the inside, secured with sutures or fixation devices. The mesh acts as a scaffold that your tissue grows into, permanently reinforcing the muscle wall.

For larger or more complex hernias — particularly incisional hernias with a wide gap — your surgeon may recommend an **open repair**. A single larger incision is made directly over the hernia, the tissue is returned to the abdomen, and mesh is placed either above or below the muscle layer depending on the technique used. Open repair may also be chosen if you have had multiple previous abdominal surgeries with significant scarring.

Some centres in Bangalore also offer **robotic hernia repair** using the da Vinci surgical system, which gives the surgeon enhanced precision and three-dimensional vision, particularly useful for complex or recurrent hernias. Your surgeon will explain which technique is most appropriate for your specific hernia.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of your surgery, you will be admitted to the pre-operative area. The nursing team will start your IV line, give you antibiotics, and attach monitoring equipment. The anaesthesiologist will review your details and gently put you to sleep with general anaesthesia. A breathing tube may be inserted to support your breathing. You will not feel or remember any of this.

Once you are asleep, your surgeon cleans the skin over the hernia and makes the small incisions (for laparoscopic repair) or a single incision (for open repair). The laparoscope is inserted, and the surgical team inspects the hernia and surrounding structures. The hernia sac and any trapped tissue are carefully returned to the abdomen, the weakness is identified, and the mesh is positioned and secured over the defect. The team checks that the mesh sits flat and that there is no tension on the repair.

The gas is released, the incisions are closed with dissolvable stitches, and dressings are applied. You will be taken to the recovery room, where the anaesthesia team wakes you up gently. Most patients are fully awake within an hour and transferred to their room later that evening.

## Post-operative Care & Recovery

When you wake up, you will be in the recovery room, connected to monitors that track your heart rate, blood pressure, and oxygen levels. You may feel some soreness around the incision sites and a sense of fullness in your abdomen, and some patients notice mild shoulder pain from the gas used during laparoscopic surgery — this is temporary and usually settles within 24 to 48 hours.

**Will it hurt?** There will be some discomfort, but because the procedure is minimally invasive, the pain is far less than with traditional open surgery. You will receive both IV and oral pain medications, and most patients find the discomfort very manageable. By the second day, many people need only simple oral painkillers.

You will be encouraged to get up and walk the evening of your surgery or the next morning — early mobilisation helps prevent blood clots, reduces the risk of chest infections, and speeds up recovery. You will start with sips of water and progress to a light diet. Most laparoscopic patients go home within 24 to 48 hours.

Before discharge, your nurse will teach you how to care for your incisions, what warning signs to watch for, and how to manage your activity. The most important instruction is to avoid heavy lifting (anything over 5 to 10 kilograms) and straining for at least 4 to 6 weeks, as this puts pressure on the repair while the mesh integrates with your tissue.

## Long-term Outcomes & Success Rates

You are probably wondering whether the hernia will come back after surgery. The reassuring answer is that modern hernia repair with mesh has excellent long-term outcomes, and recurrence rates are low.

For inguinal hernias repaired laparoscopically with mesh, the recurrence rate is around 1 to 3% at experienced centres. Umbilical and small incisional hernias have similarly low recurrence rates, while very large incisional hernias carry a somewhat higher risk of recurrence — typically 5 to 10% — because of the size of the defect and the tension on the repair. Bangalore's leading hospitals report outcomes consistent with the best international benchmarks.

The most common long-term issue is **chronic post-herniorrhaphy pain** — persistent discomfort or aching at the surgical site affecting around 2 to 5% of patients. In most cases this is mild and manageable, and it often improves over the first year. Mesh infection is rare, occurring in less than 1 to 2% of cases. There is no impact on your lifespan, and you can resume all normal activities, including exercise, within a few weeks — though you should build up gradually and follow your surgeon's guidance on lifting.

## Alternatives to Consider

You might be wondering if there is a way to treat a hernia without surgery. The honest answer is that non-surgical options do not repair the hernia — they only manage the symptoms while the underlying weakness remains.

A **truss or hernia belt** can hold the bulge in place and reduce discomfort, but it does not strengthen the muscle wall or prevent the hernia from enlarging. Long-term use of a truss can also cause pressure sores and may weaken the surrounding tissue further. Trusses are generally only recommended for patients who are not fit enough for surgery.

**Watchful waiting** is reasonable for small, completely painless hernias, particularly in older patients with other medical conditions. However, studies show that most symptomatic hernias eventually require surgery, and operating earlier — when the defect is smaller — generally means a simpler procedure, a faster recovery, and a lower recurrence rate. The risk of emergency surgery for strangulation also rises the longer a hernia is left untreated.

For **hiatal hernias** causing acid reflux, medication (proton pump inhibitors) and lifestyle changes can control symptoms for many years. Surgery is reserved for patients whose symptoms are not controlled by medication or who have complications such as oesophagitis. For most patients with a visible abdominal or groin hernia, surgical repair with mesh is the safest and most definitive solution.
