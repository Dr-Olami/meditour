## Overview

If you and your partner have been trying to conceive and have been told that male-factor infertility is making it difficult, you may be feeling frustrated, embarrassed or even guilty. These feelings are completely natural — but please know that male-factor infertility is incredibly common, and it is not anyone's fault. ICSI (Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection) was developed specifically to overcome these challenges, and it has helped millions of couples worldwide become parents. You are not alone on this journey.

ICSI is a specialised form of IVF that addresses male-factor infertility by injecting a single, carefully selected sperm directly into the centre of a mature egg. This micromanipulation technique dramatically improves fertilisation rates when sperm count, motility or morphology is compromised, or when previous conventional IVF cycles have failed to achieve fertilisation. Since its introduction in the early 1990s, ICSI has become one of the most widely used assisted reproductive technologies worldwide.

For international patients, ICSI treatment in Bangalore offers world-class fertility care at a fraction of the cost found in Western countries. Leading fertility centres are equipped with state-of-the-art embryology laboratories, highly trained reproductive medicine specialists and internationally accredited protocols. The cost ranges from $2,000 to $5,000 per cycle, compared to $15,000 or more in the USA — and even after accounting for travel and accommodation, the total cost remains substantially lower.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

ICSI is primarily indicated for couples experiencing male-factor infertility. This includes men with low sperm count (oligospermia), poor sperm motility (asthenospermia) or abnormal sperm morphology (teratospermia) that would prevent natural fertilisation of the egg in a conventional IVF setting. By bypassing the need for sperm to penetrate the egg independently, ICSI overcomes many of the barriers posed by compromised semen parameters.

If you have previously undergone conventional IVF with poor or failed fertilisation, you are also a strong candidate for ICSI. The fertilisation failure may be attributed to sperm-egg interaction issues that ICSI can directly address. Men who require surgical sperm retrieval — such as those with obstructive azoospermia undergoing TESA (testicular sperm aspiration) or PESA (percutaneous epididymal sperm aspiration) — also benefit from ICSI, as surgically retrieved sperm often lack the motility needed for conventional IVF.

ICSI is also recommended when using previously frozen sperm, particularly when post-thaw motility is reduced. Couples with unexplained infertility who have failed multiple IUI cycles, women of advanced reproductive age, and those using donor sperm may also be advised to proceed with ICSI to maximise fertilisation potential. The decision is made by your fertility specialist based on a comprehensive assessment of both partners' reproductive health.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Preparation for an ICSI cycle involves a thorough evaluation of both partners. The female partner undergoes ovarian reserve testing, including AMH levels, day 3 FSH and oestradiol, and an antral follicle count via transvaginal ultrasound. A hysterosalpingogram or saline sonography may be performed to assess the uterine cavity and tubal patency. The male partner provides a detailed semen analysis, and if abnormalities are detected, additional tests such as sperm DNA fragmentation analysis may be recommended.

Both partners should undergo screening for infectious diseases, genetic conditions and general health markers. For international patients, clinics in Bangalore often arrange preliminary consultations via telemedicine to review these results and develop a tailored treatment plan before travel. This ensures the cycle can commence promptly upon arrival.

Lifestyle factors play a role in ICSI success. Both partners are advised to avoid smoking, limit alcohol consumption and maintain a healthy weight. The male partner may be advised to take antioxidant supplements to improve sperm quality in the months leading up to the cycle.

## Procedure Details

An ICSI cycle begins with ovarian stimulation, where the female partner receives hormone injections to encourage the development of multiple mature eggs. Progress is monitored through transvaginal ultrasound and blood tests over approximately 10–14 days. Once follicles reach optimal size, a trigger injection is administered to finalise egg maturation.

Egg retrieval is performed under light sedation as a day-care procedure, using ultrasound-guided transvaginal aspiration. On the same day, a semen sample is collected (or surgically retrieved if necessary), and the embryologist selects the most morphologically normal, motile sperm. Using a high-powered microscope and micromanipulation tools, a single sperm is immobilised and injected directly into each mature egg.

Fertilised eggs are cultured in the laboratory for 3–5 days, developing into embryos. One or two selected embryos are then transferred into the uterus in a quick, painless procedure. Any remaining high-quality embryos can be frozen for future use. After transfer, a pregnancy test is scheduled approximately 10–14 days later.

## What Happens During the Procedure

The ICSI cycle unfolds over several weeks, beginning with the ovarian stimulation phase. The female partner self-administers daily hormone injections — typically a combination of FSH and LH — to stimulate the ovaries to produce multiple follicles. Monitoring visits every 2–3 days include transvaginal ultrasounds and blood tests to track follicular growth and oestrogen levels.

Once the follicles reach approximately 18mm in diameter, a trigger injection of hCG is given to trigger final egg maturation. Exactly 34–36 hours later, egg retrieval is performed under light sedation. The procedure takes 15–30 minutes, and the patient recovers in a day-care suite for a few hours before discharge.

In the embryology laboratory, the retrieved eggs are stripped of surrounding cumulus cells to assess maturity. Each mature egg is then injected with a single selected sperm using the ICSI technique. The embryologist checks for fertilisation 16–18 hours later by observing the presence of two pronuclei — one from the sperm and one from the egg — confirming successful fertilisation.

Embryos are cultured for 3–5 days, reaching either the cleavage or blastocyst stage. Blastocyst transfer on day 5 is generally preferred, as it allows better selection of viable embryos. The embryo transfer procedure is quick and painless, performed without anaesthesia using a thin catheter guided through the cervix into the uterine cavity.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

**Will it hurt?** The embryo transfer is painless and feels similar to a Pap smear. After transfer, you are advised to rest for the remainder of the day, though complete bed rest is not required. Light activities such as walking are acceptable and may even be beneficial for circulation. You should avoid strenuous exercise, heavy lifting, hot baths and sexual intercourse until the pregnancy test result is confirmed.

Progesterone support, usually in the form of vaginal pessaries or injections, is prescribed to maintain the uterine lining and support potential implantation. You may experience mild cramping, breast tenderness or bloating as a result of the hormone medications — these are normal and expected.

A blood pregnancy test (beta-hCG) is performed approximately 10–14 days after embryo transfer. If positive, an ultrasound is scheduled one to two weeks later to confirm the presence of a gestational sac and fetal heartbeat. If the cycle is unsuccessful, your fertility specialist will review the outcome and discuss next steps, which may include a frozen embryo transfer if surplus embryos were cryopreserved.

For international patients, clinics recommend staying in Bangalore for 1–2 weeks after transfer to complete the pregnancy test and early monitoring before travelling home. This allows for timely intervention if any complications arise and provides emotional support during the waiting period.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

ICSI success rates in leading Bangalore clinics range from 40% to 60% per cycle for women under 35, gradually declining with age. For women aged 35–37, rates typically fall to 30–40%, and for those over 40, rates may be 15–25% per cycle. These figures are broadly consistent with international benchmarks.

The primary factors influencing ICSI outcomes include the female partner's age, ovarian reserve, egg quality, sperm quality, and the number and quality of embryos transferred. Blastocyst transfer has been shown to improve implantation rates compared to day 3 transfers. Preimplantation genetic testing (PGT) may be offered alongside ICSI to screen embryos for chromosomal abnormalities, particularly for older women or those with recurrent implantation failure.

Children conceived through ICSI have been extensively studied, and the overwhelming majority of research confirms the technique is safe, with no significant increase in birth defects compared to natural conception.

Options may include a frozen embryo transfer if surplus embryos were cryopreserved, a repeat ICSI cycle with protocol adjustments, or additional investigations.

## Alternatives to Consider

Conventional IVF remains a viable alternative if male-factor infertility is not the primary issue. In conventional IVF, eggs and sperm are co-incubated in a culture dish, allowing natural fertilisation to occur. This approach may be preferred when semen parameters are within normal ranges, as it avoids the additional cost and micromanipulation involved in ICSI.

IUI (Intrauterine Insemination) is a less invasive and less expensive option that may be appropriate for couples with mild male-factor infertility or unexplained infertility before progressing to IVF or ICSI. However, IUI success rates per cycle are lower, and couples with significant male-factor issues are typically advised to proceed directly to ICSI. For men with severe male-factor infertility including azoospermia, surgical sperm retrieval techniques such as PESA, TESA or micro-TESE can be combined with ICSI. Donor sperm ICSI is another option when surgical retrieval is unsuccessful.
