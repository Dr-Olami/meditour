## Overview

If you are considering egg freezing, you are likely thinking ahead — perhaps you want to focus on your career right now, or you have not yet found the right partner, or you are facing a medical treatment that could affect your fertility. Whatever your reason, you are making a thoughtful decision to preserve your options for the future. It is completely natural to feel a mix of hope and uncertainty, but the technology behind egg freezing has advanced enormously, and Bangalore's fertility clinics have helped thousands of women take control of their reproductive timeline.

Egg freezing (oocyte cryopreservation) allows you to retrieve, freeze and store your eggs for future use. By freezing your eggs at a younger age, you preserve your fertility potential — increasing the likelihood of successful conception later in life when natural egg quality and quantity may have declined. The procedure is increasingly sought by women who wish to delay childbearing for personal, professional or medical reasons, including those undergoing cancer treatments that may damage ovarian function.

In India, egg freezing costs between $1,500 and $3,500 per cycle — significantly more affordable than in Western countries where the same procedure can cost upwards of $15,000. Bangalore's fertility clinics utilise advanced vitrification technology and internationally standardised protocols, ensuring high egg survival and fertilisation rates. The combination of clinical excellence, cost savings and a supportive medical tourism infrastructure has positioned Bangalore as a leading destination for fertility preservation.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Egg freezing may be suitable for you if you are a woman aged 25 to 40 who, for personal or professional reasons, is not yet ready to start a family but wants to safeguard your ability to conceive when the time is right. By freezing your eggs during your peak reproductive years, you significantly improve your chances of a successful pregnancy later in life.

Medical fertility preservation is another critical indication. If you have been diagnosed with cancer and require chemotherapy or pelvic radiotherapy, you may face irreversible damage to your ovarian reserve. Egg freezing before the commencement of oncology treatment offers a vital opportunity to preserve your fertility. Similarly, if you have an autoimmune condition requiring gonadotoxic therapies, or you are scheduled for ovarian surgery, cryopreservation may benefit you as a proactive measure.

If you have a known family history of early menopause, are a fragile X premutation carrier, or have been diagnosed with diminished ovarian reserve at a young age, you are also a strong candidate. Freezing eggs early can capture better-quality oocytes before further decline occurs. Transgender individuals undergoing gender-affirming hormone therapy or surgery may also consider egg freezing prior to treatment.

It is important to understand that egg freezing does not guarantee a future pregnancy. Success depends on the number and quality of eggs frozen, the age at which they were retrieved, and your overall health at the time of future IVF treatment. A thorough fertility assessment is essential to set realistic expectations.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Preparation begins with a comprehensive fertility evaluation. This includes blood tests to measure your anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) levels — an indicator of ovarian reserve — alongside follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), luteinising hormone (LH) and oestradiol measurements taken on day 2 or 3 of your menstrual cycle. A transvaginal ultrasound is performed to count your antral follicles, giving the fertility specialist a clear picture of how your ovaries are likely to respond to stimulation.

Based on these results, the specialist will tailor an individualised stimulation protocol. You will be counselled on the injection schedule, potential side effects and the expected timeline. It is essential to disclose any underlying medical conditions, allergies or medications you are taking. For international patients, clinics often arrange a pre-cycle consultation via video call so the treatment plan is agreed before travel.

Lifestyle adjustments can improve your outcomes. You are advised to maintain a healthy weight, avoid alcohol and smoking, and limit caffeine intake in the weeks leading up to the cycle. Arriving in Bangalore a few days before the stimulation start date allows for a baseline scan and ensures the cycle can proceed without delay.

## Procedure Details

Ovarian stimulation involves daily hormone injections for approximately 10–14 days to encourage the development of multiple mature follicles. Your follicular growth is closely monitored through serial transvaginal ultrasounds and blood oestrogen levels. Once follicles reach optimal size, a trigger injection is administered to finalise egg maturation, and egg retrieval is scheduled 34–36 hours later.

The number of eggs retrieved in a single cycle varies depending on your age and ovarian reserve. Younger women with good reserve may yield 15–20 eggs per cycle, while those over 35 or with diminished reserve may retrieve fewer. In some cases, multiple stimulation cycles are recommended to bank a sufficient number of eggs — typically 10–15 mature oocytes — to give a reasonable chance of a future live birth.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of egg retrieval, you will arrive at the clinic having fasted for approximately six hours. The procedure is performed in a dedicated procedure room under light intravenous sedation, ensuring you are comfortable and pain-free throughout. A transvaginal ultrasound probe fitted with a needle guide is used to visualise your ovaries, and each follicle is carefully aspirated in turn.

**Will it hurt?** The retrieval typically takes 15–30 minutes. You will be sedated and should not feel pain during the procedure, though you may experience mild cramping afterwards. The aspirated fluid is immediately passed to the embryology laboratory, where the embryologist identifies and isolates each egg under a microscope. Only mature eggs (at metaphase II) are suitable for vitrification.

Once mature eggs are identified, they undergo vitrification. This involves exposing the eggs to cryoprotectant solutions that replace water within the cells, followed by rapid cooling in liquid nitrogen at -196°C. The speed of cooling prevents ice crystals from forming, which would otherwise damage the delicate cellular structures. Vitrified eggs are then stored in labelled, secure cryostorage tanks monitored around the clock.

After the procedure, you will rest in a recovery area for one to two hours under observation. Mild cramping, bloating or light spotting are common and typically resolve within a day.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

Following egg retrieval, you may experience mild abdominal discomfort, bloating and fatigue as your hormone levels begin to normalise. Over-the-counter pain relief such as paracetamol is usually sufficient. You are advised to rest for the remainder of the day and avoid strenuous physical activity, heavy lifting or vigorous exercise for at least one week.

In a small percentage of cases, Ovarian Hyperstimulation Syndrome (OHSS) may develop, particularly if you produced a high number of follicles. Symptoms include significant abdominal swelling, nausea, shortness of breath and reduced urine output. Clinics in Bangalore monitor patients closely after retrieval and provide clear guidance on when to seek medical attention.

Most patients feel fully recovered within one to two weeks. A follow-up ultrasound is typically performed to confirm your ovaries are returning to their normal size. For international patients, clinics recommend staying in Bangalore for approximately one to two weeks after retrieval to ensure complete ovarian recovery before flying home.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

The long-term success of egg freezing depends primarily on the age at which your eggs were retrieved and the number of eggs stored. Clinical data consistently show that eggs frozen before the age of 35 yield the highest pregnancy rates when subsequently used in IVF cycles. Vitrification survival rates exceed 90%, and fertilisation rates using ICSI on thawed eggs are comparable to those achieved with fresh eggs. Overall, pregnancy rates per transfer using previously frozen eggs range from 40% to 55% in women under 35 at the time of freezing.

Egg freezing is an insurance policy rather than a guarantee. Not every frozen egg will survive thawing, not every surviving egg will fertilise, and not every embryo will result in a pregnancy. However, having a bank of frozen eggs significantly improves your odds compared to attempting natural conception or fresh IVF at an older age. Women who freeze 15 or more mature eggs before age 35 have an estimated 70–80% chance of achieving at least one live birth from those eggs.

## Follow-Up Care After Returning Home

After returning home, you require minimal ongoing medical follow-up related to the egg freezing procedure itself. The primary recommendation is to maintain contact with the Bangalore clinic for annual storage renewals and to keep the clinic informed of any change in contact details.

If you later decide to use your frozen eggs, the clinic will coordinate the thaw-and-fertilisation process, which involves a fresh IVF cycle using ICSI. You should plan to return to Bangalore for this phase, or arrange for the eggs to be transferred to a local fertility clinic if regulations permit cross-border cryo-shipping.

## Alternatives to Consider

Embryo freezing is a well-established alternative. This involves fertilising retrieved eggs with sperm from a partner or donor and freezing the resulting embryos. Embryo freezing generally offers higher pregnancy rates per thawed unit, but it requires a commitment to a specific genetic pairing at the time of freezing — which may not suit you if you do not have a current partner or wish to keep your options open.

Ovarian tissue cryopreservation is an emerging option, primarily offered to patients facing gonadotoxic cancer treatments where there is insufficient time for ovarian stimulation. This experimental technique involves freezing ovarian tissue before treatment and re-implanting it afterwards. For women who are not yet ready for any intervention, natural conception at the optimal time remains the simplest path — but given the age-related decline in fertility, a fertility assessment can help you make an informed decision.
