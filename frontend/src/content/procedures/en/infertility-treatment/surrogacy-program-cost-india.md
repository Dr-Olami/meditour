## Overview

If you've arrived on this page, you're likely carrying a heavy emotional weight — the longing for a child, combined with the reality that carrying a pregnancy yourself isn't possible or safe. We understand how painful that journey can be, and we want to give you honest, compassionate guidance about what surrogacy in India looks like today.

Surrogacy is a form of assisted reproduction where another woman — the surrogate — carries a pregnancy for you when you cannot conceive or carry a child to term. The process usually involves IVF to create embryos using your eggs and your partner's sperm (or donor gametes), which are then transferred into the surrogate's uterus.

Here is the most important thing you need to know: **India's surrogacy laws have changed dramatically.** Under the Surrogacy (Regulation) Act 2021, commercial surrogacy is completely banned. Only altruistic surrogacy is permitted, and it is restricted to Indian citizen couples who meet strict criteria. If you are an international patient, a foreign national, or an OCI cardholder, **surrogacy is no longer legally available to you in India.**

For eligible Indian citizen couples, an altruistic surrogacy program in India costs between $10,000 and $25,000, covering IVF procedures, the surrogate's medical expenses, legal formalities, and pregnancy care.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Under Indian law, surrogacy is available only to Indian citizen couples who meet stringent criteria. To be eligible, you must be married for at least five years, have a certified medical indication for surrogacy — such as absence of the uterus, severe uterine damage, repeated IVF failure, or a condition that makes pregnancy life-threatening for you — and fall within the prescribed age limits (wife 25-50, husband 26-55). You must also have exhausted other fertility options, with documented medical evidence.

If you are an international patient, we know this is hard news. Surrogacy in India is not a legally available option for you, and we don't want to give you false hope. What we can offer you instead is honesty and practical alternatives. You may want to explore countries where commercial surrogacy is regulated and accessible to foreigners. Alternatively, you might consider IVF or ICSI using your own eggs and sperm, donor egg IVF, embryo donation, or adoption.

Here's something worth knowing: Indian fertility clinics can still support you with the IVF component — creating embryos that you may subsequently use in a surrogacy arrangement in another country where the law permits it. This can be a cost-effective way to begin your journey, even if the surrogacy itself takes place elsewhere.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

If you are an eligible Indian couple, your preparation begins with obtaining your certificate of medical indication and completing all legal formalities under the Surrogacy Act 2021. This includes registering your surrogacy arrangement, securing insurance coverage for your surrogate, and obtaining approvals from the appropriate authorities. Both you and your surrogate will undergo medical screening — blood tests, infectious disease screening, and a psychological evaluation.

You (the intended mother) or your egg donor will undergo ovarian reserve testing and a baseline ultrasound to plan your IVF stimulation protocol. Your surrogate's uterine readiness will be assessed through a mock cycle or direct monitoring.

If you are an international patient considering creating embryos in Bangalore for use in a surrogacy arrangement abroad, your preparation is slightly different. You'll need to complete a full fertility assessment and have an honest conversation with your clinic about cryopreservation (freezing your embryos) and the cross-border embryo shipping regulations that will apply to your specific destination country. Ask your clinic about their experience shipping embryos internationally — the better clinics in Bangalore do this regularly and can guide you through the paperwork.

## Procedure Details

If you are an eligible Indian couple, here is what the surrogacy journey looks like for you, step by step.

First, your fertility doctor will conduct a thorough medical evaluation to confirm that surrogacy is medically necessary for you. This could be because you were born without a uterus, your uterus has been severely damaged, you've had repeated IVF failures, or pregnancy would be life-threatening for you. You'll need a certificate of medical indication from a registered medical practitioner, and you'll need to complete all the legal requirements under the Surrogacy Act 2021.

Next comes surrogate selection. Under Indian law, your surrogate must be a close relative of yours — a married woman between 25 and 35 years old who already has at least one living child of her own. She must act altruistically, meaning she receives only medical and insurance coverage, not commercial payment. You and your surrogate will enter into a legally binding surrogacy agreement that must be approved by the appropriate authorities.

The medical process then follows standard IVF protocols. You (the intended mother) or an egg donor will undergo ovarian stimulation, followed by egg retrieval. Your eggs will be fertilised with your partner's sperm in the laboratory, and the resulting embryos will be transferred into your surrogate's uterus. Throughout the pregnancy, your surrogate receives comprehensive medical care, and when your baby is delivered, your child is handed over to you as per the legal agreement. Your parental rights are then established through court-ordered parentage orders.

If you are an international patient, you cannot pursue this full process in India. However, you can still come to Bangalore to create embryos through IVF — and then have those embryos shipped to a country where surrogacy is legally permitted for foreigners. We discuss this option in more detail below.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the medical side, the procedure follows standard IVF protocols — here's what you'll actually experience.

If you are the one providing eggs, you'll undergo ovarian stimulation with daily hormone injections for about 10-14 days. You'll have serial ultrasounds during this time so your doctor can monitor how your follicles are growing. Once your follicles are mature, you'll receive a trigger injection, and 34-36 hours later, your eggs will be retrieved under sedation. The retrieval is a short procedure, and you'll usually go home the same day.

Your retrieved eggs are then fertilised with your partner's sperm in the laboratory, and the resulting embryos are cultured for 3-5 days. One or two embryos are transferred into your surrogate's uterus, which has been prepared with hormone replacement therapy. The transfer itself is quick and painless — similar to an IUI, and over in minutes.

After 10-14 days, a pregnancy blood test confirms whether implantation has occurred. If it's successful, your surrogate continues hormone support and receives routine antenatal care throughout the pregnancy. You'll be involved in key milestones and scans, which can be an emotionally powerful experience as you watch your baby grow.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

After the embryo transfer, your surrogate continues progesterone and oestrogen support for several weeks until the placenta takes over hormone production. The pregnancy is monitored with regular ultrasounds and antenatal check-ups, and you'll be kept informed of progress. You may attend key scans, which many intended parents find deeply meaningful.

If the transfer is unsuccessful — and this does happen, so please prepare your heart for this possibility — your specialist will review the cycle and plan next steps, which may include a repeat transfer with your frozen embryos. It's natural to feel grief and frustration, and your clinic should offer counselling support.

Upon delivery, your baby is handed over to you as per your surrogacy agreement, and legal parentage orders are processed. Your surrogate receives postpartum care and recovery support for 6-8 weeks.

If you are an international patient who created embryos in Bangalore for use abroad, your post-retrieval care follows standard IVF recovery protocols. You may experience mild cramping and bloating for a few days after egg retrieval. Your embryos will be cryopreserved and shipped to your destination clinic under regulatory compliance — your Bangalore clinic will coordinate this with you.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

We want to be honest with you about success rates, because you deserve realistic expectations.

Success rates for surrogacy depend primarily on the age and ovarian reserve of the egg provider and the quality of the embryos transferred. If you are under 35 and using your own eggs, clinical pregnancy rates per transfer typically range from 40% to 60%. Your cumulative chances improve over multiple transfers. Using donor eggs can achieve even higher rates. Your surrogate's uterine environment and overall health also influence outcomes.

It's important to understand that a 40-60% success rate means there is a real chance any single transfer may not result in a pregnancy. This is not a failure on your part — it's simply the reality of biology. Many intended parents need more than one transfer.

If you are an international patient pursuing surrogacy abroad using embryos created in Bangalore, the same embryology standards apply. Vitrified (flash-frozen) embryos survive thawing at rates exceeding 90%, which is reassuring. Legal and logistical coordination between your Indian clinic and your overseas surrogacy centre is essential — make sure both clinics communicate clearly to ensure seamless care and compliance with cross-border reproductive care guidelines.
