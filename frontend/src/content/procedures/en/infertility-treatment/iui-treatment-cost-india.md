## Overview

If you have been trying to conceive for a while and are exploring your options, IUI is often the first fertility treatment your specialist will recommend. It is natural to feel a mix of hope and apprehension — you may be wondering whether it will work, whether it will hurt, and how it fits into your broader fertility journey. The good news is that IUI is simple, minimally invasive and far more affordable than IVF, making it an excellent starting point for many couples.

IUI (Intrauterine Insemination) involves placing washed and concentrated sperm directly into your uterus at the time of ovulation. By bypassing the cervix and increasing the number of sperm that reach your fallopian tubes, IUI improves the likelihood of fertilisation for couples with mild fertility issues. It is often recommended as a first-line treatment before progressing to more advanced options like IVF or ICSI.

IUI is particularly suitable for couples with unexplained infertility, mild male-factor infertility or cervical mucus issues, and for single women or same-sex couples using donor sperm. Because it does not involve egg retrieval or embryo culture, it is simpler, less invasive and considerably more affordable than IVF — while still offering a meaningful chance of conception for the right candidates.

In India, IUI treatment costs between $300 and $800 per cycle, making it one of the most accessible fertility treatments available to international patients. Bangalore's fertility clinics combine clinical expertise, modern laboratory facilities and patient-centred care to deliver excellent outcomes at a fraction of Western prices.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

IUI may be suitable for you if you are facing mild fertility challenges where your fallopian tubes are open and ovulation can be achieved. It is commonly recommended for couples with unexplained infertility, mild male-factor infertility (slightly reduced sperm count or motility), or cervical mucus hostility that impedes sperm passage. Women under 40 with regular ovulatory cycles and at least one patent fallopian tube are ideal candidates.

Single women and same-sex couples using donor sperm also benefit from IUI as a straightforward path to conception. If you have mild endometriosis or early-stage pelvic adhesions, IUI combined with ovulation induction may be attempted before considering more invasive treatments.

However, IUI is not appropriate if you have blocked fallopian tubes, severe male-factor infertility, or significant ovarian reserve decline — in these cases, IVF offers better prospects. A thorough fertility assessment — including semen analysis, tubal patency testing and ovarian reserve evaluation — is essential before starting IUI to ensure the treatment is matched to the underlying cause.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Preparation begins with a comprehensive fertility evaluation for both partners. For the female partner, this includes blood tests for ovarian reserve (AMH, FSH, oestradiol), a transvaginal ultrasound to assess the uterus and ovaries, and confirmation of tubal patency via hysterosalpingogram or sonosalpingography. For the male partner, a semen analysis evaluates count, motility and morphology.

Based on these results, your specialist will recommend a treatment protocol — natural cycle, oral ovulation induction, or mild injectable stimulation. You will be counselled on the injection schedule, potential side effects and the expected timeline. International patients often have a pre-cycle video consultation so the plan is agreed before travel. Lifestyle advice includes maintaining a healthy weight, avoiding smoking and alcohol, and timing travel to align with your menstrual cycle.

## Procedure Details

An IUI cycle typically begins on day 2 or 3 of your menstrual cycle, when you undergo a baseline ultrasound and blood tests. Depending on your treatment protocol, you may take oral ovulation-inducing medications (such as letrozole or clomiphene) or mild injectable hormones to encourage the development of one or two mature follicles.

Your follicular growth is monitored through serial transvaginal ultrasounds over 10–14 days. Once the lead follicle reaches approximately 18–20mm in diameter, a trigger injection of hCG is administered to induce ovulation. The insemination is scheduled 24–36 hours after the trigger, timed to coincide with the release of the egg.

On the day of the procedure, a semen sample is processed in the laboratory to isolate the healthiest, most motile sperm through a technique known as sperm washing. The prepared sample is loaded into a thin catheter, which is gently passed through your cervix into your uterus, where the sperm is deposited. The procedure takes only a few minutes and requires no anaesthesia. A pregnancy test is performed approximately 14 days later to confirm whether conception has occurred.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of insemination, the male partner provides a semen sample (or donor sperm is thawed), which is processed in the laboratory through sperm washing to concentrate the most motile sperm. You will lie on an examination table while the fertility specialist inserts a speculum and gently passes a thin catheter through your cervix into your uterus.

**Will it hurt?** IUI is generally not painful and is often compared to a Pap smear. The washed sperm sample is deposited directly into your uterine cavity over a few seconds. You may experience mild cramping, but no anaesthesia is required. The entire process takes approximately five to ten minutes. You will rest for 10–15 minutes afterwards and can then resume normal activities. A pregnancy blood test is scheduled 14 days post-insemination.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

Recovery after IUI is immediate for most patients. Mild cramping or light spotting may occur on the day of the procedure but typically resolves within hours. You are advised to avoid strenuous exercise and heavy lifting for a few days, though normal daily activities can be resumed straight away. There is no hospital stay required.

If ovarian stimulation was used, you should watch for symptoms of ovarian hyperstimulation, such as significant abdominal bloating or discomfort, and report these to the clinic. During the two-week wait before the pregnancy test, you are encouraged to maintain a healthy routine, avoid stress, and refrain from smoking or alcohol. Clinics provide clear guidance on medication continuation, such as progesterone support, if prescribed.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

IUI success rates typically range from 10% to 20% per cycle, depending on your age, the cause of infertility and whether ovulation induction is used. Cumulative success rates improve over multiple cycles, with many conceptions occurring within the first three to four attempts. Women under 35 with unexplained infertility tend to have the best outcomes.

If you do not conceive after several IUI cycles, your fertility specialist will review your case and may recommend progressing to IVF or ICSI. Importantly, IUI does not reduce your chances of success with subsequent IVF. For couples using donor sperm, IUI remains a highly effective and low-cost option, with success rates comparable to natural conception for young women with no underlying tubal or ovulatory issues.

## Follow-Up Care After Returning Home

After returning home, you will await the results of the 14-day pregnancy blood test, which can be arranged with a local laboratory or the treating clinic's remote monitoring service. If the result is positive, an ultrasound is scheduled at 6–7 weeks to confirm a viable intrauterine pregnancy. If negative, your specialist will review the cycle and advise on next steps, which may include repeating IUI or considering alternative treatments.

## Alternatives to Consider

If IUI is unsuccessful or not medically appropriate, IVF and ICSI offer higher success rates by fertilising eggs outside the body and transferring embryos directly into the uterus. For women with ovulatory dysfunction, ovulation induction with timed intercourse may be attempted before IUI. Donor sperm IUI is an option for single women or couples with severe male-factor infertility, while donor egg IVF may be considered for women with diminished ovarian reserve. Your fertility specialist will guide you through these options based on your specific circumstances.
