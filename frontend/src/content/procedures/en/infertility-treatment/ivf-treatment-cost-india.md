## Overview

If you've been trying to have a baby and it's just not happening, you may be feeling frustrated, heartbroken, or even guilty. These feelings are completely normal, and you are not alone. Millions of couples around the world face the same struggle, and IVF has helped many of them become parents.

In Vitro Fertilization, or IVF, is a fertility treatment where your eggs are collected from your ovaries and fertilised with sperm in a carefully controlled laboratory. The embryos that develop are grown for 3 to 5 days, and then one or two are gently placed into your uterus. Any remaining good-quality embryos can be frozen and saved for future attempts. IVF is currently the most effective fertility treatment available, especially if you have blocked fallopian tubes, endometriosis, male factor infertility, or unexplained infertility.

In Bangalore, your IVF journey would take place at accredited fertility centres like Aster CMI, Manipal, Cloudnine, Milann, or Fortis, staffed by reproductive medicine specialists who have trained internationally. The embryology labs use the same time-lapse incubators, laser-assisted hatching, and vitrification technology you would find at top clinics in the US or UK. Clinical pregnancy rates of 45 to 55 percent per fresh embryo transfer for women under 35 match global benchmarks, and cumulative success rates including frozen embryo transfers can exceed 70 percent.

For international patients, Bangalore offers English-speaking medical teams, dedicated international coordinators, transparent pricing, and a pleasant climate. A complete IVF cycle here costs 70 to 80 percent less than in the United States, with no compromise on laboratory standards or clinical expertise.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

IVF may be the right choice for you if you and your partner have a specific fertility barrier that makes natural conception unlikely or impossible. If you've been diagnosed with blocked or severely damaged fallopian tubes, IVF can bypass the tubes entirely. If you've had a tubal ligation reversal that didn't work, or if you have a hydrosalpinx affecting embryo implantation, IVF offers real hope.

If your partner has a low sperm count, poor motility, or abnormal morphology, IVF combined with ICSI can overcome these challenges. If your partner requires surgical sperm retrieval due to azoospermia, IVF makes fatherhood possible.

If you're over 35 and have diminished ovarian reserve, repeated IUI failures, or advanced endometriosis, your doctor may advise moving directly to IVF because age-related decline in egg quality makes time critical. If you have PCOS and haven't responded well to ovulation induction, IVF can work for you too. IVF is also a path for women who need fertility preservation before cancer treatment, and for same-sex couples or single women using donor sperm.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Before you travel to Bangalore, gather all your medical records from previous fertility evaluations — hormone panels (AMH, FSH, LH, oestradiol), hysterosalpingography reports, semen analysis results, and any previous IVF cycle summaries. Having these ready allows your treating specialist to design a stimulation protocol tailored to your body's response profile.

About 4 to 6 weeks before stimulation begins, start taking prenatal vitamins containing folic acid (400 to 800 micrograms daily). Both you and your partner should stop smoking, avoid excessive alcohol, maintain a healthy BMI, and limit caffeine. Your partner should avoid hot baths and saunas, as elevated testicular temperature can impair sperm quality.

We strongly recommend that your partner or a close companion accompanies you. The daily injection schedule and frequent clinic visits can be emotionally demanding. If you have chronic conditions like hypertension, diabetes, or thyroid disorders, make sure they're well-controlled before you travel.

## Procedure Details

A standard IVF cycle begins with ovarian stimulation. For about 10 to 12 days, you'll give yourself daily hormone injections containing follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinising hormone (LH) to produce multiple eggs. You'll visit the clinic every 2 to 3 days for a transvaginal ultrasound and blood test to monitor your response. Your specialist will choose the stimulation protocol based on your ovarian reserve markers (AMH and AFC), age, and previous cycle history.

Once your follicles reach 17 to 18 millimetres, you'll take a trigger injection of hCG, and egg retrieval is scheduled 34 to 36 hours later. The retrieval is done under short general anaesthesia using ultrasound guidance and takes about 20 to 30 minutes. Your retrieved eggs are inseminated with your partner's prepared sperm, and if there's a male factor concern, ICSI may be used. Your embryos are cultured to the blastocyst stage on day 5, allowing selection of embryos with the highest implantation potential.

A few days later, one or two embryos are transferred into your uterus through a thin, soft catheter — a painless day-care procedure requiring no anaesthesia. After the transfer, you'll take progesterone support, and 10 to 14 days later, a blood pregnancy test checks your beta-hCG levels. Surplus embryos are vitrified and frozen for potential future transfers, which are simpler, less expensive, and equally effective.

## What Happens During the Procedure

Your IVF cycle begins on day 2 or 3 of your menstrual period with a baseline transvaginal ultrasound and blood tests. Once confirmed, you'll begin daily FSH and LH injections subcutaneously using a small pen device. The fertility nurse will train you on the first day, and most women find it much easier than expected.

Over the next 10 to 12 days, you'll return to the clinic every 2 to 3 days for monitoring. When at least two or three follicles reach 17 to 18 millimetres, you'll take your trigger injection, since retrieval is scheduled 34 to 36 hours later.

On the day of egg retrieval, you'll fast from midnight. The procedure is performed under short general anaesthesia in a day-care theatre. Using ultrasound guidance, your doctor gently aspirates follicular fluid from each ovary, and the embryologist immediately collects your eggs. The procedure takes 20 to 30 minutes, and you'll rest for 1 to 2 hours before going home. While you're recovering, your partner provides a semen sample.

Your embryo transfer happens 3 to 5 days after retrieval. Using ultrasound guidance, your doctor threads a thin catheter through your cervix and places one or two embryos into your uterine cavity. The procedure is painless and takes only a few minutes.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

After your embryo transfer, you'll begin progesterone support, continuing daily until your pregnancy test and, if positive, through the first 8 to 12 weeks. You may notice mild side effects like breast tenderness, bloating, and fatigue — these are normal and don't indicate whether the cycle was successful.

You'll be advised to avoid heavy lifting, vigorous exercise, and sexual intercourse until your pregnancy test. Light walking and normal daily activities are fine, and bed rest does not improve implantation rates. Some mild cramping or spotting can occur without necessarily meaning a negative outcome.

The two-week wait between embryo transfer and your pregnancy test is often the most emotionally challenging part of the IVF journey. Try to stay busy and lean on your partner and loved ones. Your blood pregnancy test (quantitative beta-hCG) will be performed 10 to 14 days after transfer. If positive, a repeat test may confirm rising hormone levels, followed by an ultrasound at 6 to 7 weeks. If negative, your fertility specialist will review the cycle and discuss adjustments.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

You deserve honest information about your chances. IVF success rates at Bangalore's leading clinics are comparable to international benchmarks. For women under 35, clinical pregnancy rates are 45 to 55 percent per fresh embryo transfer. For women aged 38 to 40, this declines to 25 to 30 percent, and for women over 42, about 10 to 15 percent. When frozen embryo transfers from the same cycle are included, cumulative success rates often reach 65 to 75 percent for women under 35. The decline with age is primarily driven by egg quality, which is why your doctor may recommend starting sooner if you're in your late thirties or early forties.

For couples who achieve pregnancy, the vast majority go on to have a healthy delivery. IVF pregnancies carry a slightly elevated risk of preterm delivery and low birth weight, partly because of higher rates of multiple pregnancy, though single embryo transfer has significantly reduced these risks. Long-term studies of children conceived through IVF show no significant differences in physical health, cognitive development, or psychological wellbeing compared to naturally conceived children.

## Alternatives to Consider

Before committing to IVF, you and your doctor should consider whether a less invasive option might work for you. Intrauterine insemination (IUI) places washed sperm directly into your uterus during ovulation, costing only $300 to $800 per cycle in Bangalore. IUI is a reasonable first option for mild male factor infertility or unexplained infertility of short duration, with success rates of 10 to 20 percent per cycle. Most specialists recommend trying 3 to 6 IUI cycles before moving to IVF.

ICSI is a variation of IVF used when male factor infertility is severe. Egg freezing is an option if you wish to preserve your fertility for the future. If you have diminished ovarian reserve, particularly if you're over 42, donor egg IVF may offer a more realistic path to parenthood. For couples where the female partner cannot carry a pregnancy, surrogacy may be an option, though Indian law restricts surrogacy to Indian citizens under the Surrogacy (Regulation) Act 2021.
