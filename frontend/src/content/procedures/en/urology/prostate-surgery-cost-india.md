## Overview

If you are a man over 50 and finding that your trips to the bathroom have become increasingly frequent, urgent, and frustrating — especially at night — you are not alone. An enlarged prostate, medically known as benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), affects millions of men worldwide as they age. The prostate gland, which sits just below your bladder and surrounds your urethra, gradually grows larger over the years. As it does, it squeezes the urethra, making it harder for urine to flow through. You may experience a weak stream, difficulty starting urination, a feeling of incomplete emptying, and the need to urinate multiple times during the night. For many men, these symptoms progress to the point where they significantly disrupt daily life and sleep.

When medications such as alpha-blockers and 5-alpha reductase inhibitors no longer provide adequate relief, surgery becomes the next step. Transurethral Resection of the Prostate (TURP) has been the gold-standard surgical treatment for BPH for decades. It is a minimally invasive procedure — meaning no external incisions are made. Your urologist passes a specialised instrument called a resectoscope through your urethra and uses a tiny wire loop electrode to remove the excess prostate tissue that is blocking urine flow. The pieces of tissue are flushed out, and the area is cauterised to control bleeding. The result is a wider channel through which urine can flow freely.

Bangalore's urology centres have extensive experience with TURP and the newer variations such as bipolar TURP and laser enucleation (HoLEP). ## Who Is This Procedure For?

TURP may be the right choice for you if you have moderate to severe lower urinary tract symptoms caused by BPH that have not responded adequately to medication. If you find yourself waking up three, four, or more times a night to urinate, if your stream is so weak that urination takes several minutes, or if you frequently feel an urgent need to urinate but cannot pass much urine, these are signs that your bladder outlet obstruction is significant.

You may also need TURP if you have developed complications of BPH. These include recurrent urinary retention — where you are unable to urinate at all and require a catheter to drain your bladder — recurrent urinary tract infections caused by incomplete bladder emptying, bladder stones that form because of stagnant urine, or even kidney damage from prolonged back-pressure on your bladder and kidneys (hydronephrosis). These are situations where surgery is not just about improving quality of life but about preventing serious harm to your urinary system.

The size of your prostate matters. TURP is most suitable for prostates that are moderately enlarged — typically under 80 to 100 grams. For very large prostates, your urologist may recommend a different technique such as Holmium Laser Enucleation of the Prostate (HoLEP) or even open simple prostatectomy. Your urologist will assess your prostate size through a transrectal ultrasound and discuss the best approach for you.

## Pre-operative Preparation

Before you travel to Bangalore, share your medical records with your urologist for advance review. This should include any recent ultrasound or CT scans of your prostate and urinary tract, uroflowmetry results (a test that measures how fast and how much you urinate), blood tests including PSA (prostate-specific antigen), and a complete list of your medications. If you are taking blood thinners such as aspirin, clopidogrel, or warfarin, your urologist will advise you to stop them several days before surgery to reduce bleeding risk. Do not stop any medication without consulting your doctor first.

Once you arrive in Bangalore, you will undergo a comprehensive pre-operative assessment. This includes a physical examination, a digital rectal examination to assess prostate size and consistency, blood tests (complete blood count, kidney function, coagulation profile, PSA), a urinalysis to check for infection, and an ultrasound of your urinary tract to measure your prostate volume and check for residual urine and hydronephrosis. You may also undergo uroflowmetry and a post-void residual urine measurement. If your PSA is elevated, your urologist may recommend a prostate biopsy to rule out prostate cancer before proceeding with surgery. You will be asked to fast for 6 to 8 hours before the procedure.

## Procedure Details

Your TURP is performed under spinal or general anaesthesia. With spinal anaesthesia, you remain awake but feel nothing from the waist down; many urologists prefer this approach because it allows for early detection of any fluid absorption issues and reduces certain surgical risks. The procedure typically takes 45 to 90 minutes, depending on the size of your prostate.

The resectoscope — a combined telescope and surgical instrument — is passed through your urethra to reach the prostate. No external incisions are needed. Your urologist visualises the enlarged prostate tissue on a high-definition monitor and uses a wire loop electrode to systematically shave away the excess tissue, piece by piece. Each piece is cut using electrical current and then flushed into the bladder, where it is collected and sent for pathological examination. This is important because the tissue is always checked under a microscope to confirm it is benign.

Modern bipolar TURP technology uses saline irrigation instead of the traditional non-electrolyte fluids, which dramatically reduces the risk of a rare but serious complication called TUR syndrome — a condition caused by excessive absorption of irrigation fluid that leads to low blood sodium levels. Many Bangalore hospitals now use bipolar systems as standard practice. Once all the excess tissue has been removed and the bleeding points have been cauterised, a three-way urinary catheter is inserted to allow for continuous bladder irrigation, which prevents blood clots from blocking the catheter in the first 24 to 48 hours.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of your surgery, you will be admitted to the pre-operative area. The nursing team will start an intravenous line, give you antibiotics to prevent infection, and attach monitoring equipment. The anaesthesiologist will discuss your anaesthesia options and administer either spinal or general anaesthesia. Once you are anaesthetised, you will be positioned on the operating table with your legs in supports (lithotomy position).

Your urologist gently passes the resectoscope through your urethra to the prostate. Using continuous irrigation to maintain a clear view, the wire loop electrode is used to resect the obstructing prostate tissue in a systematic fashion, starting from the region around the bladder neck and working outward. The electrical current both cuts the tissue and coagulates blood vessels to control bleeding. The removed tissue fragments are flushed out through the resectoscope and collected for pathological analysis.

Once your urologist has created a wide, open channel through the prostate, the area is carefully inspected for any remaining bleeding points, which are individually cauterised. A three-way Foley catheter is then inserted into your bladder, and continuous bladder irrigation with saline is started to wash out any blood and prevent clot formation. The entire procedure typically takes 45 to 90 minutes, and you will be transferred to the recovery area for monitoring as the anaesthesia wears off.

## Post-operative Care & Recovery

After your surgery, you will be transferred to the urology ward. The most important aspect of your immediate post-operative care is the continuous bladder irrigation, which runs through your catheter for the first 24 to 48 hours. This gentle flow of saline keeps your bladder clear of blood clots that could block the catheter. You may notice that the irrigation fluid is pink or reddish at first — this is normal. Over the first day, the colour should gradually lighten. The nursing staff will monitor the irrigation closely and adjust the flow rate as needed.

You will likely feel some discomfort from the catheter, including a sensation of bladder fullness or mild cramping. These symptoms are manageable with medication and resolve once the catheter is removed. You will be encouraged to drink plenty of fluids to help flush the system. Once the irrigation is stopped and your urine remains clear, the catheter will be removed — usually on the second or third day after surgery. You will then be observed to ensure you can urinate independently before being discharged.

Before you leave the hospital, your urologist will review the pathology results to confirm that the removed tissue was benign. You will receive detailed instructions on activity restrictions, fluid intake, and medications. You should avoid heavy lifting, strenuous exercise, and sexual activity for 4 to 6 weeks. It is normal to experience some urinary frequency, urgency, mild burning, and occasional light bleeding for the first few weeks as the prostatic fossa heals.

## Long-term Outcomes & Success Rates

TURP has excellent long-term outcomes, with symptom improvement reported in 85-90% of patients. Most men experience a dramatic improvement in their urinary flow rate, a reduction in night-time urination, and a significant improvement in quality of life. Bangalore's urology centres report outcomes consistent with international benchmarks, thanks to high procedural volumes and access to the latest endoscopic technology.

The resected tissue is always sent for pathological examination, and in a small percentage of cases (typically 5-10%), previously unsuspected prostate cancer may be detected in the removed tissue. If this happens, your urologist will discuss further evaluation and management with you.

It is important to understand that TURP removes the obstructing tissue but does not cure the underlying disease process. Over time, the remaining prostate tissue can continue to grow, and approximately 5-10% of patients may require a repeat procedure within 10 years. Maintaining regular follow-up with your urologist and monitoring your symptoms and PSA levels is essential for long-term success.

## Follow-up Care After Returning Home

Before you leave Bangalore, you will receive a comprehensive discharge summary detailing your procedure, pathology results, medications prescribed, and follow-up recommendations. Share this document with your urologist at home, who will take over your ongoing care. A follow-up appointment is typically scheduled at 4 to 6 weeks, at which point a uroflowmetry test and post-void residual ultrasound may be performed to assess your improvement.

You should continue to drink plenty of fluids and avoid constipation, as straining can precipitate bleeding from the healing prostatic fossa. If you have been prescribed antibiotics, complete the full course. If you were on blood thinners before surgery, your urologist will advise when it is safe to restart them.

Seek immediate medical attention if you experience severe bleeding with bright red blood or clots in your urine, inability to urinate, fever with chills, or severe pelvic pain. ## Alternatives to Consider

If your prostate is only mildly enlarged and your symptoms are manageable, medications may be an effective first approach. Alpha-blockers (such as tamsulosin) relax the smooth muscle in the prostate and bladder neck, improving urine flow within days. 5-alpha reductase inhibitors (such as finasteride) slowly shrink the prostate over several months. Combination therapy is effective for many men and may delay or prevent the need for surgery.

If your prostate is very large (over 80-100 grams), Holmium Laser Enucleation of the Prostate (HoLEP) may be a better option than TURP. HoLEP uses a laser to enucleate the entire adenoma, regardless of prostate size, with minimal bleeding and a shorter catheter time. It is available at select Bangalore centres. Other minimally invasive options include GreenLight laser vaporisation, UroLift (a mechanical implant that holds the prostate tissue open), and Rezūm (steam therapy). Your urologist will discuss which technique is most appropriate for your specific situation.
