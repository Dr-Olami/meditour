## Overview

If you rely on glasses or contact lenses to see clearly and have wondered whether you could reduce or eliminate that dependence, LASIK eye surgery may be the answer. LASIK — which stands for Laser-Assisted In Situ Keratomileusis — is the most commonly performed elective vision correction procedure in the world, with over 30 million procedures completed globally. It works by reshaping your cornea, the clear dome-shaped window at the front of your eye, so that light focuses precisely on your retina rather than in front of or behind it. The result is clearer distance vision without the need for corrective lenses.

LASIK corrects the three most common refractive errors: myopia (short-sightedness), hyperopia (long-sightedness), and astigmatism. During the procedure, a thin flap is created on the surface of your cornea, the underlying tissue is reshaped with a cool ultraviolet excimer laser, and the flap is repositioned where it heals naturally without stitches. Modern LASIK is performed entirely with lasers — a femtosecond laser creates the flap, and an excimer laser reshapes the cornea — in what is known as bladeless or all-laser LASIK. For patients seeking the least invasive option, SMILE (Small Incision Lenticule Extraction) uses a single femtosecond laser to create and remove a small disc of tissue through a keyhole incision, preserving more of the cornea's structural integrity.

Bangalore's ophthalmology centres are equipped with the latest laser platforms, including the Carl Zeiss VisuMax for SMILE, the Alcon WaveLight EX500 excimer laser, and the Johnson & Johnson iDesign advanced wavefront system. The city's refractive surgeons perform thousands of LASIK procedures each year, and international patients benefit from English-speaking teams, streamlined logistics, and costs that are up to 80% lower than in Western countries. Whether you need standard bladeless LASIK or a customised topography-guided treatment for complex astigmatism, Bangalore offers world-class care at an accessible price.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

LASIK may be right for you if you are tired of the inconvenience, cost, or discomfort of glasses and contact lenses and want a permanent solution for your refractive error. The ideal candidate is aged 18 or older with a stable glasses prescription that has not changed significantly for at least 12 months. Your refractive error should fall within the treatable range — typically up to -10.00 dioptres of myopia, up to +6.00 dioptres of hyperopia, and up to 6.00 dioptres of astigmatism — though the exact limits depend on your corneal thickness and overall eye health.

Not everyone is suitable for LASIK, and a thorough pre-operative assessment is essential to determine your candidacy. You may not be a good candidate if you have a thin or irregularly shaped cornea, severe dry eye, active eye disease such as glaucoma or cataract, an autoimmune condition such as rheumatoid arthritis or lupus, uncontrolled diabetes, or if you are pregnant or breastfeeding. Contact lens wearers should stop wearing their lenses for a period before the assessment — typically one week for soft lenses and longer for rigid gas-permeable lenses — to allow the cornea to return to its natural shape.

It is also important to have realistic expectations. LASIK is designed to reduce your dependence on glasses, not necessarily to eliminate them entirely. Most patients achieve 20/20 vision or better, but some may still need glasses for certain tasks, particularly reading after the age of 40 due to presbyopia — the natural age-related loss of near focusing ability. Your surgeon will discuss what LASIK can and cannot achieve in your specific case and may recommend alternative options such as monovision LASIK, where one eye is corrected for distance and the other for near vision.

## Pre-operative Preparation

Before you travel to Bangalore, share your recent eye examination records, including your current glasses prescription and any previous corneal topography or OCT scans, with your treating ophthalmologist for advance review. If you wear contact lenses, you will need to stop wearing them before your pre-operative assessment — soft lenses for at least one week, toric lenses for two weeks, and rigid gas-permeable lenses for at least three weeks — as contact lenses can temporarily alter the shape of your cornea and affect the accuracy of your measurements.

Once you arrive in Bangalore, you will undergo a comprehensive pre-operative assessment that takes approximately two hours. This includes a detailed refraction to measure your exact prescription, corneal topography to map the shape and curvature of your cornea, pachymetry to measure your corneal thickness, and wavefront analysis to detect subtle optical imperfections unique to your eyes. Your tear film will be assessed for dry eye, your pupil size will be measured in dim light, and your retina will be examined to rule out any conditions that could affect your outcome.

Your surgeon will use all of this data to determine whether you are a suitable candidate for LASIK and, if so, which technique is best for you. Standard bladeless femtosecond LASIK is suitable for most patients with average corneal thickness and straightforward prescriptions. Topography-guided custom LASIK is recommended for patients with irregular corneas or higher-order aberrations that affect visual quality. SMILE is preferred for patients with thin corneas, dry eye, or active lifestyles where a smaller incision and greater structural preservation are advantageous. You will be asked to fast for a few hours before the procedure and to arrange for someone to accompany you home afterwards, as your vision will be blurry immediately after surgery.

## Procedure Details

LASIK is performed in a dedicated laser suite under strict sterile conditions. You will receive anaesthetic eye drops that numb the surface of your eye completely, so you will not feel pain during the procedure. You may be given a mild oral sedative to help you relax. You will remain awake and comfortable throughout, which typically takes 10 to 15 minutes per eye, with the actual laser treatment lasting only a few seconds to a minute.

For bladeless femtosecond LASIK, the surgeon begins by placing a suction ring on your eye to hold it steady, and the femtosecond laser creates a thin, precise corneal flap. The suction ring is then removed, and the surgeon lifts the flap to expose the underlying corneal tissue. You will be asked to focus on a fixation light while the excimer laser delivers rapid, painless pulses of ultraviolet light that reshape your cornea according to your personalised treatment plan. The laser tracks your eye movements thousands of times per second to ensure perfect centring even if your eye moves slightly.

Once the laser treatment is complete, the surgeon replaces the flap, which adheres naturally without stitches, and rinses the interface to remove any debris. For SMILE, the process is even simpler — the femtosecond laser creates a small lenticule (disc of tissue) within the cornea and a keyhole incision of just 2 to 4 millimetres, through which the surgeon removes the lenticule, reshaping the cornea without creating a flap. After confirming that the treatment is complete and the cornea is clear, the surgeon applies antibiotic and anti-inflammatory drops and places a protective shield or goggles over your eyes.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of your surgery, you will be admitted to the day-care unit. The nursing team will instil anaesthetic eye drops and may give you a mild sedative. Your eyes will be cleaned with antiseptic solution, and you will be positioned comfortably under the laser. A speculum will be placed to keep your eyelids open — this is painless due to the numbing drops, though you may feel a slight pressure. You will be asked to focus on a small blinking light throughout the procedure.

Throughout the surgery, your surgeon monitors your eye through a high-magnification microscope and communicates with you at each stage. When the femtosecond laser creates the flap, you will feel a brief pressure sensation and your vision will momentarily dim — this is completely normal. When the excimer laser reshapes your cornea, you will hear a clicking sound and may notice a slight odour, but there is no pain. The entire process is monitored in real time by the laser's eye-tracking system, which ensures the treatment is delivered with micron-level precision.

Once the laser treatment is complete and the flap is replaced, the surgeon examines your eye under the microscope to confirm everything is in the correct position. The protective goggles are placed over your eyes, and you are transferred to the recovery area for a short rest. Most patients are ready to leave the eye centre within 30 to 60 minutes of the procedure. You will need someone to drive you home or to your accommodation, as your vision will not be clear enough to drive immediately.

## Post-operative Care & Recovery

After surgery, you will rest briefly before being discharged. Your eyes will be sensitive to light and may water or feel as though there is a foreign body in them — this is normal and usually subsides within a few hours. Vision is typically hazy on the first day, with most patients noticing significant improvement by the next morning. You should wear the protective goggles while sleeping for the first week to prevent accidental rubbing of your eyes.

You will be prescribed a regimen of antibiotic and anti-inflammatory eye drops to use for one to four weeks, along with preservative-free lubricating drops to keep your eyes comfortable. It is critical to follow the drop schedule exactly as prescribed, as this prevents infection, controls inflammation, and promotes proper healing. Avoid rubbing or pressing on your eyes, avoid getting water in your eyes for the first week, and wear sunglasses outdoors to protect against glare, dust, and wind. You can resume most light activities the next day, but should avoid swimming, eye makeup, and contact sports for at least one month.

A follow-up review will be scheduled within the first 24 hours to check the flap position, corneal healing, and your visual acuity. Further reviews are typically arranged at one week, one month, and three months. Your surgeon will advise when you can resume driving, which is usually within a day or two once your vision meets the legal standard. If you have both eyes treated on the same day, recovery is straightforward, though some patients prefer to have one eye treated at a time.

## Long-term Outcomes & Success Rates

LASIK has one of the highest patient satisfaction rates of any elective procedure, with over 95% of patients achieving 20/20 vision or better and satisfaction rates exceeding 98% in large studies. Bangalore's refractive surgery centres achieve outcomes consistent with international benchmarks, thanks to experienced surgeons, advanced laser technology, and rigorous pre-operative screening. Most patients enjoy clear, stable distance vision without the need for glasses or contact lenses for daily activities.

The corneal reshaping performed during LASIK is permanent, but your eyes continue to age naturally. If you had LASIK in your twenties or thirties, you will still develop presbyopia after the age of 40 and may need reading glasses for close work. Some patients experience a gradual regression — a small return of their original prescription — over the years, which can usually be corrected with a quick enhancement procedure if your corneal thickness allows. Your surgeon will discuss the likelihood of regression based on your original prescription and corneal characteristics.

Your long-term visual outcome also depends on the health of the rest of your eye. If you develop cataracts later in life, you can still have cataract surgery, and your surgeon will take your previous LASIK into account when calculating the power of your intraocular lens. Regular eye examinations are important after LASIK to monitor for conditions such as dry eye, glaucoma, or retinal disease, which are not prevented by LASIK but can affect your vision over time.

## Follow-up Care After Returning Home

Before you leave Bangalore, you will receive a comprehensive discharge summary detailing the LASIK procedure performed, the laser platform used, your pre-operative and post-operative prescriptions, and follow-up recommendations. Share this document with your ophthalmologist at home, who will take over your ongoing care. A follow-up appointment is typically scheduled at one week, one month, and three months after surgery to assess your visual outcome and confirm stability.

Continue your eye drops exactly as prescribed and do not stop them early, even if your eyes feel comfortable. Use preservative-free lubricating drops as needed, as dry eye is the most common complaint in the first few months and can affect visual quality. Seek immediate medical attention if you experience sudden loss of vision, increasing pain, a curtain or shadow across your vision, or significant light sensitivity, as these may indicate a flap complication or infection. ## Alternatives to Consider

If you are not a suitable candidate for LASIK — for example, if your cornea is too thin, your prescription is too high, or you have dry eye — there are several alternatives. Photorefractive Keratectomy (PRK) and its variant Epi-LASIK remove the surface layer of the cornea before laser reshaping, avoiding the creation of a flap, which makes them suitable for thinner corneas though with a longer recovery time. Implantable Collamer Lenses (ICL) are placed inside the eye behind the iris and can correct very high prescriptions that are beyond the range of LASIK.

For patients over 45 who are developing presbyopia, refractive lens exchange — removing the natural lens and replacing it with a multifocal intraocular lens — can correct both distance and near vision in a single procedure and also prevents future cataracts. Your surgeon will discuss which option is best suited to your eyes, lifestyle, and visual goals. For some patients, a combination of approaches, such as monovision LASIK or blended vision, provides the best overall outcome.
