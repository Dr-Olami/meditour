## Overview

If you have been told that your vision is becoming cloudy, colours appear faded, or you struggle to read and drive at night, you may be developing a cataract. A cataract forms when the natural lens inside your eye gradually becomes opaque, scattering light and blurring the image that reaches your retina. It is the leading cause of reversible blindness worldwide, and it progresses slowly—often over years—until daily activities become difficult. The encouraging news is that cataract surgery is one of the most frequently performed and most successful surgical procedures in modern medicine, with a success rate exceeding 98% in experienced hands.

Cataract surgery involves removing the clouded natural lens and replacing it with a clear artificial intraocular lens (IOL). The lens is responsible for focusing light onto your retina, so restoring its clarity can dramatically improve your vision. Modern cataract surgery is performed using a technique called phacoemulsification, in which a tiny ultrasonic probe breaks the clouded lens into fragments that are gently suctioned out through a self-sealing incision as small as 2.2 millimetres. No stitches are typically required, and recovery is rapid. For patients seeking the highest precision, femtosecond laser-assisted cataract surgery (FLACS) uses a computer-guided laser to perform key steps of the procedure with micron-level accuracy.

Bangalore's ophthalmology centres are equipped with the latest diagnostic and surgical technology, including optical coherence tomography (OCT), advanced biometry for accurate IOL power calculation, and femtosecond laser platforms. ## Who Is This Procedure For?

Cataract surgery may be right for you if your vision has deteriorated to the point where it interferes with your daily life. Common signs include blurred or cloudy vision, difficulty reading, increased glare from headlights at night, fading of colours, and frequent changes in your glasses prescription. If you find yourself avoiding driving at night, struggling to recognise faces, or needing brighter light for close work, it is worth having a comprehensive eye examination to determine whether a cataract is the cause.

You do not need to wait until your vision is severely impaired before considering surgery. The old advice of waiting until the cataract is "ripe" no longer applies with modern techniques. Early surgery can restore your quality of life sooner and carries no greater risk than delayed surgery. Your ophthalmologist will assess the density of the cataract, your visual acuity, and the impact on your lifestyle to recommend the right timing.

Cataract surgery is also suitable for patients who have other eye conditions, though these may need to be managed alongside the cataract. If you have glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, or age-related macular degeneration, your surgeon will evaluate whether cataract surgery will meaningfully improve your vision and will plan the procedure to minimise risk to your existing eye health. Patients with diabetes or hypertension should have these conditions well controlled before surgery to reduce the risk of complications.

## Pre-operative Preparation

Before you travel to Bangalore, share your recent eye examination records, including any visual acuity measurements, intraocular pressure readings, and retinal photographs, with your treating ophthalmologist for advance review. If you take blood-thinning medications such as aspirin or warfarin, you may be advised to continue them, as modern cataract surgery is performed through a tiny self-sealing incision with minimal bleeding risk. However, always confirm this with your surgeon. Disclose all medications and supplements you take, including over-the-counter products.

Once you arrive in Bangalore, you will undergo a comprehensive pre-operative assessment. This includes detailed biometry to measure the length of your eye and the curvature of your cornea, which allows precise calculation of the IOL power needed to give you the best possible vision. Additional tests may include optical coherence tomography (OCT) to assess your retina, corneal topography to map the shape of your cornea, and a slit-lamp examination to grade the cataract. Blood tests, including coagulation profile and blood sugar, are performed to ensure you are fit for surgery.

Your surgeon will discuss the type of IOL that best suits your needs. Standard monofocal lenses provide clear vision at one distance and are included in the base package. Premium lenses—toric lenses for astigmatism correction, multifocal lenses for near and far vision, and accommodating lenses that shift focus—can reduce your dependence on glasses but carry an additional cost. Your surgeon will explain the benefits and limitations of each option based on your lifestyle, occupation, and eye health. You will be asked to fast for a few hours before the procedure and to arrange for someone to accompany you home afterwards, as you will not be able to drive immediately.

## Procedure Details

Cataract surgery is performed in a dedicated ophthalmic operating theatre under strict sterile conditions. You will receive local anaesthesia, most commonly in the form of eye drops that numb the surface of the eye, combined with a mild sedative to help you relax. In some cases, a small injection around the eye may be used for deeper anaesthesia. You will remain awake throughout the procedure, which typically takes 30 to 45 minutes, but you will not feel pain.

The surgeon begins by making a tiny incision—often less than 2.2 millimetres—at the edge of your cornea. Through this incision, a circular opening is created in the front of the lens capsule to access the clouded lens. A thin ultrasonic probe is then inserted, and the lens is broken into small fragments using high-frequency sound waves (phacoemulsification). These fragments are gently suctioned out, leaving the back wall of the lens capsule intact to support the new IOL.

The folded intraocular lens is then inserted through the same small incision and unfolds into position inside the capsule. Because the incision is self-sealing, stitches are rarely needed. In femtosecond laser-assisted surgery, a computer-guided laser performs the corneal incisions, the capsular opening, and the initial fragmentation of the lens before the ultrasonic step, adding precision and reducing the amount of ultrasound energy used. After confirming the IOL is correctly positioned, the surgeon applies antibiotic and anti-inflammatory drops and places a protective shield over your eye.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of your surgery, you will be admitted to the day-care unit. The nursing team will instil dilating drops to enlarge your pupil, which is essential for the surgeon to access the lens. Your eye will be cleaned with antiseptic solution and draped with a sterile cover. You will be positioned comfortably under the operating microscope, and the local anaesthetic will be administered. You may feel slight pressure or a sensation of movement, but the procedure is not painful.

Throughout the surgery, your surgeon views your eye through a high-magnification microscope and uses foot-pedal controls to operate the phacoemulsification instrument with precision. If you are having femtosecond laser-assisted surgery, the laser step is performed first while you are positioned under a separate laser platform, after which you are moved to the operating microscope for the lens removal and IOL implantation. The entire process is monitored in real time, and your surgeon will talk you through each stage to keep you calm and informed.

Once the IOL is implanted and confirmed to be in the correct position, the surgeon removes the instruments, verifies that the incision is watertight, and applies the final medications. The protective shield is placed over your eye, and you are transferred to the recovery area for a short observation period. Most patients are ready to leave the hospital within two to three hours of arriving.

## Post-operative Care & Recovery

After surgery, you will rest briefly in the recovery area before being discharged. Your eye will be covered with a clear plastic shield, which you should wear for the first night and during sleep for the first week to prevent accidental rubbing. Vision is typically blurry on the first day, and you may experience mild discomfort, watering, or sensitivity to light—all of which are normal and usually subside within a few days.

You will be prescribed a regimen of antibiotic and anti-inflammatory eye drops to use for two to four weeks. It is critical to follow the drop schedule exactly as prescribed, as this prevents infection and controls inflammation that could affect your visual outcome. Avoid rubbing or pressing on the eye, avoid getting water in the eye for the first week, and wear sunglasses outdoors to protect against glare and dust. You can resume light activities the next day, but should avoid heavy lifting, strenuous exercise, and swimming for at least two weeks.

A follow-up review will be scheduled within the first one to three days to check the healing of your incision, the position of your IOL, and your intraocular pressure. Your surgeon will assess your visual acuity and advise when you can resume driving and other activities. If you have surgery on both eyes, the second eye is typically scheduled a few days to a week after the first, once the surgeon is satisfied with the recovery of the first eye.

## Long-term Outcomes & Success Rates

Cataract surgery has one of the highest success rates of any surgical procedure, with over 98% of patients experiencing improved vision when no other eye disease is present. Bangalore's ophthalmology centres achieve outcomes consistent with international benchmarks, thanks to experienced surgeons, advanced technology, and rigorous pre-operative assessment. Most patients enjoy significantly clearer vision, improved colour perception, and reduced glare after surgery.

The intraocular lens implanted during cataract surgery is permanent and does not need to be replaced. However, some patients develop a condition called posterior capsule opacity—sometimes referred to as a "secondary cataract"—months or years after surgery, when the membrane behind the IOL becomes cloudy. This is not a recurrence of the cataract and is easily treated with a quick, painless YAG laser capsulotomy that restores clear vision within minutes.

Your long-term visual outcome depends on the health of the rest of your eye, particularly your retina and optic nerve. If you have age-related macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy, or glaucoma, these conditions will continue to affect your vision after cataract surgery and require ongoing management. Your surgeon will discuss realistic expectations based on your individual eye health and will coordinate with your local ophthalmologist for long-term follow-up.

## Alternatives to Consider

If your cataract is mild and not significantly affecting your vision, a change in your glasses prescription may temporarily improve your sight. However, glasses cannot reverse the underlying clouding of the lens, and as the cataract progresses, surgery will eventually be needed. There is no medication, eye drop, or dietary supplement that has been proven to prevent or reverse cataracts.

For patients who have both a cataract and significant refractive error (short-sightedness, long-sightedness, or astigmatism), cataract surgery with a premium IOL can address both problems in a single procedure. If you are considering refractive lens exchange—removing a clear lens to correct vision before a cataract has formed—your surgeon will discuss the risks and benefits, as this involves the same surgical technique but is performed earlier. For patients who are not suitable for cataract surgery due to severe eye disease or general health, low-vision aids and rehabilitation can help maximise remaining vision.
