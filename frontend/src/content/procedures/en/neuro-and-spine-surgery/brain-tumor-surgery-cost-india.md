## Overview

Brain tumor surgery is a highly specialised neurosurgical procedure aimed at removing all or part of a tumour from the brain while preserving critical neurological function. The approach depends on the tumour's size, location, type, and the patient's overall health, and may involve open craniotomy, microsurgical resection, or minimally invasive endoscopic techniques. India has emerged as a leading destination for international patients seeking affordable, high-quality brain tumour surgery, with Bangalore home to several JCI-accredited hospitals equipped with intraoperative MRI, neuronavigation systems, and advanced neuro-ICUs.

The cost of brain tumour surgery in India ranges from $6,000 to $20,000, compared to $50,000-$150,000 in the United States. This dramatic cost saving does not come at the expense of quality — Indian neurosurgeons are globally trained, and top hospitals report outcomes comparable to leading Western institutions. International patients benefit from short waiting times, English-speaking medical teams, and dedicated international patient services.

## Procedure Details

Brain tumour surgery typically begins with detailed pre-operative imaging, including contrast MRI and functional MRI, to map the tumour's relationship to critical brain regions. The neurosurgeon may use neuronavigation — a GPS-like system — to plan the safest surgical corridor. In selected cases, awake craniotomy is performed to monitor speech or motor function in real time while the tumour is being resected, maximising safe removal.

During a standard craniotomy, a section of the skull is temporarily removed to access the brain. Using an operating microscope and microsurgical instruments, the surgeon carefully separates and removes the tumour, aiming for maximal safe resection. Intraoperative neuromonitoring helps protect critical nerves and pathways. For pituitary tumours, an endoscopic transsphenoidal approach through the nose avoids any external incision.

After resection, the bone flap is replaced and secured, and the patient is transferred to the neuro-ICU for close monitoring. Depending on pathology results, adjuvant treatment such as radiotherapy or chemotherapy may be recommended, and Khan Meditour can coordinate referrals to oncology specialists within the same hospital network.
