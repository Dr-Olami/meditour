## Overview

Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS) is an advanced neuromodulation therapy that uses surgically implanted electrodes to deliver precisely controlled electrical pulses to specific regions of the brain. It is the gold-standard surgical treatment for advanced Parkinson's disease, essential tremor, and dystonia in patients whose symptoms are no longer adequately controlled by medication. DBS does not cure the underlying disease but can dramatically reduce tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, and dystonic movements, significantly improving quality of life.

The procedure involves implanting thin electrodes into targeted brain structures — most commonly the subthalamic nucleus (STN) or globus pallidus internus (GPi) for Parkinson's disease, the ventral intermediate nucleus (VIM) of the thalamus for essential tremor, and the GPi for dystonia. These electrodes are connected by extension wires to an implantable pulse generator (IPG) placed under the skin in the chest or abdomen, similar to a pacemaker.

India offers DBS surgery at $15,000-$35,000, compared to $60,000-$150,000 in the United States. ## Procedure Details

DBS surgery is typically performed in two stages. In the first stage, the patient is fitted with a stereotactic head frame and undergoes an MRI to map the target brain region with sub-millimetre accuracy. Under local anaesthesia, the neurosurgeon inserts the electrodes through small burr holes in the skull. Because the patient is awake, the surgical team can perform microelectrode recording and test stimulation to confirm optimal electrode placement and assess for any side effects. In some centres, this stage is performed under general anaesthesia using intraoperative MRI guidance.

In the second stage, performed either immediately or a few days later, the extension wires are tunnelled under the skin from the scalp to the chest, where the implantable pulse generator is placed in a subcutaneous pocket. This stage is performed under general anaesthesia. The device is typically switched on 2-4 weeks after surgery to allow the brain to recover from the immediate effects of implantation.

DBS programming is a critical part of the treatment and requires multiple sessions over several weeks to optimise stimulation parameters for each patient.
