## Overview

Spinal fusion is a surgical procedure that permanently joins two or more vertebrae to eliminate painful motion and restore stability to the spine. It is commonly performed for degenerative disc disease, spondylolisthesis (slipped vertebra), spinal stenosis, spinal fractures, and certain deformities such as scoliosis. The surgery uses bone grafts — taken from the patient, a donor, or synthetic material — along with hardware such as screws, rods, and cages to hold the vertebrae together while the fusion heals.

India has become a preferred destination for spinal fusion surgery, offering costs of $5,000-$15,000 compared to $50,000-$150,000 in the United States. ## Procedure Details

Spinal fusion surgery is performed under general anaesthesia with the patient positioned face down. The surgeon makes an incision over the affected spinal segment and carefully retracts the muscles to expose the vertebrae. Using fluoroscopic guidance, pedicle screws are placed into the vertebral bodies and connected by rods to stabilise the spine. The intervertebral disc is removed and replaced with a cage packed with bone graft, which encourages the two vertebrae to fuse into a single solid unit over several months.

Minimally invasive variants such as transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF) use smaller incisions, muscle-splitting tubular retractors, and percutaneous screw placement. These techniques reduce blood loss, post-operative pain, and hospital stay, and are increasingly available at Bangalore's leading spine centres. Intraoperative neuromonitoring is used throughout to protect the spinal cord and nerve roots.

After surgery, patients spend 1-2 days in the neuro-ICU or HDU before transferring to the ward. Early mobilisation with a spinal brace is encouraged, and a structured physiotherapy programme begins before discharge. Full bony fusion typically takes 3-6 months, during which patients must avoid heavy lifting, twisting, and high-impact activities.
