## Overview

If you've been living with persistent neck or back pain that radiates into your arms or legs, makes it difficult to work, sleep, or enjoy everyday activities, and hasn't improved after months of physiotherapy, painkillers, and injections, you may be wondering whether spine surgery is your next option. Disc replacement surgery could be the answer — and it offers something spinal fusion cannot: it preserves your natural spinal movement.

Here's what the procedure does in plain terms. Between the bones of your spine sit cushion-like discs that act as shock absorbers. When one of these discs wears out or becomes damaged — a condition called degenerative disc disease — it can press on nearby nerves and cause chronic pain. Disc replacement surgery removes that damaged disc and replaces it with an artificial one made of medical-grade metal and plastic components. Unlike spinal fusion, which permanently locks two vertebrae together, this artificial disc allows your spine to keep bending and moving naturally, reducing the risk of the discs above and below wearing out prematurely.

The procedure is most commonly performed in the cervical spine (your neck) and, for carefully selected patients, in the lumbar spine (your lower back). In India, disc replacement surgery costs between $4,000 and $12,000 — compared to $40,000-$120,000 in the United States. That's up to 90% less, without compromising on the quality of the implant or the expertise of the surgeon.

Bangalore's leading spine hospitals use the same FDA-approved artificial disc implants you would receive in the West, performed by surgeons who have trained internationally and performed hundreds of these procedures. ## Who Is This Procedure For?

Disc replacement may be right for you if you have degenerative disc disease confirmed on an MRI scan that hasn't improved after at least six months of conservative treatment — including physiotherapy, pain management, and lifestyle changes. The ideal candidate has one or two affected disc levels causing persistent neck or back pain, with or without pain radiating into the arms or legs.

If you're considering cervical disc replacement, you may be experiencing chronic neck pain that travels to your shoulders or arms, sometimes with numbness or weakness. This procedure is particularly good for younger, active people who want to preserve their neck mobility.

For lumbar disc replacement, the criteria are stricter. It's best suited if you have disc-related low back pain without significant facet joint arthritis or multilevel disease. You need healthy bone quality, no prior fusion at the same level, and a BMI that supports surgical success. If you do heavy manual labour or have osteoporosis, your surgeon may recommend fusion instead.

It's important to have realistic expectations. Disc replacement can significantly reduce your pain and restore your function, but it's not a guarantee of complete pain relief. The artificial disc is designed to last for decades, but may eventually need revision.

## Procedure Details

It's natural to feel anxious about what happens during surgery. Disc replacement is performed under general anaesthesia, meaning you'll be completely asleep and won't feel anything during the procedure.

For cervical disc replacement (in your neck), your surgeon makes a small incision in the front of your neck, moves aside your windpipe and blood vessels to reach your spine, and uses a surgical microscope to remove the damaged disc and position the artificial disc between your vertebrae. The procedure usually takes 1-2 hours per disc level.

For lumbar disc replacement (in your lower back), your surgeon makes an incision in your abdomen and approaches your spine from the front, gently moving your abdominal organs aside. The diseased disc is removed and the artificial disc is inserted, with careful attention to restoring the proper height and alignment of your spine.

The artificial disc consists of two metal endplates with a sliding core made of medical-grade plastic or metal, designed to mimic your natural disc's range of motion. Your surgeon will choose the implant that best fits your anatomy.

One of the biggest advantages of disc replacement over fusion is your recovery. You'll be encouraged to get up and walk within 24 hours of surgery, often without needing a neck or back brace. Most patients stay in hospital for 3-7 days, followed by a structured physiotherapy programme. Many people return to desk work within 2-3 weeks and full activity within 6-12 weeks — noticeably faster than fusion recovery.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of your surgery, you'll be admitted to the pre-operative area where the anaesthesia team will place your IV lines and attach monitoring equipment. Once you're given general anaesthesia, you'll fall asleep comfortably and won't be aware of anything during the procedure.

Your surgeon performs the approach described above — cervical through a small neck incision, or lumbar through an abdominal incision — using a surgical microscope and X-ray guidance to precisely position the artificial disc. The size and angle are chosen to restore your natural disc height and alignment.

Once the implant is securely in place, your surgeon verifies its position with final imaging and closes the incision in layers. The procedure typically lasts 1-2 hours per level. You'll then be transferred to the recovery room, and afterwards to the spinal ward, where you'll be encouraged to start moving within 24 hours.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

**Will it hurt?** You'll have some pain and discomfort after surgery — that's normal and expected. Your pain will be well managed with oral painkillers, and it typically improves significantly within the first few days.

You'll stay in the spinal ward for 3-7 days, during which nurses and doctors will check your neurological function frequently. One advantage of disc replacement over fusion is that you usually won't need a spinal brace, which makes moving around more comfortable.

Your physiotherapy programme begins within 24-48 hours of surgery, starting with gentle mobilisation, core stability, and range of motion exercises. Most patients are walking independently by the second day. Before you're discharged, you'll have a follow-up X-ray to confirm your implant is in the correct position.

During your 1-2 week recovery in Bangalore, you'll continue physiotherapy as an outpatient, focusing on restoring functional mobility and strengthening your core and back muscles. You'll receive a detailed home exercise programme and clear activity guidelines — including avoiding heavy lifting, twisting, and high-impact activities for 6-12 weeks.

**Will you need someone with you?** Yes, having a family member or companion with you is highly recommended. They can help with daily tasks and provide emotional support.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

You deserve an honest picture of what to expect. Disc replacement surgery has excellent clinical outcomes, particularly in the cervical spine. Studies show that cervical disc replacement achieves success rates of 85-95% at 5-10 years of follow-up, with significant improvements in neck pain, arm pain, and overall function. Importantly, the rate of adjacent segment degeneration — where the discs above and below the treated level wear out — is significantly lower than after spinal fusion. This is one of the main reasons surgeons recommend disc replacement when you're a suitable candidate.

For lumbar disc replacement, outcomes are good but somewhat more variable, reflecting the greater mechanical demands on your lower back. Carefully selected patients report significant reductions in back pain and improved function, with implant survival rates of 80-90% at 10 years. Modern implants are designed to last for decades, but the implant may wear or fail over time. If revision surgery is ever needed, it is possible but technically complex.

## Alternatives to Consider

Before deciding on disc replacement, make sure you've explored all reasonable non-surgical options. These include physiotherapy, activity modification, weight management, anti-inflammatory medications, epidural steroid injections, and facet joint injections. Many people achieve meaningful relief with these measures alone.

Spinal fusion is the traditional surgical alternative and remains the preferred option if you have significant spinal instability, facet joint arthritis, spinal stenosis, or multilevel disease that makes disc replacement unsuitable. Fusion permanently joins the affected vertebrae together and is highly effective for pain relief, though it does carry a higher risk of adjacent segment degeneration over time.

If you're not fit for surgery or prefer to avoid it, pain management programmes combining medications, injections, and rehabilitation can provide sustained symptom control. Khan Meditour can arrange consultations with the spine team in Bangalore to discuss all your available options.
