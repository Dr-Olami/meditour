## Overview

Disc replacement surgery, also known as artificial disc replacement or total disc arthroplasty, is a modern spine procedure that replaces a damaged intervertebral disc with an artificial implant designed to preserve natural spinal motion. Unlike spinal fusion, which eliminates movement at the affected segment, disc replacement maintains flexibility and reduces the risk of adjacent segment degeneration — a common long-term complication of fusion.

The procedure is most commonly performed in the cervical spine (neck) for degenerative disc disease causing arm pain or neurological symptoms, and increasingly in the lumbar spine for carefully selected patients. India offers disc replacement surgery at $4,000-$12,000, compared to $40,000-$120,000 in the United States, making it one of the most cost-effective destinations for this advanced procedure.

## Procedure Details

Disc replacement surgery is performed under general anaesthesia. For cervical disc replacement, the surgeon makes a small incision in the front of the neck and carefully moves aside the trachea, oesophagus, and blood vessels to reach the spine. The damaged disc is removed, the spinal canal is decompressed to relieve nerve pressure, and the artificial disc implant is precisely positioned between the vertebral bodies under fluoroscopic guidance.

For lumbar disc replacement, an anterior approach through the abdomen is used to access the lower spine. The diseased disc is excised and the artificial disc is inserted, with careful attention to restoring proper disc height and spinal alignment. The implant typically consists of two metal endplates with a polyethylene or metal core that allows controlled motion in multiple planes.

Patients are encouraged to mobilise within 24 hours of surgery, often without the need for a spinal brace. Most stay in hospital for 3-7 days, followed by a structured physiotherapy programme. Recovery is generally faster than spinal fusion, with many patients returning to desk work within 2-3 weeks and full activity within 6-12 weeks.
