## Overview

If you have been struggling with severe obesity and related health conditions — and diet, exercise and medication have not produced lasting results — you may be considering gastric sleeve surgery as a path toward meaningful, long-term weight loss. It is completely understandable to want a solution that addresses not just the number on the scale but the broader impact that obesity has on your health, your confidence and your quality of life.

Gastric sleeve surgery, also known as sleeve gastrectomy, is one of the most commonly performed and effective weight-loss procedures available today. It works through a single, powerful mechanism: the surgeon removes approximately 80% of your stomach, leaving behind a narrow, tube-shaped sleeve that resembles a banana. This dramatically reduces the amount of food you can eat and also decreases the production of ghrelin — the hormone responsible for hunger — so you feel less hungry between meals. Because the procedure does not involve bypassing any portion of your intestine, it is considered a purely restrictive operation, which means the risk of nutritional deficiencies is lower than with procedures that combine restriction with malabsorption.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Gastric sleeve surgery is designed for individuals with severe obesity who have not achieved sustained weight loss through non-surgical methods. You may be a suitable candidate if your body mass index (BMI) is 40 or higher, or if your BMI is 35 or higher and you have obesity-related health conditions such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, severe sleep apnoea or significant joint problems.

The procedure is an excellent option for patients who want significant weight loss without the intestinal rerouting and malabsorption associated with gastric bypass. Because the digestive tract remains intact, food continues to pass through the stomach and intestines in the normal sequence, which means the risk of nutritional deficiencies, dumping syndrome and other malabsorption-related complications is substantially lower. This makes the sleeve particularly suitable for patients who may struggle with the strict, lifelong supplementation required after bypass, or those who have conditions that make intestinal surgery higher risk.

To be eligible, you should have attempted sustained weight loss through diet, exercise and medication for at least six to twelve months without success. You will undergo a comprehensive evaluation that includes a nutritional assessment, psychological screening and medical review by the bariatric team. This ensures you are physically and mentally prepared for the significant lifestyle changes required after surgery. Smokers must quit at least four weeks before and after surgery, as nicotine impairs wound healing and increases the risk of complications.

## Pre-operative Preparation

Thorough preparation is essential for a safe and successful gastric sleeve. Three to four weeks before your surgery, you will complete a series of evaluations including blood tests, an upper gastrointestinal endoscopy, a cardiac assessment with ECG, and a pulmonary function test if indicated. A pre-anaesthetic evaluation will confirm your fitness for general anaesthesia.

You will be asked to follow a pre-operative diet — typically a high-protein, low-calorie liquid diet for 1-2 weeks before surgery. This reduces the size of your liver, making the laparoscopic procedure safer and technically easier, as an enlarged fatty liver can obstruct the surgeon's view of the upper stomach. Your bariatric team will provide detailed dietary instructions.

Stop taking blood-thinning medications such as aspirin, ibuprofen and certain herbal supplements at least two weeks before surgery. If you smoke, you must stop completely at least four weeks prior. Arrange for a companion to travel with you, as you will need assistance during your hospital stay and the first week of recovery at your accommodation. On the day before surgery, you will fast for at least eight hours and shower with an antibacterial soap.

## Procedure Details

Gastric sleeve surgery in Bangalore is performed laparoscopically, meaning the surgeon makes five to six small incisions (each 5-12 mm) in your abdomen rather than a single large open cut. A laparoscope — a thin tube with a high-definition camera — is inserted through one incision, and specialised surgical instruments are passed through the others. This minimally invasive approach results in less pain, smaller scars, shorter hospital stays and faster recovery compared to open surgery.

The sleeve gastrectomy involves a single key step. The surgeon inserts a calibration tube (bougie) through your mouth and into your stomach to act as a guide for the size of the new sleeve. Using advanced laparoscopic staplers with reinforced cartridges, the surgeon then divides the stomach vertically along its greater curvature, removing approximately 80% of the stomach and leaving behind a narrow sleeve that holds roughly 100-150 millilitres. The removed portion of the stomach is extracted through one of the slightly larger incisions. Unlike gastric bypass, no intestine is divided or reconnected, which makes the procedure technically simpler and reduces operative time.

The entire operation typically takes 1-2 hours, depending on your anatomy and whether any adhesions from previous surgeries are present. The staple line is often reinforced with sutures or an absorbable buttressing material to reduce the risk of bleeding and leaks.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of your surgery, you will be admitted to the hospital and prepared in the surgical suite. The anaesthetist will administer general anaesthesia, so you will be completely asleep and will not feel any pain during the procedure. Once you are anaesthetised and monitored, the surgical team will prepare your abdomen with antiseptic solution and insert the laparoscopic ports.

The surgeon begins by inflating your abdomen with carbon dioxide gas to create a working space. The laparoscope is inserted, and the surgical team visualises your stomach on high-definition monitors. The calibration bougie is passed into the stomach to size the sleeve. Using advanced stapling devices, the surgeon creates a row of staples along the greater curvature of the stomach, progressively dividing and separating the sleeve from the portion that will be removed. The excised stomach is placed in a retrieval bag and removed through an enlarged port site.

Throughout the procedure, the surgical team monitors your vital signs, fluid balance and blood oxygen levels. Once the sleeve is complete, the surgeon tests the staple line for leaks by injecting a coloured dye or air through a tube and inspecting the line under direct vision. The abdominal incisions are then closed with dissolvable sutures, and small dressings are applied. You are transferred to the recovery room for close monitoring as the anaesthesia wears off.

**Will it hurt?** Under general anaesthesia you will not feel anything during the procedure. After surgery, you will experience abdominal pain and discomfort, particularly around the incision sites, but this is well managed with intravenous pain medication during your hospital stay and oral analgesics after discharge.

## Post-operative Care & Recovery

After surgery, you will remain in hospital for 2-3 days for close monitoring. You will be encouraged to sit up and walk short distances within 24 hours of surgery to promote circulation, prevent blood clots and support lung function. Pain and nausea are most pronounced in the first 48 hours and are managed with medication.

Your diet will progress in stages. On the first day after surgery, you will be allowed small sips of water. This progresses to clear liquids, then full liquids, pureed foods and finally soft foods over the following weeks. Your dietitian will provide a detailed dietary plan and explain the importance of eating slowly, chewing thoroughly and stopping as soon as you feel full.

A follow-up appointment is scheduled within 10-14 days to assess wound healing, review your dietary progress and clear you for international travel. You should avoid heavy lifting and strenuous activity for at least four to six weeks. Walking is encouraged from the first day, and you can gradually increase your activity level as your recovery progresses.

Watch for signs of complications including fever, severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, rapid heart rate or redness and discharge at the incision sites. These could indicate a leak, infection or other issue that requires prompt medical attention.

## Long-term Outcomes & Success Rates

Gastric sleeve surgery is one of the most effective treatments for severe obesity, with patients typically losing 50-70% of their excess body weight within 12-18 months after surgery. Weight loss is most rapid in the first six months and gradually stabilises over the following year. Beyond weight loss, the procedure produces significant improvements in obesity-related conditions — type 2 diabetes improves or resolves in approximately 60-70% of patients, hypertension improves in roughly 60%, and sleep apnoea resolves in the majority of cases.

Studies report long-term success rates of 80-85% for gastric sleeve, with the majority of patients maintaining substantial weight loss at five years and beyond. Because the procedure reduces ghrelin production, many patients experience a lasting reduction in appetite, which supports long-term dietary compliance. However, long-term success depends on your commitment to dietary changes, regular exercise, vitamin and mineral supplementation, and ongoing follow-up with your bariatric team.

Nutritional deficiencies can still occur, particularly vitamin B12, iron, calcium and vitamin D, though they are less common and less severe than after gastric bypass. These are preventable with proper supplementation and regular blood monitoring. Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) can develop or worsen in some patients after sleeve gastrectomy, and your surgeon will discuss this risk with you during your consultation, particularly if you already experience heartburn.

## Alternatives to Consider

If gastric sleeve is not the right fit for you, several alternatives may be more appropriate depending on your health profile and weight-loss goals. Gastric bypass surgery (Roux-en-Y) combines restriction with malabsorption by creating a small stomach pouch and bypassing a portion of the small intestine. It typically produces slightly greater weight loss and is particularly effective for patients with type 2 diabetes, but carries a higher risk of nutritional deficiencies and dumping syndrome.

The mini gastric bypass (MGB) is a simplified version of the traditional Roux-en-Y bypass with a single anastomosis rather than two. It offers comparable weight loss with a shorter operative time and potentially fewer complications. For patients with a lower BMI (30-35), non-surgical options such as the intragastric balloon or endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty may provide moderate weight loss without surgery.

Revisional bariatric surgery is available for patients who have experienced inadequate weight loss, weight regain or complications from a previous bariatric procedure. A gastric sleeve can, in some cases, be converted to a gastric bypass if reflux or inadequate weight loss becomes a problem. During your consultation, the bariatric team will discuss all viable options and recommend the approach that best matches your anatomy, health conditions and long-term goals.
