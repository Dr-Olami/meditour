## Overview

If you have been struggling with severe obesity and related health conditions — and diet, exercise and medication have not produced lasting results — you may be considering gastric bypass surgery as a path toward meaningful, long-term weight loss. It is completely understandable to want a solution that addresses not just the number on the scale but the broader impact that obesity has on your health, your confidence and your quality of life.

Gastric bypass surgery, specifically the Roux-en-Y gastric bypass, is one of the most effective and well-established weight-loss procedures available today. It works through two mechanisms: it creates a small stomach pouch that restricts how much you can eat, and it bypasses a portion of your small intestine, reducing the number of calories and nutrients your body absorbs. This dual approach — restriction combined with malabsorption — typically produces greater and more sustained weight loss than restrictive procedures alone.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Gastric bypass surgery is designed for individuals with severe obesity who have not achieved sustained weight loss through non-surgical methods. You may be a suitable candidate if your body mass index (BMI) is 40 or higher, or if your BMI is 35 or higher and you have obesity-related health conditions such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, severe sleep apnoea or significant joint problems.

The procedure is particularly effective for patients with type 2 diabetes, as the bypass of the duodenum and proximal small intestine can lead to rapid improvement in blood sugar control — often before significant weight loss occurs. This metabolic effect is one of the reasons gastric bypass is considered the gold standard for patients with diabetes and severe obesity.

To be eligible, you should have attempted sustained weight loss through diet, exercise and medication for at least six to twelve months without success. You will undergo a comprehensive evaluation that includes a nutritional assessment, psychological screening and medical review by the bariatric team. This ensures you are physically and mentally prepared for the significant lifestyle changes required after surgery. Smokers must quit at least four weeks before and after surgery, as nicotine impairs wound healing and increases the risk of complications.

## Pre-operative Preparation

Thorough preparation is essential for a safe and successful gastric bypass. Three to four weeks before your surgery, you will complete a series of evaluations including blood tests, an upper gastrointestinal endoscopy, a cardiac assessment with ECG, and a pulmonary function test if indicated. A pre-anaesthetic evaluation will confirm your fitness for general anaesthesia.

You will be asked to follow a pre-operative diet — typically a high-protein, low-calorie liquid diet for 1-2 weeks before surgery. This reduces the size of your liver, making the laparoscopic procedure safer and technically easier, as an enlarged fatty liver can obstruct the surgeon's view of the upper stomach. Your bariatric team will provide detailed dietary instructions.

Stop taking blood-thinning medications such as aspirin, ibuprofen and certain herbal supplements at least two weeks before surgery. If you smoke, you must stop completely at least four weeks prior. Arrange for a companion to travel with you, as you will need assistance during your hospital stay and the first week of recovery at your accommodation. On the day before surgery, you will fast for at least eight hours and shower with an antibacterial soap.

## Procedure Details

Gastric bypass surgery in Bangalore is performed laparoscopically, meaning the surgeon makes five to six small incisions (each 5-12 mm) in your abdomen rather than a single large open cut. A laparoscope — a thin tube with a high-definition camera — is inserted through one incision, and specialised surgical instruments are passed through the others. This minimally invasive approach results in less pain, smaller scars, shorter hospital stays and faster recovery compared to open surgery.

The Roux-en-Y gastric bypass involves two key steps. First, the surgeon creates a small pouch — approximately 15-30 millilitres in volume — from the upper portion of your stomach, effectively separating it from the larger remaining stomach. This pouch can hold only a small amount of food, which is what restricts your intake. Second, the surgeon divides the small intestine and connects the lower portion (the Roux limb) directly to the new stomach pouch, bypassing the remaining stomach, duodenum and first portion of the jejunum. Food now travels from the pouch directly into the lower small intestine, reducing the absorption of calories and nutrients.

The procedure uses advanced laparoscopic staplers with reinforced cartridges to create secure closures on the stomach and intestinal connections. The entire operation typically takes 2-4 hours, depending on your anatomy and whether any adhesions from previous surgeries are present.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of your surgery, you will be admitted to the hospital and prepared in the surgical suite. The anaesthetist will administer general anaesthesia, so you will be completely asleep and will not feel any pain during the procedure. Once you are anaesthetised and monitored, the surgical team will prepare your abdomen with antiseptic solution and insert the laparoscopic ports.

The surgeon begins by inflating your abdomen with carbon dioxide gas to create a working space. The laparoscope is inserted, and the surgical team visualises your stomach and intestines on high-definition monitors. Using advanced stapling devices, the surgeon creates the small gastric pouch from the upper stomach. The small intestine is then divided approximately 50-100 cm below the stomach, and the Roux limb is brought up and connected to the new pouch using either stapled or hand-sewn anastomosis.

Throughout the procedure, the surgical team monitors your vital signs, fluid balance and blood oxygen levels. Once the bypass is complete, the surgeon tests the connections for leaks by injecting a coloured dye or air through a tube. The abdominal incisions are then closed with dissolvable sutures, and small dressings are applied. You are transferred to the recovery room for close monitoring as the anaesthesia wears off.

**Will it hurt?** Under general anaesthesia you will not feel anything during the procedure. After surgery, you will experience abdominal pain and discomfort, particularly around the incision sites, but this is well managed with intravenous pain medication during your hospital stay and oral analgesics after discharge.

## Post-operative Care & Recovery

After surgery, you will remain in hospital for 3-4 days for close monitoring. You will be encouraged to sit up and walk short distances within 24 hours of surgery to promote circulation, prevent blood clots and support lung function. Pain and nausea are most pronounced in the first 48 hours and are managed with medication.

Your diet will progress in stages. On the first day after surgery, you will be allowed small sips of water. This progresses to clear liquids, then full liquids, pureed foods and finally soft foods over the following weeks. Your dietitian will provide a detailed dietary plan and explain the importance of eating slowly, chewing thoroughly and stopping as soon as you feel full.

A follow-up appointment is scheduled within 10-14 days to assess wound healing, review your dietary progress and clear you for international travel. You should avoid heavy lifting and strenuous activity for at least six weeks. Walking is encouraged from the first day, and you can gradually increase your activity level as your recovery progresses.

Watch for signs of complications including fever, severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, rapid heart rate or redness and discharge at the incision sites. These could indicate a leak, infection or other issue that requires prompt medical attention.

## Long-term Outcomes & Success Rates

Gastric bypass surgery is one of the most effective treatments for severe obesity, with patients typically losing 60-80% of their excess body weight within 12-18 months after surgery. Weight loss is most rapid in the first six months and gradually stabilises over the following year. Beyond weight loss, the procedure produces significant improvements in obesity-related conditions — type 2 diabetes resolves or improves in over 80% of patients, hypertension improves in approximately 70%, and sleep apnoea resolves in the majority of cases.

Studies report long-term success rates of 85-90% for gastric bypass, with the majority of patients maintaining substantial weight loss at five years and beyond. However, long-term success depends on your commitment to dietary changes, regular exercise, lifelong vitamin and mineral supplementation, and ongoing follow-up with your bariatric team.

Nutritional deficiencies are the most common long-term complication, particularly vitamin B12, iron, calcium and vitamin D. These are preventable with proper supplementation and regular blood monitoring. Dumping syndrome — causing nausea, sweating, cramping and diarrhoea after consuming sugary foods — occurs in many patients and actually serves as a deterrent to eating high-sugar foods, supporting long-term dietary compliance.

## Follow-up Care After Returning Home

After returning home, it is essential to maintain regular contact with both your local physician and your bariatric surgeon in Bangalore. Schedule a visit with your local doctor within two weeks of returning to confirm that your healing is progressing well and to arrange baseline blood tests for nutritional markers. Share your discharge summary, surgical notes and dietary plan with your local doctor so they have a complete record.

Lifelong vitamin and mineral supplementation is non-negotiable after gastric bypass. Your supplementation protocol will typically include a daily multivitamin, calcium with vitamin D, iron, and monthly vitamin B12 injections or high-dose oral supplements. Blood tests should be performed at three months, six months and then annually to monitor for deficiencies.

## Alternatives to Consider

If gastric bypass is not the right fit for you, several alternatives may be more appropriate depending on your health profile and weight-loss goals. Gastric sleeve surgery (sleeve gastrectomy) is a purely restrictive procedure that removes approximately 80% of the stomach without intestinal bypass, resulting in fewer nutritional deficiencies and a simpler surgical approach, though weight loss may be slightly less than with bypass.

The mini gastric bypass (MGB) is a simplified version of the traditional Roux-en-Y bypass with a single anastomosis rather than two. It offers comparable weight loss with a shorter operative time and potentially fewer complications. For patients with a lower BMI (30-35), non-surgical options such as the intragastric balloon or endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty may provide moderate weight loss without surgery.

Revisional bariatric surgery is available for patients who have experienced inadequate weight loss, weight regain or complications from a previous bariatric procedure. During your consultation, the bariatric team will discuss all viable options and recommend the approach that best matches your anatomy, health conditions and long-term goals.
