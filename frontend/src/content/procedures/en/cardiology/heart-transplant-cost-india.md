## Overview

Heart transplantation is the definitive treatment for end-stage heart failure when all other medical and surgical options have been exhausted. The procedure involves removing the patient's diseased heart and replacing it with a healthy heart from a brain-dead organ donor. It is reserved for patients with severe heart failure who have a poor prognosis without transplantation and who meet strict medical and psychological criteria.

Heart transplantation offers remarkable outcomes for appropriately selected patients, with 1-year survival exceeding 90% and many recipients returning to near-normal activity levels. The procedure requires a coordinated multi-disciplinary team including cardiac surgeons, transplant cardiologists, immunologists, infectious disease specialists, and transplant coordinators, along with a robust organ procurement and allocation network.

Bangalore's transplant centres are among India's most experienced, with dedicated heart transplant programmes accredited by the National Organ and Tissue Transplant Organisation (NOTTO). International patients benefit from shorter waiting times compared to many Western countries, experienced surgical teams, and costs that are up to 85% lower than in the United States, making this life-saving procedure accessible to patients who face prohibitive costs or long waiting lists at home.

## Procedure Details

Heart transplantation is performed under general anaesthesia with cardiopulmonary bypass and typically takes 4-6 hours. The procedure is coordinated with the organ retrieval team to minimise ischaemic time (the period the donor heart is without blood flow), which should ideally be under 4-6 hours. The recipient's diseased heart is excised, leaving the posterior atrial walls and vena cava connections intact for the donor heart anastomosis.

The donor heart is prepared and connected to the recipient's remaining atrial tissue, aorta, pulmonary artery, and vena cava using a series of sutures (biatrial or bicaval technique). The bicaval technique is increasingly preferred as it preserves atrial geometry and reduces atrial arrhythmias. After completing the anastomoses, air is evacuated from the heart, the aortic clamp is released, and the donor heart is reperfused and allowed to resume normal sinus rhythm.

Following surgery, the patient is transferred to the transplant ICU for intensive monitoring. Immunosuppression is initiated immediately with induction therapy (anti-thymocyte globulin or basiliximab), followed by maintenance immunosuppression (tacrolimus, mycophenolate mofetil, and corticosteroids). Endomyocardial biopsies are performed at regular intervals to detect rejection. The ICU stay typically lasts 5-10 days, with total hospital stay of 14-21 days. Post-discharge care includes close outpatient monitoring, immunosuppression level checks, infection surveillance, and a structured cardiac rehabilitation programme.
