## Overview

Atrial Septal Defect (ASD) and Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD) are congenital heart conditions characterised by holes in the wall (septum) that separates the heart's upper or lower chambers. These defects allow oxygen-rich blood to mix with oxygen-poor blood, causing the heart and lungs to work harder. Over time, significant defects can lead to right heart enlargement, pulmonary hypertension, arrhythmias, and heart failure if left untreated.

ASD/VSD closure surgery involves repairing the septal defect using a patch of the patient's own tissue (pericardium) or a synthetic patch (Dacron or Gore-Tex), or in some cases, direct suturing of small defects. The procedure is performed under cardiopulmonary bypass and has excellent long-term outcomes, with most patients achieving complete closure and normal cardiac function post-operatively.

## Procedure Details

ASD/VSD closure is performed under general anaesthesia with cardiopulmonary bypass (heart-lung machine) and typically takes 2-3 hours. The surgeon opens the chest through a median sternotomy, and the heart is arrested with cold cardioplegia solution. The right atrium is opened to access the ASD, or the right atrium or pulmonary artery is opened to access the VSD, depending on its location.

For an ASD, the defect is inspected and closed either by direct suture (for small defects) or by sewing in a patch (pericardial or synthetic) that covers the hole. For a VSD, the surgeon visualises the defect through the tricuspid valve or via a right ventriculotomy (rarely), and closes it with a Dacron or pericardial patch using continuous or interrupted pledgeted sutures. Intraoperative transoesophageal echocardiography (TEE) is used to confirm complete closure and assess valve function before weaning from bypass.

After the procedure, the heart is restarted, bypass is discontinued, and the chest is closed with wires. Patients spend 24-48 hours in the cardiac ICU with continuous monitoring. A post-operative echocardiogram is performed to confirm complete defect closure and assess cardiac function. Early mobilisation begins on the first post-operative day, with discharge typically at 4-7 days. Post-discharge care includes wound monitoring, activity restrictions, and a follow-up echocardiogram before flying home.
