## Overview

Pediatric heart surgery encompasses a wide range of procedures to correct congenital heart defects (CHDs) present from birth. These defects range from relatively simple conditions such as atrial and ventricular septal defects to complex single-ventricle physiology requiring staged surgical reconstruction. Congenital heart disease affects approximately 8-10 per 1,000 live births, and many defects require surgical intervention in infancy or early childhood to ensure normal growth, development, and life expectancy.

Bangalore's paediatric cardiac centres are among the most experienced in Asia, performing the full spectrum of congenital heart surgery from neonatal arterial switch operations to Fontan completion for single-ventricle patients. The surgical teams include paediatric cardiac surgeons, paediatric cardiac anaesthesiologists, and dedicated paediatric cardiac intensivists, supported by state-of-the-art paediatric cardiac catheterisation and intensive care facilities.

International families choose Bangalore for their children's heart surgery because of the combination of world-class surgical expertise, child-friendly hospital environments, and costs that are up to 90% lower than in Western countries. Dedicated international patient teams coordinate every aspect of the family's journey, from visa assistance to post-operative follow-up planning, ensuring families can focus on their child's recovery.

## Procedure Details

Pediatric heart surgery is performed under general anaesthesia with cardiopulmonary bypass adapted for paediatric patients, including miniaturised circuits and blood conservation strategies. The specific technique depends on the congenital defect being corrected. For tetralogy of Fallot, the surgeon closes the ventricular septal defect and relieves the right ventricular outflow tract obstruction. For transposition of the great arteries, an arterial switch operation repositions the aorta and pulmonary artery to their correct anatomical positions.

For single-ventricle conditions such as hypoplastic left heart syndrome, surgery is staged across the child's early years: the Norwood or hybrid procedure in the neonatal period, the bidirectional Glenn at 4-6 months, and the Fontan completion at 2-4 years. Each stage is carefully planned based on the child's growth, pulmonary vascular resistance, and cardiac function.

Intraoperative transoesophageal echocardiography (TEE) and, in smaller infants, epicardial echocardiography are used to confirm the surgical result before weaning from bypass. After surgery, the child is transferred to the paediatric cardiac ICU for ventilator support, haemodynamic monitoring, and gradual weaning from inotropes and ventilation. The ICU stay typically ranges from 3-7 days, with total hospital stay of 7-14 days depending on the complexity of the procedure. Post-discharge care includes feeding support, wound monitoring, and a detailed follow-up plan coordinated with the family's home paediatric cardiology team.
