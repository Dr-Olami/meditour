## Overview

As a parent, learning that your child has a congenital heart defect is one of the most frightening moments you can face. Your child's safety is your top priority, and you want the very best surgical care available. Pediatric heart surgery corrects congenital heart defects (CHDs) that are present from birth, ranging from relatively simple conditions such as atrial and ventricular septal defects to complex single-ventricle physiology requiring staged surgical reconstruction. Congenital heart disease affects approximately 8-10 per 1,000 live births, and many defects require surgical intervention in infancy or early childhood to ensure normal growth, development, and life expectancy.

It is natural to feel anxious about your child's surgery, but you should know that Bangalore's paediatric cardiac centres are among the most experienced in Asia. These hospitals perform the full spectrum of congenital heart surgery, from neonatal arterial switch operations to Fontan completion for single-ventricle patients. The surgical teams include paediatric cardiac surgeons, paediatric cardiac anaesthesiologists, and dedicated paediatric cardiac intensivists, supported by state-of-the-art paediatric cardiac catheterisation and intensive care facilities designed specifically for infants and children.

International families from Bangladesh, the Gulf countries, Africa, and beyond choose Bangalore for their children's heart surgery because of the combination of world-class surgical expertise, child-friendly hospital environments, and costs that are up to 90% lower than in Western countries. Dedicated international patient teams coordinate every aspect of your family's journey, from visa assistance to post-operative follow-up planning, so that you can focus entirely on your child's recovery.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Pediatric heart surgery is recommended for infants and children diagnosed with congenital heart defects that cannot be safely managed with medication alone. If your child has been diagnosed with tetralogy of Fallot, transposition of the great arteries, atrioventricular septal defect, or single-ventricle physiology, surgery is usually necessary to restore normal circulation and support healthy growth. Your paediatric cardiologist will recommend surgery based on the specific defect, your child's symptoms, and their overall condition.

Many parents first learn of their child's heart condition during a routine ultrasound or shortly after birth, when a bluish tint (cyanosis), rapid breathing, or failure to thrive prompts further investigation. If your child has been referred for surgery, you may be feeling overwhelmed by the decisions ahead. You are not alone—hundreds of international families travel to Bangalore each year for exactly this reason, and the surgical teams are deeply experienced in guiding parents through every step.

Children who are good candidates for surgery typically have adequate weight and overall condition for cardiopulmonary bypass, though even small or fragile infants can often be treated with staged or hybrid approaches. Your surgical team will assess your child individually and explain the recommended plan in plain language, so you can make informed decisions with confidence.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Once you arrive in Bangalore, your child will undergo a thorough pre-operative evaluation. This includes a detailed echocardiogram, blood tests, and sometimes a cardiac catheterisation to measure pressures inside the heart and lungs. Your paediatric cardiac surgeon will sit down with you to explain the planned procedure, the expected benefits, and the risks, and you will have the opportunity to ask every question on your mind.

In the days before surgery, keep your child well-hydrated and follow the fasting instructions given by the anaesthesia team. Bring a familiar toy or blanket to comfort your child in the hospital. As a parent, you will be able to stay with your child until they are taken into the operating theatre, and you will be reunited in the paediatric ICU as soon as they are stable.

## Procedure Details

Pediatric heart surgery is performed under general anaesthesia with cardiopulmonary bypass adapted specifically for paediatric patients, including miniaturised circuits and blood conservation strategies to protect your child's small blood volume. The specific technique depends on the congenital defect being corrected. For tetralogy of Fallot, the surgeon closes the ventricular septal defect and relieves the right ventricular outflow tract obstruction so that blood can flow properly to your child's lungs. For transposition of the great arteries, an arterial switch operation repositions the aorta and pulmonary artery to their correct anatomical positions, restoring normal circulation.

For single-ventricle conditions such as hypoplastic left heart syndrome, surgery is staged across your child's early years: the Norwood or hybrid procedure in the neonatal period, the bidirectional Glenn at 4-6 months, and the Fontan completion at 2-4 years. Each stage is carefully planned based on your child's growth, pulmonary vascular resistance, and cardiac function, and your surgical team will walk you through every step before you consent.

Intraoperative transoesophageal echocardiography (TEE) and, in smaller infants, epicardial echocardiography are used to confirm the surgical result before weaning from bypass. After surgery, your child is transferred to the paediatric cardiac ICU for ventilator support, haemodynamic monitoring, and gradual weaning from inotropes and ventilation. The ICU stay typically ranges from 3-7 days, with a total hospital stay of 7-14 days depending on the complexity of the procedure. Post-discharge care includes feeding support, wound monitoring, and a detailed follow-up plan coordinated with your home paediatric cardiology team.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of surgery, your child is given general anaesthesia and carefully monitored throughout the procedure. The surgical team uses cardiopulmonary bypass to take over the work of the heart and lungs, allowing the surgeon to operate on a still, bloodless heart. Depending on the defect, the surgeon will close holes in the heart, repair or replace valves, or reconstruct the major blood vessels to restore normal circulation.

The procedure typically lasts between 3 and 6 hours, depending on complexity. Throughout the operation, the team uses echocardiography to confirm that the repair is complete and effective before your child comes off bypass. Once the surgery is finished, your child is transferred directly to the paediatric cardiac ICU, where specialist nurses and intensivists provide round-the-clock monitoring. You will be updated regularly by the surgical team and can see your child once they are settled in the ICU.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

The first few days after surgery are the most critical, and your child will be closely monitored in the paediatric cardiac ICU. Most children need ventilator support for a day or two, with gradual weaning as their heart function stabilises. Chest tubes, intravenous lines, and monitoring leads are removed one by one as your child recovers. The ICU team will keep you informed at every stage and encourage your involvement in your child's care as soon as it is safe.

Once your child is transferred to the ward, the focus shifts to feeding, pain management, and gentle activity. Most children are ready to go home from the hospital within 7-14 days, though complex repairs may require a longer stay. Before discharge, the team will teach you how to care for the surgical wound, recognise signs of complications, and manage medications. You will stay in Bangalore for another 1-2 weeks for follow-up echocardiography and a fitness-to-fly assessment.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

Bangalore's leading paediatric cardiac centres achieve surgical success rates of 95-98% for common congenital heart procedures, comparable to the best hospitals in the world. The long-term outlook for children who undergo successful congenital heart surgery is excellent, with most children going on to attend school, play sports, and live full, active lives. However, lifelong follow-up with a paediatric and later an adult congenital cardiologist is essential, as some children may need further procedures as they grow.

Your surgical team will provide a detailed discharge summary and follow-up plan to share with your cardiologist at home. This ensures continuity of care and gives your local doctor the information needed to monitor your child's progress over the years to come.

## Follow-Up Care After Returning Home

After you return home, schedule a follow-up appointment with your paediatric cardiologist within 2-4 weeks. Bring all discharge documents, surgical reports, and echocardiogram images from Bangalore. Your child will need regular echocardiograms—typically at 6 months, 1 year, and annually thereafter—to monitor the repair and heart function. If your child develops a fever, breathing difficulty, or changes in skin colour, contact your cardiologist promptly.

## Alternatives to Consider

In some cases, congenital heart defects can be treated with catheter-based procedures rather than open surgery. For example, atrial septal defects and certain valve problems may be repaired with devices inserted through a blood vessel, avoiding the need for a surgical incision and cardiopulmonary bypass. Your heart team in Bangalore will evaluate whether your child is a candidate for a catheter approach or whether surgery remains the safest and most effective option. In rare cases, a hybrid procedure combining both techniques may be recommended.
