## Overview

Coronary angioplasty, formally known as percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), is a minimally invasive procedure that opens narrowed or blocked coronary arteries to restore blood flow to the heart muscle. A balloon-tipped catheter is threaded through an artery in the wrist (radial) or groin (femoral) to the site of the blockage, where the balloon is inflated to compress the plaque against the artery wall. A stent—a tiny wire mesh tube—is then deployed to keep the vessel open.

Angioplasty with stenting is the most common cardiac intervention worldwide and is used to treat both stable coronary artery disease and acute heart attacks (primary PCI). Bangalore's interventional cardiologists use the latest-generation drug-eluting stents and advanced imaging techniques such as intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) and optical coherence tomography (OCT) to optimise stent placement.

International patients benefit from Bangalore's combination of round-the-clock catheterisation laboratories, experienced interventional teams, and costs that are up to 85% lower than in Western countries. The procedure is typically performed under local anaesthesia with conscious sedation, allowing a rapid recovery and short hospital stay.

## Procedure Details

The procedure is performed in a cardiac catheterisation laboratory under local anaesthesia and mild sedation, typically taking 30-90 minutes. After numbing the access site (usually the radial artery in the wrist), a sheath is inserted and a guiding catheter is advanced to the coronary artery origin. Contrast dye is injected to visualise the blockages on live X-ray imaging (fluoroscopy).

A guidewire is carefully passed across the lesion, and a balloon catheter is advanced over the wire to the narrowing. The balloon is inflated to high pressure, compressing the atherosclerotic plaque and expanding the artery. A drug-eluting stent mounted on a balloon is then positioned at the lesion and deployed by balloon inflation. The stent remains permanently in the artery, acting as a scaffold to maintain patency.

After confirming adequate stent expansion with repeat angiography or intravascular imaging, the catheters are removed and the access site is sealed with a closure device or manual compression. Patients are monitored in the cardiac care unit for 12-24 hours, during which dual antiplatelet therapy is started and cardiac enzymes are checked. Most patients are discharged within 1-3 days with detailed medication instructions and a follow-up plan.
