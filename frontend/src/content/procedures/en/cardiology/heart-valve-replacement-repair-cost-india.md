## Overview

If you have been told that one of your heart valves needs repairing or replacing, you are probably feeling a mix of anxiety and relief — anxiety about facing open-heart surgery, but relief that there is a clear solution for the breathlessness, fatigue or chest pain you have been living with. These feelings are completely natural. Heart valve surgery is one of the most established and successful cardiac procedures, and Bangalore's cardiac surgeons have performed thousands of these operations with excellent outcomes.

Your heart has four valves — mitral, aortic, tricuspid and pulmonary — that ensure one-way blood flow through its chambers. When a valve becomes narrowed (stenosis) or starts leaking (regurgitation), your heart has to work harder, leading to breathlessness, fatigue, chest pain and eventually heart failure if left untreated. Valve repair preserves your own tissue and is preferred when the anatomy allows, particularly for mitral valve regurgitation. Valve replacement uses either mechanical valves (durable and lifelong, but requiring anticoagulation) or biological tissue valves (no long-term blood thinners, but limited lifespan).

Bangalore's cardiac surgeons are skilled in both repair and replacement techniques, including minimally invasive approaches for selected patients. You will have access to the latest-generation prosthetic valves from global manufacturers and costs up to 90% lower than in the United States — with no compromise in valve quality or surgical expertise.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Heart valve surgery is recommended if you have moderate to severe valve disease confirmed on echocardiography. Common indications include aortic stenosis with symptoms such as exertional breathlessness, angina or syncope; mitral regurgitation causing volume overload and declining left ventricular function; and tricuspid valve disease often associated with left-sided pathology.

If you have endocarditis causing valve destruction that cannot be managed with antibiotics alone, you may need urgent surgical intervention. If you have a congenital valve abnormality such as bicuspid aortic valve disease, early surgical planning may benefit you. Even if you are asymptomatic, if your severe valve dysfunction is causing ventricular enlargement or reduced pumping function, surgery may be advised to prevent irreversible cardiac damage.

You must be fit for general anaesthesia and cardiopulmonary bypass. If you have significant comorbidities such as severe lung disease, advanced kidney failure or frailty, a less invasive approach or staged treatment plan may be recommended. Your treating cardiologist in Bangalore will conduct a thorough pre-operative assessment to determine your suitability.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Preparation begins in your home country — share a referral letter, recent echocardiogram and relevant medical records with the Bangalore surgical team for review. Once you arrive, a comprehensive evaluation is conducted over 2–3 days. This includes a detailed transthoracic and transoesophageal echocardiogram, coronary angiography to rule out concurrent artery disease, blood tests and a pulmonary function test if needed.

The anaesthesia team will assess your fitness for bypass and review your medication history. If you are on blood thinners, you will be advised when to stop them. Dental clearance is recommended to eliminate infection sources that could seed the prosthetic valve. Your surgeon will discuss valve choice — mechanical versus biological — based on your age, lifestyle and anticoagulation feasibility, and obtain your informed consent.

## Procedure Details

Your valve surgery is performed under general anaesthesia with cardiopulmonary bypass (heart-lung machine) and typically takes 2–4 hours. The surgeon opens your chest through a median sternotomy — or a minimally invasive approach in selected cases — and your heart is arrested with cold cardioplegia solution. The diseased valve is exposed and assessed for repairability.

For valve repair, techniques include leaflet resection, chordal reconstruction, annuloplasty ring placement and commissurotomy. For valve replacement, your diseased valve is excised and the annulus measured. The prosthetic valve — mechanical or biological — is then sutured into position using pledgeted sutures. Intraoperative transoesophageal echocardiography (TEE) is used to confirm proper valve function before weaning from bypass.

After the procedure, your heart is restarted, bypass is discontinued, and your chest is closed with wires. You will spend 24–48 hours in the cardiac ICU, where cardiac monitoring, anticoagulation initiation (for mechanical valves) and early mobilisation begin. The choice between mechanical and biological valves is finalised pre-operatively based on your age, anticoagulation suitability and future re-operation risk.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of surgery, you will be transferred to the operating theatre and general anaesthesia administered. A central venous line and arterial line will be placed for haemodynamic monitoring. Your chest will be opened via median sternotomy, and the pericardium incised to expose your heart. Heparin will be administered and you will be connected to the cardiopulmonary bypass machine, which takes over circulation and oxygenation.

Your heart will be arrested with cold cardioplegia solution, creating a still, bloodless field. The surgeon will open the relevant chamber to expose your diseased valve. For repair, techniques such as triangular resection, chordal transfer or annuloplasty ring placement will be employed. For replacement, your native valve leaflets will be excised, the annulus debrided and measured, and the prosthetic valve secured with pledgeted sutures.

Intraoperative TEE will confirm the valve is functioning correctly with no significant leakage or gradient. Your heart will be reperfused and allowed to resume normal rhythm. Bypass will be weaned, heparin reversed with protamine, and chest drains and pacing wires placed. The sternum will be closed with stainless steel wires and the skin sutured. The entire procedure typically lasts 2–4 hours.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

After surgery, you will be transferred to the cardiac ICU for 24–48 hours of intensive monitoring. Your heart rhythm, blood pressure and drainage output will be closely watched. Mechanical ventilation is usually weaned within 4–6 hours. Chest tubes are removed on the first or second post-operative day, and anticoagulation therapy is initiated if you have a mechanical valve.

**Will it hurt?** You will have some chest pain, particularly around your incision and when coughing. This is well managed with pain medication and gradually subsides over the first week. Once transferred to the ward, you will begin assisted walking and breathing exercises under physiotherapy guidance. A repeat echocardiogram is performed before discharge to confirm satisfactory valve function. Most patients are discharged between day 5 and day 8.

During your remaining 1–2 weeks in Bangalore, you will attend outpatient reviews for wound checks and anticoagulation dose adjustment. A fitness-to-fly certificate is issued once the surgical team is satisfied with your recovery. Strenuous activity, heavy lifting and driving are restricted until sternal healing is confirmed at the 6-week mark.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

Heart valve surgery in accredited Bangalore hospitals achieves outcomes comparable to leading Western centres, with in-hospital mortality rates below 2% for isolated valve procedures in low-risk patients. Valve repair for mitral regurgitation shows durable results, with freedom from re-operation exceeding 90% at 10 years when performed by experienced surgeons.

Mechanical valves are designed to last a lifetime, though they require lifelong anticoagulation with regular INR monitoring. Biological valves typically last 10–15 years, with newer-generation tissue valves demonstrating improved durability. If you adhere to follow-up schedules, maintain anticoagulation targets and adopt heart-healthy lifestyles, you can expect significant symptom relief and improved exercise tolerance. Long-term survival after successful valve surgery approaches that of the general age-matched population.

## Follow-Up Care After Returning Home

After returning home, a structured follow-up plan is essential. If you have a mechanical valve, you will require lifelong INR monitoring — initially weekly, then monthly once stable. Your local physician or anticoagulation clinic should be briefed with the discharge summary from Bangalore.

An echocardiogram is recommended at 6 months, then annually, to assess valve function and ventricular health. Any fever, new murmur or unexplained breathlessness should prompt urgent evaluation for possible endocarditis. Khan Meditour remains available to coordinate follow-up communication between your local cardiologist and the Bangalore surgical team.

## Alternatives to Consider

If you are high-risk for open-heart surgery, transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) offers a minimally invasive alternative for aortic valve disease. Balloon valvuloplasty may provide temporary relief for selected stenotic valves, particularly mitral stenosis, though results are less durable. The MitraClip procedure is an option for selected patients with severe mitral regurgitation who are unsuitable for surgery.

Medical therapy alone can manage symptoms in early-stage valve disease but does not address the mechanical problem. Your Bangalore cardiologist will discuss all options during consultation, weighing the risks and benefits of each approach against your specific anatomy and overall health status.
