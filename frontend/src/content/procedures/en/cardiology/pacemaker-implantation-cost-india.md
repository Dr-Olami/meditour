## Overview

A pacemaker is a small electronic device implanted under the skin below the collarbone to regulate slow or irregular heartbeats (bradycardia). It consists of a pulse generator (battery and electronic circuitry) and one or more leads (thin wires) that deliver electrical impulses to the heart muscle, ensuring it beats at a normal rate and rhythm. Pacemakers are indicated for patients whose natural pacemaker (the SA node) or electrical conduction system (the AV node) is diseased or damaged.

Pacemaker implantation is one of the most common cardiac procedures performed worldwide and is highly effective in eliminating symptoms such as dizziness, fatigue, and syncope associated with slow heart rates. Bangalore's cardiac centres implant pacemakers from leading global manufacturers including Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Abbott, and Biotronik, with both single-chamber and dual-chamber systems available.

International patients choose Bangalore for pacemaker implantation because of the combination of experienced electrophysiologists, state-of-the-art device programming facilities, and costs that are up to 80% lower than in Western countries. The procedure is minimally invasive, performed under local anaesthesia, and typically requires only a short hospital stay.

## Procedure Details

Pacemaker implantation is performed in a cardiac catheterisation laboratory under local anaesthesia with mild sedation, typically taking 45-90 minutes. The surgeon makes a small incision (about 5cm) below the collarbone and creates a subcutaneous pocket for the pulse generator. A needle is inserted into the subclavian or cephalic vein, and the pacing lead(s) are advanced through a sheath into the heart under fluoroscopic guidance.

For a dual-chamber pacemaker, one lead is positioned in the right atrial appendage and a second lead in the right ventricular apex. The leads are tested for sensing and pacing thresholds, impedance, and stability before being secured to the underlying tissue. The pulse generator is then connected to the leads and placed in the subcutaneous pocket, and the incision is closed with absorbable sutures.

After the procedure, patients are monitored in the cardiac care unit for 12-24 hours. A chest X-ray is performed to confirm lead position and exclude pneumothorax, and the pacemaker is interrogated to verify proper function and programming. Most patients are discharged within 1-3 days with instructions on arm movement restrictions (avoid raising the arm on the implant side above shoulder height for 4-6 weeks), wound care, and a follow-up pacemaker check schedule.
