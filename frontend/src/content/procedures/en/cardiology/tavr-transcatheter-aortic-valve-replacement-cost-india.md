## Overview

If you've been told you need valve replacement but open-heart surgery is too risky because of your age, frailty, or other health conditions, Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (TAVR) may be the solution you have been looking for. Also known as Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation (TAVI), TAVR is a minimally invasive procedure that implants a prosthetic aortic valve via a catheter without the need for open-heart surgery or cardiopulmonary bypass. The new valve, made of bovine or porcine tissue mounted on a metal frame, is compressed into a delivery catheter and positioned inside your diseased native aortic valve, where it is expanded to restore normal blood flow.

Many patients worry that a minimally invasive approach means a compromise in quality. The opposite is true: TAVR has revolutionised the treatment of severe aortic stenosis in patients who are at high or intermediate surgical risk, or who are considered inoperable due to age, frailty, or comorbidities. The procedure is most commonly performed via the femoral artery in your groin (transfemoral approach) under local anaesthesia or moderate sedation, allowing rapid recovery and a short hospital stay. Alternative access routes include the subclavian artery and transapical (through the chest wall) approaches for patients with unsuitable femoral vessels.

Bangalore's leading cardiac centres offer TAVR using the latest-generation valve systems from Edwards Lifesciences and Medtronic, with dedicated structural heart teams comprising interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, and imaging specialists. International patients benefit from costs that are up to 85% lower than in the United States, combined with world-class expertise and comprehensive care coordination that supports you from your first enquiry to your safe return home.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

TAVR is designed for patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis—meaning your aortic valve has become narrowed and stiff, making it difficult for your heart to pump blood effectively. You may have been experiencing chest pain, breathlessness, fatigue, or fainting episodes that have worsened over time. If your cardiologist has told you that open-heart surgery carries too high a risk because of your age, previous surgeries, lung disease, kidney problems, or general frailty, TAVR offers a safer alternative.

It is natural to feel anxious about any heart procedure, especially if you have been living with symptoms for months or years. Many patients describe a dramatic improvement in breathing and energy within days of the procedure. The multidisciplinary heart team in Bangalore will evaluate your STS risk score, frailty, heart anatomy, and vascular access to confirm that TAVR is the right choice for you. If you are a younger, low-risk patient, the team may still recommend surgical valve replacement, and they will explain why in clear terms.

Patients travelling from Bangladesh, the Gulf, and African nations often find that Bangalore offers both the expertise and the affordability they need. You will be cared for by teams who routinely treat international patients and who understand the concerns that come with travelling far from home for a major procedure.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Once you arrive in Bangalore, your heart team will conduct a thorough pre-operative assessment. This includes a detailed echocardiogram to measure your valve area and heart function, a CT angiogram to assess the size of your aortic annulus and the condition of your blood vessels, and blood tests to check your kidney function and clotting. You may also have a coronary angiogram to check for blockages in your heart arteries.

Your cardiologist will sit down with you and your family to explain the planned procedure, the type of valve recommended, and the expected benefits and risks. You will have the chance to ask every question on your mind. If you are taking blood thinners, your team will advise you when to stop them before the procedure. Follow all fasting instructions carefully, and bring a family member or friend to stay with you during your hospital visit.

## Procedure Details

TAVR is performed in a hybrid cardiac catheterisation laboratory by a multidisciplinary heart team. The procedure typically takes 1-2 hours and is performed under local anaesthesia with conscious sedation (transfemoral approach) or general anaesthesia (transapical approach). You will be awake but comfortable during a transfemoral procedure, and many patients are surprised by how straightforward the experience feels compared to what they imagined.

After accessing the femoral artery, a sheath is placed and a pigtail catheter is advanced to the aortic root for angiographic imaging. Your diseased aortic valve may be pre-dilated with a balloon valvuloplasty to create space for the new valve. The transcatheter valve, compressed on its delivery system, is then advanced across the native valve and positioned precisely using fluoroscopy and transoesophageal echocardiography. The valve is deployed—either balloon-expandable (Edwards SAPIEN) or self-expanding (Medtronic Evolut)—and its position and function are immediately assessed with angiography and echocardiography.

After confirming satisfactory valve deployment with minimal paravalvular leak and unobstructed coronary flow, the delivery system is removed and the access site is closed with a percutaneous closure device. You will be monitored in the cardiac care unit for 24-48 hours, with continuous rhythm monitoring for conduction abnormalities. An echocardiogram is performed before discharge to document valve function, and dual antiplatelet therapy is initiated. Most patients are discharged within 3-6 days.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of your TAVR, you will be taken to the hybrid catheterisation laboratory. If you are having the transfemoral approach, you will receive local anaesthesia and mild sedation, meaning you will be awake but relaxed and free of pain. The team will access the femoral artery in your groin and thread the catheter carefully up to your heart under X-ray guidance.

The new valve is positioned inside your narrowed native valve and deployed with precision. The entire team watches on fluoroscopy and echocardiography to confirm that the valve is sitting correctly, opening fully, and that blood is flowing through it without significant leakage. Once the team is satisfied, the catheter is removed and the access site is sealed. The procedure usually takes about an hour, and you will be moved to the cardiac care unit for monitoring shortly afterwards.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

The first 24-48 hours after TAVR are spent in the cardiac care unit, where nurses monitor your heart rhythm closely. Some patients develop a slow heart rate after the procedure, which may require a temporary or permanent pacemaker. Your team will watch for this and act quickly if needed. You will have an echocardiogram before discharge to confirm that your new valve is working well.

Most patients are up and walking within a day of the procedure and are discharged from hospital within 3-6 days. You will stay in Bangalore for another 1-2 weeks for a follow-up echocardiogram, wound review at the access site, and medication counselling. Before you fly home, your team will provide a fitness-to-fly certificate and a detailed discharge summary for your cardiologist at home.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

TAVR has excellent outcomes, with survival rates exceeding 90% at one year for high-risk patients in experienced centres. Most patients experience a significant improvement in symptoms within weeks—breathing becomes easier, energy returns, and the chest pain or fainting that limited daily life often resolves completely. Current-generation valves have demonstrated durability at 5-10 years comparable to surgical bioprosthetic valves, and long-term data continues to accumulate.

You will need lifelong follow-up with a cardiologist, with echocardiograms scheduled at 30 days, 6 months, and annually thereafter. This surveillance ensures that your valve continues to function well and that any changes are detected early.

## Follow-Up Care After Returning Home

After you return home, schedule a follow-up appointment with your cardiologist within 2-4 weeks. Bring all discharge documents, the echocardiogram images from Bangalore, and your medication list. Continue your prescribed blood thinners exactly as directed, and do not stop them without consulting your doctor. If you experience chest pain, rapid heartbeat, leg swelling, or breathlessness, contact your cardiologist immediately.

## Alternatives to Consider

If you are not a candidate for TAVR, or if your heart team recommends a different approach, surgical aortic valve replacement remains the gold standard for younger, lower-risk patients. Balloon aortic valvuloplasty can provide temporary relief of symptoms in some patients, though the results are not durable. Your heart team in Bangalore will discuss all options with you and help you choose the treatment that offers the best balance of safety, durability, and quality of life for your individual situation.
