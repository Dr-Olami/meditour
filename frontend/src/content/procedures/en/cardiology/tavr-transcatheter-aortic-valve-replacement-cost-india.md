## Overview

Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (TAVR), also known as Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation (TAVI), is a minimally invasive procedure that implants a prosthetic aortic valve via a catheter without the need for open-heart surgery or cardiopulmonary bypass. The new valve, made of bovine or porcine tissue mounted on a metal frame, is compressed into a delivery catheter and positioned inside the diseased native aortic valve, where it is expanded to restore normal blood flow.

TAVR has revolutionised the treatment of severe aortic stenosis in patients who are at high or intermediate surgical risk, or who are considered inoperable due to age, frailty, or comorbidities. The procedure is most commonly performed via the femoral artery (transfemoral approach) under local anaesthesia or moderate sedation, allowing rapid recovery and a short hospital stay. Alternative access routes include the subclavian artery and transapical (through the chest wall) approaches for patients with unsuitable femoral vessels.

Bangalore's leading cardiac centres offer TAVR using the latest-generation valve systems from Edwards Lifesciences and Medtronic, with dedicated structural heart teams comprising interventional cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, and imaging specialists. International patients benefit from costs that are up to 85% lower than in the United States, combined with world-class expertise and comprehensive care coordination.

## Procedure Details

TAVR is performed in a hybrid cardiac catheterisation laboratory by a multidisciplinary heart team. The procedure typically takes 1-2 hours and is performed under local anaesthesia with conscious sedation (transfemoral approach) or general anaesthesia (transapical approach). After accessing the femoral artery, a sheath is placed and a pigtail catheter is advanced to the aortic root for angiographic imaging.

The diseased aortic valve may be pre-dilated with a balloon valvuloplasty to create space for the new valve. The transcatheter valve, compressed on its delivery system, is then advanced across the native valve and positioned precisely using fluoroscopy and transoesophageal echocardiography. The valve is deployed—either balloon-expandable (Edwards SAPIEN) or self-expanding (Medtronic Evolut)—and its position and function are immediately assessed with angiography and echocardiography.

After confirming satisfactory valve deployment with minimal paravalvular leak and unobstructed coronary flow, the delivery system is removed and the access site is closed with a percutaneous closure device. Patients are monitored in the cardiac care unit for 24-48 hours, with continuous rhythm monitoring for conduction abnormalities. An echocardiogram is performed before discharge to document valve function, and dual antiplatelet therapy is initiated. Most patients are discharged within 3-6 days.
