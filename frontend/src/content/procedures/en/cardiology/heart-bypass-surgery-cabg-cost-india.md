## Overview

Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG), commonly known as heart bypass surgery, is a procedure that improves blood flow to the heart by creating new pathways around narrowed or blocked coronary arteries. Surgeons use healthy blood vessels—typically the internal mammary artery, saphenous vein, or radial artery—to bypass the diseased segments, restoring oxygen-rich blood supply to the heart muscle.

CABG is one of the most frequently performed major surgical procedures worldwide and remains the gold standard for patients with multi-vessel coronary artery disease, left main disease, or diabetes with complex lesions. Bangalore's leading cardiac centres perform CABG using both conventional on-pump and off-pump (beating heart) techniques, with outcomes comparable to the best international registries.

## Procedure Details

CABG is performed under general anaesthesia and typically takes 3-5 hours depending on the number of grafts. In conventional on-pump surgery, the patient is connected to a cardiopulmonary bypass (heart-lung) machine, which takes over circulation while the heart is arrested with a cold cardioplegia solution. The surgeon then harvestes the graft conduits and constructs the bypasses to the target coronary arteries beyond the blockages.

In off-pump (beating heart) CABG, the heart continues to beat throughout the procedure. Specialised stabilisation devices hold the target artery still while the surgeon performs the anastomosis. This technique avoids the systemic inflammatory response associated with cardiopulmonary bypass and may be preferred in elderly patients or those with compromised kidney or lung function.

After grafting, the heart is restarted (if on-pump), the chest tubes and temporary pacing wires are placed, and the sternum is closed with stainless steel wires. Patients spend 24-48 hours in the cardiac ICU before transferring to a step-down unit. Early mobilisation, breathing exercises, and wound care begin on the first post-operative day, with discharge typically at 5-7 days.
