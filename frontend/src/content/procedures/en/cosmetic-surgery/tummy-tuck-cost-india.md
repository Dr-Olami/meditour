## Overview

If you've been struggling with loose skin and weakened abdominal muscles — perhaps after significant weight loss, pregnancy, or simply as part of ageing — you may be considering a tummy tuck to help you feel more confident in your body. It's completely understandable to want a flatter, firmer abdomen, especially when no amount of diet and exercise seems to address that stubborn excess skin.

A tummy tuck, also called abdominoplasty, is a surgical procedure that removes extra skin and fat from your abdomen while tightening the muscles underneath. It's not a weight-loss procedure — it's designed for people who are already near their stable weight but want to contour an area that has been resistant to other approaches.

In India, you'll find experienced, board-certified plastic surgeons working in internationally accredited hospitals in Bangalore. ## Who Is This Procedure For?

A tummy tuck might be right for you if you've been at a stable weight for at least six months and have excess abdominal skin, fat, or weakened muscles that haven't responded to diet and exercise. It's particularly effective if you're a woman who has completed her family, as pregnancy can stretch the abdominal muscles and skin in ways that can't be reversed naturally.

To be a good candidate, you should be in good overall health, with no uncontrolled chronic conditions such as diabetes, heart disease, or bleeding disorders. If you smoke, you'll need to commit to stopping at least six weeks before and after surgery — nicotine significantly impairs wound healing and increases the risk of wound separation and infection. If your BMI is above 30, your surgeon may advise you to lose some weight first to optimise your results and reduce complications.

If you're planning future pregnancies, it's usually best to postpone the procedure, as pregnancy can reverse the results by re-stretching the tightened muscles and skin. It's also important to have realistic expectations about scarring — a full tummy tuck leaves a horizontal scar across the lower abdomen, which fades over time but doesn't disappear entirely. ## Pre-Operative Preparation

Good preparation is key to a safe surgery and smooth recovery. Two to three weeks before your procedure, you'll need to complete all recommended blood tests, including a complete blood count, coagulation profile, metabolic panel, and an ECG if indicated. A pre-anaesthetic evaluation will confirm your fitness for general anaesthesia.

You should stop taking blood-thinning medications such as aspirin, ibuprofen, and certain herbal and dietary supplements at least two weeks before surgery. If you smoke, you must stop completely at least six weeks before the procedure. Try to maintain a stable weight in the weeks leading up to surgery.

Arrange for a companion to travel with you — you'll need assistance during your hospital stay and for at least the first week after discharge. Pack loose, comfortable clothing that opens at the front, slip-on shoes, and a pillow for your journey home. On the day before surgery, you'll be asked to fast for at least eight hours and shower with an antibacterial soap.

## Procedure Details

It's natural to want to understand exactly what will happen to your body. A full tummy tuck is performed under general anaesthesia, so you'll be completely asleep and won't feel anything during the surgery. It typically takes 2-4 hours. Your surgeon will make a horizontal incision across your lower abdomen, usually from hip to hip, positioned just above the pubic area so that any resulting scar stays hidden beneath your underwear or swimwear. The skin and fat are then gently lifted from your abdominal wall, and the underlying muscles are tightened with sutures to give you a firmer, flatter contour. The excess skin is trimmed away, the remaining skin is repositioned smoothly, and your belly button is brought through a new opening so it looks natural.

If your concern is limited to the lower abdomen, your surgeon may recommend a mini tummy tuck instead. This involves a shorter incision, doesn't require repositioning your belly button, and comes with a shorter recovery time.

After surgery, small drains are usually placed to remove excess fluid — these are removed after 5-10 days. You'll wear a compression garment for 4-6 weeks to support healing and reduce swelling. Expect to stay in hospital for 2-4 days. For the first week, you'll walk in a slightly bent-forward position to reduce tension on your incision. Most people return to light activities within 2-3 weeks, with full recovery and clearance for strenuous exercise at 6-8 weeks.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of your surgery, you'll be admitted to the hospital and prepared in the surgical suite. The anaesthetist will administer general anaesthesia, so you'll be fully asleep and won't feel any pain. Your surgeon will mark the incision sites and confirm the surgical plan with you beforehand.

For a full tummy tuck, a horizontal incision is made across your lower abdomen, typically from hip to hip, positioned to keep the resulting scar below your bikini line. Your surgeon lifts the skin and fat from your abdominal wall up to the rib cage, exposing the underlying muscles. These muscles are tightened with strong sutures to correct diastasis recti — the separation of abdominal muscles that commonly occurs after pregnancy or significant weight gain. The excess skin is then pulled downward and trimmed, and the remaining skin is repositioned smoothly over your abdomen. A new opening is created for your belly button.

If you're having liposuction combined with your tummy tuck, it's typically performed before the skin tightening to remove excess fat from your flanks, hips, or upper abdomen. Once contouring is complete, surgical drains are placed to prevent fluid accumulation, the incisions are closed in layers, and a compression garment is applied. The entire procedure takes 2-4 hours, after which you're transferred to the recovery room.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

**Will it hurt?** You'll feel discomfort, especially in the first few days. Pain and tightness are most pronounced right after surgery, but they're managed with prescribed pain medication. Most people feel significantly better by the end of the second week.

After the procedure, you'll remain in hospital for 2-4 days for pain management, drain care, and monitoring. You'll be encouraged to walk short distances within a day of surgery to promote circulation and reduce the risk of blood clots, but you'll need to walk in a slightly bent-forward position for the first week to avoid tension on your incision.

Your surgical drains are typically removed after 5-10 days, once fluid output decreases. The compression garment must be worn continuously for 4-6 weeks to support the healing tissues, minimise swelling, and help your skin conform to its new contour. A follow-up appointment is scheduled within 10-14 days to assess wound healing and clear you for international travel.

Strenuous activity, heavy lifting, and vigorous exercise must be avoided for at least six weeks. Scar management can begin once your incision has fully closed, typically using silicone sheets or gels recommended by your surgeon. Watch for signs of infection such as increasing redness, warmth, or discharge at the incision site.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

A tummy tuck delivers long-lasting results, especially if you maintain a stable weight and don't become pregnant after surgery. The removal of excess skin and tightening of your abdominal muscles creates a flatter, firmer abdomen that can significantly improve your body confidence and how your clothes fit. Satisfaction rates for abdominoplasty are consistently above 90%, making it one of the most rewarding cosmetic procedures.

**When will I see the final result?** Your final results become visible after 3-6 months, once all residual swelling resolves and your tissues settle into their new position. Your abdominal scar will continue to fade over 12-18 months, transitioning from red and raised to pale and flat. While the scar is permanent, it's positioned to be concealed by most underwear and swimwear. Maintaining a healthy weight and a regular exercise routine will help preserve your results for many years.

Complications are uncommon when the procedure is performed by an experienced surgeon in an accredited facility. The most frequently reported issues are minor wound healing delays and seroma formation, both of which are typically managed without additional surgery.

## Alternatives to Consider

If a full tummy tuck doesn't feel like the right fit for you, there are several alternatives. A mini tummy tuck is suitable if your excess skin is limited to the lower abdomen — it doesn't involve muscle tightening or belly button repositioning and comes with a shorter recovery. Liposuction alone may suffice if you have good skin elasticity and localised fat deposits without significant skin laxity.

If you've experienced massive weight loss and have excess skin around your entire lower body, a lower body lift or belt lipectomy may deliver more comprehensive results. Non-surgical skin tightening treatments using radiofrequency or ultrasound can provide mild improvement if you have minimal laxity. During your consultation, your surgeon will discuss all viable options and recommend the approach that best matches your anatomy and goals.
