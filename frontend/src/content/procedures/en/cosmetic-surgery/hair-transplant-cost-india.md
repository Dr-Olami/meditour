## Overview

If you are reading this, chances are you have spent months — perhaps years — watching your hairline recede, your crown thin, or your confidence quietly erode with every glimpse in the mirror. Hair loss is deeply personal, and the decision to do something about it is rarely taken lightly. A hair transplant works by relocating healthy, genetically resistant hair follicles from the back and sides of your scalp — the donor area — to the bald or thinning areas that concern you most. Because these follicles are immune to the hormone that causes pattern baldness, the transplanted hair continues to grow naturally for the rest of your life. It is not a wig, a weave, or a temporary fix; it is your own hair, growing in a new place.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

You are likely a good candidate if you are over 25 with a stable hair loss pattern — typically Norwood stage 3 or above. The age guideline exists for a practical reason: if your hair loss is still progressing rapidly, transplanting now could leave you with an unnatural-looking island of hair as the surrounding native hair continues to thin. If you are younger with aggressive loss, your surgeon may advise medical management first to stabilise things.

You need sufficient donor hair density on the back and sides of your scalp. If your thinning is diffuse — including the donor area — the transplanted follicles may be just as vulnerable to future loss, and a transplant may not serve you well.

This procedure is sought by men with male pattern baldness, women with female pattern hair loss, and people who have lost hair through trauma, burns, or surgery. It can also restore eyebrows, beards, and moustaches. If you have scarring alopecia or an inflammatory scalp condition, it must be controlled before surgery.

Be honest with yourself about expectations. A transplant can significantly improve your density and restore a natural hairline, but it cannot recreate the thick, full head of hair you had as a teenager. If you smoke, you should stop for at least two weeks before and after the procedure — smoking restricts blood flow to the scalp and can lower graft survival.

## Procedure Details

You will choose between two techniques — Follicular Unit Extraction (FUE) and Follicular Unit Transplantation (FUT) — and understanding the difference matters because it directly affects your scarring, your recovery, and how short you can wear your hair afterwards.

With FUE, your surgeon uses a micro-punch tool (0.6–0.8 mm) to extract follicular units one at a time from the donor area. Each tiny puncture heals into a dot scar so small that it is virtually invisible, even with a short haircut. FUE recovers faster, which is why most people who want the freedom to wear their hair short prefer it. The trade-off is that it takes longer and, for very large sessions, may not yield as many grafts in a single sitting.

With FUT, your surgeon removes a thin strip of scalp (about 1–1.5 cm wide) from the back of your head and closes the wound with sutures. The strip is dissected under a microscope into individual grafts. FUT leaves a linear scar across the back of your scalp. If you wear your hair longer than a grade 3 or 4 clipper cut, the scar is usually hidden by surrounding hair — but if you favour a very short style, it may be visible. FUT can yield more grafts in one session, which matters if you need extensive coverage or have a tight scalp that makes FUE extraction difficult.

Both techniques are done under local anaesthesia, so you are awake but comfortable. The session lasts 4–8 hours depending on how many grafts you need — typically 1,500–3,500, though larger sessions of up to 5,000 are possible. Your surgeon designs the hairline to suit your face and age, and implants each graft at the precise angle and direction of natural growth. Graft survival rates in Bangalore clinics typically reach 90–95%.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day, your surgeon reviews the plan with you, confirms the hairline design, marks the donor and recipient areas, and photographs your scalp. Local anaesthesia is injected into the donor area — you will feel a brief sting, then numbness.

If you are having FUE, follicular units are extracted one by one and placed in a specialised holding solution to keep them viable. If you are having FUT, a strip of scalp is removed, the wound is sutured, and the strip is dissected under a microscope into individual grafts. In both cases, grafts are sorted by how many hairs each contains — singles for the hairline, doubles and triples for density behind it.

Your recipient area is then numbed, and your surgeon makes tiny incisions at the exact angles and directions of natural growth. Single-hair grafts go at the front for a soft, feathered hairline; multi-hair grafts go further back for fullness. The procedure takes 4–8 hours, and you remain awake throughout — many people listen to music or watch a film on a tablet.

Once all grafts are in place, your scalp is lightly bandaged and you rest for 1–2 hours before same-day discharge. A follow-up is scheduled within 48 hours.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

Expect mild swelling, redness, and scabbing in both the donor and recipient areas. Swelling usually peaks at 2–3 days and may spread to your forehead before resolving within a week. Scabbing in the recipient area clears within 10–14 days. For the first few nights, sleep with your head elevated to reduce swelling and avoid pressure on the grafts.

You will be prescribed antibiotics and possibly a mild pain reliever for 1–2 days. The donor area may feel tender — more so with FUT, where sutures are removed at 10–14 days. Gentle hair washing can begin 2–3 days after the procedure, using the technique the clinic staff will demonstrate so you do not disturb the grafts.

Your surgeon will see you within the first week to check graft healing. Most people are cleared to fly home within 5–10 days. You can resume normal daily activities within a few days, but avoid strenuous exercise, swimming, and direct sun exposure for 2–4 weeks.

Here is the part that catches many people off guard: your transplanted hair will shed within 2–4 weeks. This is called shock loss, and it is completely normal — it does not mean the grafts have failed. The follicles rest beneath the skin and start producing new hair at 3–4 months, gradually thickening over the following months. Your final result appears at 12–18 months.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

When performed by an experienced surgeon, graft survival typically reaches 90–95%. The transplanted hair is genetically resistant to pattern baldness and grows for the rest of your life. Most people are highly satisfied, especially when the hairline is designed to age naturally with their face.

Your result keeps refining for 12–18 months as all follicles mature and reach full thickness, with subtle density improvements possible up to 24 months. But here is a reality you should understand before you commit: a transplant does not stop the hair loss around it. Your native, non-transplanted hair may continue to thin over time, which can leave the transplanted area looking isolated. This is why many surgeons recommend continuing finasteride or minoxidil after your procedure — to protect the hair you still have.

About 15–25% of people eventually need a second session, usually for additional density or coverage as surrounding hair thins. Bangalore's leading clinics have extensive experience with both FUE and FUT, and their results are comparable to top international centres.

## Alternatives to Consider

Before committing to surgery, consider whether non-surgical options might serve you. Finasteride (an oral medication that blocks the hormone behind pattern baldness) and minoxidil (a topical solution that stimulates growth) can slow hair loss and partially restore density in early-stage cases. These are often recommended as first-line treatment and may be continued after a transplant to protect your existing hair.

Low-level laser therapy (LLLT) and platelet-rich plasma (PRP) injections can improve thickness and slow progression. LLLT uses red light to stimulate follicle activity; PRP involves injecting concentrated platelets from your own blood into your scalp. Both work best for mild to moderate thinning.

If you are not a good candidate for a transplant — due to insufficient donor hair, diffuse thinning, or active scalp conditions — scalp micropigmentation (SMP) or high-quality hair systems are worth exploring. SMP creates the look of a closely shaved head using specialised scalp tattooing, while modern hair systems offer a natural appearance with regular maintenance.
