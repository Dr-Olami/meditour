## Overview

If you or a loved one has been struggling with asthma—waking up at night gasping for air, avoiding exercise, carrying a reliever inhaler everywhere, and living with the constant fear of the next attack—you know how deeply this chronic disease affects daily life. Asthma is one of the most common respiratory conditions worldwide, yet it remains under-diagnosed and under-treated in many regions. The good news is that with modern, evidence-based treatment, the vast majority of asthma patients can achieve full symptom control and lead completely normal, active lives.

Asthma is a chronic inflammatory disease of the airways characterised by recurrent episodes of wheezing, breathlessness, chest tightness, and coughing. These symptoms occur because the airways become inflamed, narrowed, and hyper-responsive to various triggers such as allergens, infections, exercise, cold air, or irritants. While asthma cannot be cured, it can be very effectively managed with a combination of controller medications, reliever medications, trigger avoidance, and patient education. The international standard of care, as outlined in the GINA (Global Initiative for Asthma) guidelines, focuses on achieving and maintaining control rather than merely reacting to symptoms.

Bangalore's pulmonology centres offer a comprehensive, structured asthma management programme that goes far beyond simply prescribing an inhaler. The approach begins with a thorough diagnostic workup to confirm the asthma phenotype—whether it is allergic, eosinophilic, exercise-induced, occupational, or asthma-COPD overlap—and to identify your specific triggers through detailed allergy testing. From there, a personalised treatment plan is developed, incorporating the latest inhaled corticosteroids, long-acting bronchodilators, leukotriene modifiers, and where appropriate, cutting-edge biologic therapies for severe asthma. Crucially, every patient receives hands-on inhaler technique training and a written asthma action plan so they know exactly what to do when symptoms worsen.

For international patients, Bangalore offers a compelling combination: highly experienced pulmonologists trained at top institutions, advanced pulmonary function laboratories, access to the latest biologic therapies, and costs that are up to 80% lower than in Western countries. Dedicated international patient teams coordinate every step of your journey, from visa assistance to accommodation and follow-up.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

This comprehensive asthma treatment programme is designed for patients who have asthma that is not adequately controlled, or who have never had a proper specialist evaluation. You may benefit from this programme if you experience frequent asthma symptoms—more than twice a week—night-time awakenings due to breathlessness, limitation of activities, or recurrent emergency visits for asthma attacks. If your current inhaler regimen is not providing relief, or if you find yourself using your reliever inhaler more and more, a specialist review is essential.

Patients with severe asthma who require high-dose inhaled corticosteroids or frequent courses of oral steroids are excellent candidates, as the programme includes assessment for biologic therapies that can dramatically reduce steroid dependence and exacerbation frequency. If you have occupational asthma triggered by workplace exposures, or allergic asthma where specific triggers have never been properly identified, the comprehensive allergy testing and trigger identification panel will provide answers.

The programme is also suitable for patients with asthma-COPD overlap syndrome, a condition that shares features of both diseases and requires a carefully tailored management approach. Children over the age of five with poorly controlled asthma can also be evaluated, though the treatment approach is adapted for paediatric patients. If you have been told your asthma is "difficult to treat," a structured specialist review in Bangalore can often uncover contributing factors such as incorrect inhaler technique, unrecognised triggers, co-existing conditions like allergic rhinitis or gastro-oesophageal reflux, or an alternative diagnosis altogether.

## Pre-operative Preparation

Although asthma treatment is not a surgical procedure, thorough preparation is essential to ensure an accurate diagnosis and an effective treatment plan. Before you travel to Bangalore, share all your relevant medical records with your treating pulmonologist for advance review. This includes any previous spirometry or pulmonary function test results, allergy test reports, chest X-rays or CT scans, blood work, and a complete list of every medication and inhaler you currently use, including dosages and frequency. Keep a symptom diary for at least two weeks before your visit, noting when symptoms occur, what seems to trigger them, and how often you use your reliever inhaler.

Be prepared to provide a detailed history of your condition: when symptoms first started, how they have progressed, what treatments you have tried, and what has or has not worked. Disclose any other medical conditions you have, particularly allergic rhinitis, eczema, sinusitis, gastro-oesophageal reflux disease, or a family history of asthma or allergies. If you are taking oral corticosteroids regularly, do not stop them abruptly before your visit. Bring all your current inhalers with you so the team can check your technique and ensure the devices are appropriate for you.

Once you arrive in Bangalore, you will undergo a comprehensive diagnostic assessment. This typically includes detailed spirometry with bronchodilator reversibility testing to measure your lung function and confirm airflow limitation, fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) testing to assess airway inflammation, and a comprehensive allergy panel to identify specific triggers. Depending on your case, additional tests such as a chest X-ray, complete blood count with eosinophil count, total IgE level, or a methacholine challenge test may be ordered. Your pulmonologist will review all results and discuss your personalised treatment plan with you in detail.

## Procedure Details

Your asthma treatment programme in Bangalore is structured as a multi-day outpatient protocol rather than a single visit. The first phase focuses on comprehensive diagnostics. Over two to three days, you will undergo pulmonary function testing in a state-of-the-art laboratory. Spirometry measures how much air you can exhale and how quickly, both before and after a bronchodilator medication, to confirm reversible airflow obstruction—the hallmark of asthma. FeNO testing measures the level of nitric oxide in your exhaled breath, which is a marker of eosinophilic airway inflammation and helps guide anti-inflammatory therapy.

Allergy testing is a key component, particularly for patients with suspected allergic asthma. A skin prick test or specific IgE blood test panel identifies sensitisation to common allergens such as house dust mites, pollen, animal dander, mould, and cockroach. For patients with occupational asthma, specific inhalation challenges may be considered. If your asthma is severe or not responding to standard therapy, additional blood tests including eosinophil count and total IgE will determine your eligibility for biologic therapies.

The second phase is treatment initiation and education. Based on your diagnostic results, your pulmonologist will prescribe a personalised medication regimen following GINA guidelines. This typically includes a daily controller inhaler—usually an inhaled corticosteroid combined with a long-acting beta-agonist (ICS-LABA)—to suppress airway inflammation, and a fast-acting reliever for symptom relief. For patients with severe eosinophilic or allergic asthma, biologic therapies such as omalizumab, mepolizumab, or dupilumab may be initiated, administered as subcutaneous injections at regular intervals.

A critical and often overlooked component is inhaler technique training. Studies show that the majority of asthma patients use their inhalers incorrectly, dramatically reducing medication effectiveness. The respiratory therapy team will work with you one-on-one, using training devices and spacer chambers, to ensure you can use your inhaler correctly and consistently. You will also receive a written, personalised asthma action plan that tells you exactly what medications to take, when to increase them, and when to seek emergency care.

## What Happens During the Procedure

During your treatment programme, each day follows a structured schedule. On day one, you will meet your pulmonologist for a comprehensive consultation. Expect a detailed discussion lasting 45 to 60 minutes, covering your symptom history, triggers, previous treatments, and quality-of-life impact. Your doctor will examine you, listen to your chest, and review any records you have brought. You will then be scheduled for pulmonary function testing, which takes about 45 minutes and requires you to blow forcefully into a spirometer several times.

On day two, allergy testing is typically performed. For a skin prick test, small drops of allergen extracts are placed on your forearm and the skin is gently pricked; results are read after 15 to 20 minutes. This is generally painless, though you may experience mild itching at positive test sites. Blood tests for specific IgE and eosinophil count are drawn at the same visit. If you are being assessed for biologic therapy, additional blood work may be ordered.

On day three, your pulmonologist reviews all results with you and prescribes your personalised treatment plan. This is when inhaler technique training takes place—a hands-on session with a respiratory therapist who will demonstrate proper technique, watch you practise, and correct any errors. You will receive your asthma action plan in writing, with clear instructions on daily management and what to do during an exacerbation. If biologic therapy is indicated, your first dose may be administered under observation.

**Will I feel any discomfort?** Pulmonary function testing requires vigorous exhalation and may cause mild breathlessness or lightheadedness that passes quickly. Allergy skin testing may cause temporary itching at test sites. Biologic injections involve a brief, minor sting. None of these procedures require sedation or anaesthesia.

## Post-operative Care & Recovery

Although asthma treatment does not involve surgery, the "recovery" phase is about achieving and maintaining symptom control. After your treatment plan is initiated, you will remain in Bangalore for several days of monitoring. Your pulmonologist will assess your response to the new medication regimen, checking whether your symptoms have improved and your reliever inhaler use has decreased. Repeat spirometry may be performed to document improvements in lung function.

Pulmonary rehabilitation sessions may be recommended, particularly if you have been avoiding exercise due to breathlessness. These supervised sessions include breathing exercises, aerobic conditioning, and techniques such as pursed-lip breathing and diaphragmatic breathing that help you manage breathlessness. You will also receive education on trigger avoidance—how to reduce exposure to dust mites, manage indoor air quality, and handle seasonal allergens.

Before you leave Bangalore, your pulmonologist will conduct a final review to confirm that your asthma is well-controlled, your inhaler technique is correct, and you fully understand your asthma action plan. A fitness-to-fly certificate will be issued. You will be given a supply of medications to take home, along with prescriptions that can be filled in your home country. Most patients notice significant improvement in their symptoms within two to four weeks of starting optimised treatment.

## Long-term Outcomes & Success Rates

With modern, evidence-based treatment, the outlook for asthma patients is excellent. International studies show that over 80% of asthma patients can achieve good symptom control with appropriate therapy, and Bangalore's pulmonology centres achieve outcomes consistent with international benchmarks. For patients with severe asthma who qualify for biologic therapy, the results can be transformative: clinical trials show reductions in exacerbations of 50% to 70% and significant reductions in oral steroid dependence.

However, long-term success depends heavily on adherence to treatment. The most common reason for treatment failure is inconsistent use of controller medication—patients often stop their daily inhaler once they feel better, leading to a recurrence of inflammation and symptoms. Understanding that asthma is a chronic condition requiring ongoing maintenance therapy, even when you feel well, is the single most important factor in long-term control. Regular follow-up with a pulmonologist, annual review of your asthma action plan, and ongoing attention to trigger avoidance are all essential.

## Follow-up Care After Returning Home

Before you leave Bangalore, you will receive a comprehensive discharge summary detailing your diagnostic results, asthma phenotype, prescribed medications with dosages, inhaler technique assessment, and your personalised asthma action plan. Share this document with your respiratory physician or general practitioner at home, who will take over your ongoing care. A follow-up appointment is typically scheduled at 4 to 6 weeks to assess your response to treatment, with regular reviews at least every 6 to 12 months thereafter.

If you are on biologic therapy, arrangements will be made for continued administration in your home country, or you may return to Bangalore for periodic doses. It is critical that you continue your controller medication exactly as prescribed and never stop it without consulting your doctor. Seek immediate medical attention if you experience a severe asthma attack that does not respond to your reliever inhaler, or if you are using your reliever more frequently than usual. ## Alternatives to Consider

If your symptoms are mild and infrequent, a simpler treatment approach with a low-dose inhaled corticosteroid or a leukotriene modifier alone may be sufficient, and a full specialist programme may not be necessary. If your primary issue is allergic triggers, allergen immunotherapy (desensitisation) may be considered as an adjunct to standard treatment, particularly for patients with allergic rhinitis co-existing with asthma.

If your diagnosis is uncertain—particularly if symptoms are more consistent with COPD, vocal cord dysfunction, or gastro-oesophageal reflux-related cough—your pulmonologist will investigate these possibilities, as the treatments differ significantly. For patients with asthma-COPD overlap, a combined treatment approach addressing both conditions will be developed. In all cases, the goal is to identify the most effective, least burdensome treatment that achieves full symptom control and prevents exacerbations.
