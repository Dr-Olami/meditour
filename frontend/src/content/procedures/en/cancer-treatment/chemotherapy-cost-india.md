## Overview

Chemotherapy is a systemic cancer treatment that uses powerful drugs to destroy rapidly dividing cancer cells throughout the body. It is one of the most widely used cancer treatments and can be administered alone or in combination with surgery, radiation therapy, immunotherapy, or targeted therapy. Chemotherapy may be given with curative intent, as adjuvant therapy after surgery, or as palliative treatment to control symptoms and improve quality of life.

In India, chemotherapy is delivered in world-class oncology centres that follow internationally recognised protocols such as NCCN and ESMO guidelines. The drugs used are sourced from reputable global manufacturers and are identical to those administered in Western hospitals. Treatment is supervised by medical oncologists with extensive experience in managing both common and rare cancers.

The cost of chemotherapy in India is among the lowest in the world, starting at approximately $500 per cycle. This affordability, combined with high-quality care, makes India a leading destination for international patients seeking cancer treatment. Patients benefit from personalised treatment plans, modern infusion suites, and comprehensive supportive care to manage side effects.

## Procedure Details

Chemotherapy drugs are administered through various routes, most commonly intravenously (IV) through a central line or port, or orally in tablet form. Each chemotherapy session is called a cycle, and cycles are typically spaced 2–3 weeks apart to allow the body—particularly the bone marrow—to recover between treatments. The specific drugs, dosages, and number of cycles are determined by the type and stage of cancer, the patient's overall health, and the treatment goal.

Before each cycle, patients undergo blood tests to check blood counts, liver function, and kidney function. If counts are adequate, the chemotherapy drugs are administered in a dedicated infusion suite under the supervision of trained oncology nurses and medical oncologists. Depending on the regimen, a single session may last from 30 minutes to several hours. Some patients receive day-care treatment and return to their accommodation the same day, while others may require a short hospital stay for observation.

Supportive care is an integral part of chemotherapy in India. Patients receive anti-nausea medications, growth factors to boost white blood cell counts if needed, and nutritional support. Regular monitoring through blood tests and imaging helps the oncology team assess treatment response and adjust the regimen as necessary. The medical team also educates patients and their families about managing side effects at home.
