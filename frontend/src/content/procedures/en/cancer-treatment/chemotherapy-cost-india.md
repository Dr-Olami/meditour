## Overview

Being told you need chemotherapy can feel like your whole world has shifted. You probably have questions racing through your mind — what will the treatment be like, how long will it take, how will you feel during it, and what about your hair, your energy, your family. These are completely normal concerns, and you deserve honest answers.

Chemotherapy is a treatment that uses powerful medicines to destroy cancer cells throughout your body. You might have it on its own, or alongside other treatments like surgery, radiation, or immunotherapy. Sometimes it's given to cure the cancer, sometimes to shrink a tumour before surgery, and sometimes to control symptoms and help you feel better when the cancer is advanced. Whatever your situation, your oncologist will explain exactly why it's being recommended for you.

In India, you'll receive chemotherapy in hospitals that follow the same international treatment guidelines used in the UK, USA, and Europe. The drugs are the same ones used worldwide — sourced from reputable global manufacturers. The difference isn't in quality; it's in cost. Chemotherapy in India starts at around $500 per cycle, compared to $10,000 or more in the USA. That's because India has lower operational costs and a strong generic pharmaceutical industry — not because the care is any less.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Chemotherapy may be recommended for you if you've been diagnosed with cancer through a biopsy and pathology testing. It's used for many types of cancer — breast, lung, bowel, ovarian, lymphoma, and leukaemia, among others. The decision about whether it's right for you depends on your specific cancer type, its stage, its molecular characteristics, and the overall treatment plan.

Before each cycle, your blood counts need to be adequate because chemotherapy affects your bone marrow. You'll also need to be well enough overall — doctors use a scale called ECOG performance status, and you'll generally need to be at 0–2 on that scale. If you have severe problems with your liver, kidneys, or heart, certain chemotherapy drugs may not be suitable, but alternatives can often be found.

Chemotherapy can be particularly helpful if your cancer has spread, because it treats the whole body. It's also used after surgery to reduce the chance of the cancer coming back, or before surgery to shrink a tumour. If your cancer is advanced, chemotherapy can help control your symptoms and improve your quality of life, even if a cure isn't possible.

If the cost of chemotherapy in your home country feels overwhelming, many international patients come to India because they simply can't afford treatment at home — and here, you'll receive the same drugs and protocols at a fraction of the cost.

Before you travel to Bangalore, we'll help you send all your medical records to your oncologist — pathology reports, biopsy results, scans, blood tests, and any previous treatment records. Having everything organised in advance means your doctor can review your case and prepare a preliminary treatment plan with a cost estimate before you leave home.

If you have conditions like diabetes, high blood pressure, or heart disease, make sure they're well controlled. Talk to your doctor about any medications you're taking, especially blood thinners.

Please bring a companion with you if at all possible. In the weeks before your treatment starts, try to eat well and build up your strength — good nutrition helps your body cope better with chemotherapy. When packing, bring comfortable clothes, a hat or scarf in case you experience hair loss, and anything that brings you comfort.

## Procedure Details

Chemotherapy is usually given through a vein in your arm (intravenously) or sometimes as tablets. Each treatment session is called a cycle, and you'll typically have 2–3 weeks between cycles to give your body time to recover. The specific drugs, doses, and number of cycles depend on your type of cancer, its stage, and your overall treatment plan. Common combinations include AC for breast cancer, FOLFOX for bowel cancer, and carboplatin with paclitaxel for ovarian and lung cancers.

Before each cycle, you'll have blood tests to check your blood counts, liver function, and kidney function. If your counts are good, you'll receive your treatment in a comfortable infusion suite. A session can take anywhere from 30 minutes to several hours. Many people are treated as day-care patients and go home the same day.

To make repeated treatments easier on your veins, your doctor may recommend placing a small device called a port (Port-a-Cath) or a PICC line under your skin. This stays in place throughout your treatment so you don't need a new needle each time. Your care team will give you anti-sickness medicines before and after each infusion, and if your white blood cell counts drop, you may receive growth factor injections.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of your treatment, you'll go to the oncology day-care unit or infusion suite. A nurse will check your vital signs and review your morning blood test results. If your counts are too low, your session may be postponed by a few days, or you may be given growth factor injections — this is normal and nothing to be alarmed about.

Once you're cleared, you'll settle into a comfortable reclining chair. If you have a port or PICC line, the nurse will access it using sterile technique. If not, a small needle (cannula) will be placed in your arm. First, you'll receive pre-medications — anti-sickness drugs and sometimes antihistamines or steroids. Then your chemotherapy drugs will be infused slowly, with the nurse checking on you throughout.

The infusion itself can take anywhere from 30 minutes to several hours. You can read, listen to music, watch something on your phone, or simply rest. Your companion can usually stay with you. Once the drugs are fully administered, your IV line will be flushed and removed, and you'll be observed for 30–60 minutes before you can go.

If you're having oral chemotherapy, your oncologist or nurse will explain exactly how and when to take your tablets, what side effects to watch for, and which foods or medications to avoid.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

After each cycle, you'll likely feel the side effects most strongly in the first 3–7 days, and then they'll gradually ease. You may experience tiredness, nausea, loss of appetite, changes in how food tastes, mouth sores, and hair thinning or loss. Your care team will provide a clear plan to manage them — anti-sickness medications, mouth care rinses, and practical dietary advice.

Will you lose your hair? It depends on the drugs you receive. Some cause significant hair loss, while others cause only thinning. If hair loss is expected, it usually begins 2–3 weeks after your first cycle. It's temporary for most people, and your hair will start growing back after treatment ends. Many patients bring a hat, scarf, or wig in advance.

Monitoring your blood counts between cycles is important. Watch for warning signs: a fever above 38°C (100.4°F) needs immediate attention because it could mean an infection when your immunity is low. Unusual bruising or bleeding could mean low platelets, and feeling excessively tired or short of breath could indicate anaemia. Contact your oncology team right away if you notice any of these.

Between cycles, rest and hydration are your best friends. Drink plenty of fluids, eat small frequent meals, and try gentle activity like short walks. Avoid crowds and people who are unwell because your immunity will be lower than usual.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

We understand that you want to know what to expect in the long run, and your oncologist will be honest with you about your specific situation. The effectiveness of chemotherapy depends on your cancer type, its stage, and your individual circumstances. For many cancers, chemotherapy makes a real difference to survival when it's part of a combined treatment plan. For example, chemotherapy after surgery for stage II and III bowel cancer reduces the chance of recurrence by about 30–40%, and chemotherapy before surgery for breast cancer can shrink tumours enough to allow breast-conserving surgery instead of a mastectomy.

If your cancer has spread, chemotherapy may not cure it, but it can extend your life and improve how you feel by controlling the disease. Modern treatment combinations with good supportive care can add months or even years. Your oncologist will discuss realistic expectations with you openly — no false promises, but genuine hope based on the evidence.

Some long-term side effects are possible, such as tingling or numbness in your hands and feet (peripheral neuropathy), effects on your heart with certain drugs, or difficulty with concentration and memory sometimes called "chemo brain." Most side effects gradually improve after treatment finishes, though some may persist.

## Alternatives to Consider

Chemotherapy isn't your only option, and your oncologist will discuss all the possibilities with you. Depending on your cancer's specific characteristics, other treatments might be more suitable or combined with chemotherapy for better results.

Targeted therapy attacks specific molecular changes driving your cancer's growth. If your cancer has an identifiable target — like EGFR-mutant lung cancer or HER2-positive breast cancer — targeted therapy may work better than standard chemotherapy and often causes fewer side effects. Some targeted therapies come as tablets, which means you might not need to spend as much time in hospital.

Immunotherapy helps your own immune system recognise and fight the cancer. It has shown remarkable results for melanoma, certain lung cancers, and kidney cancer. If your tumour tests show high PD-L1 or high microsatellite instability (MSI-H), immunotherapy might be recommended as your first treatment. Your oncologist in Bangalore will test your cancer's molecular profile and discuss whether these options are right for you.

Sometimes, the best approach is a combination — chemotherapy alongside targeted therapy or immunotherapy. Every person's cancer is different, and the decision is always personalised to you.

## Travel & Visa

To travel to Bangalore for chemotherapy, you'll need an Indian e-Medical Visa, which allows you to stay for up to 60 days and return for follow-up. The application is done online and usually processed within 3–5 working days. You'll need a letter from the treating hospital, which Khan Meditour will arrange once your treatment plan is confirmed.

One of the biggest decisions you'll face is whether to stay in Bangalore for your entire treatment or travel home between cycles. If your cycles are 3 weeks apart, you may be able to return home during the gap. Staying in Bangalore means continuous monitoring and immediate access to your oncology team, while going home lets you recover in familiar surroundings. Your oncologist will advise based on how well you're responding and how your blood counts are holding up.

We recommend bringing a companion with you — a family member or close friend who can support you emotionally and practically. Pack comfortable, loose-fitting clothes, a hat or scarf in case you lose your hair, your medical records organised in a folder, and any personal comfort items.

Most surgeons recommend waiting at least 7-14 days before taking a long-haul flight, to reduce the risk of deep vein thrombosis and other travel-related complications. Khan Meditour can arrange a fitness-to-fly certificate from your medical team and advise you on in-flight precautions such as compression stockings, hydration, and periodic movement during the journey.
