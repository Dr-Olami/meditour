## Overview

Being diagnosed with lung cancer is one of the most frightening experiences you can face. You may be struggling to catch your breath — both literally and figuratively — as you try to process what this means for you and your family. The questions can feel overwhelming: What treatment will I need? Will I lose my hair? Can I still travel? Will I be able to breathe normally again? This guide is here to walk you through what lung cancer treatment in Bangalore actually involves, so you can face the road ahead with clarity rather than fear.

Lung cancer treatment depends on the type you have — non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) or small cell lung cancer (SCLC) — as well as the stage at diagnosis and your overall lung function. Modern lung cancer care integrates surgery, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy, often in combination. Bangalore's multidisciplinary thoracic oncology teams include thoracic surgeons, medical oncologists, radiation oncologists, pulmonologists and pathologists who collaborate to design a personalised plan for you.

You will have access to advanced diagnostic capabilities including high-resolution CT, PET-CT, bronchoscopy with biopsy, EBUS and comprehensive molecular profiling for EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF and PD-L1. The cost ranges from $6,000 to $25,000 for a complete course — savings of up to 90% compared to the United States — with access to the same advanced thoracic surgical techniques, radiation technology and targeted/immunotherapy drugs available worldwide.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Lung cancer treatment in Bangalore is suitable for you whether you have early-stage disease requiring curative surgery or advanced disease needing systemic therapy. You should have a confirmed tissue diagnosis of lung cancer (NSCLC or SCLC) and have undergone or be able to undergo appropriate staging and molecular profiling.

If you have early-stage NSCLC (Stage I–II) and are fit for surgery, you will benefit from VATS or robotic lobectomy, achieving high cure rates with minimal recovery time. If you are medically inoperable due to poor pulmonary function or comorbidities, SBRT is an excellent alternative. If you have locally advanced (Stage III) disease, chemoradiation is typically recommended. For metastatic (Stage IV) disease, treatment is tailored to your molecular profile — oral targeted therapy if you have EGFR/ALK/ROS1 mutations, or immunotherapy if you have high PD-L1 expression without driver mutations.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Before commencing treatment, you will undergo a thorough pre-treatment assessment. This includes a complete medical history review, physical examination and evaluation of comorbidities such as COPD, cardiac conditions or diabetes. Pulmonary function tests (spirometry, diffusion capacity) are essential if you are being considered for surgery.

Cardiac evaluation with ECG and echocardiography is performed for surgical patients. Nutritional assessment helps identify if you need dietary support before treatment, as lung cancer often causes weight loss and reduced appetite. Smoking cessation is critical even after diagnosis, as continued smoking can reduce treatment effectiveness and increase complications.

## Procedure Details

Your treatment journey begins with a comprehensive diagnostic workup. This includes CT and PET-CT imaging, bronchoscopy or CT-guided biopsy for tissue diagnosis, and a brain MRI to check for metastases. Molecular profiling is performed on your biopsy tissue to identify targetable mutations (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF) and PD-L1 expression, which guide the choice of targeted therapy or immunotherapy. Pulmonary function tests assess your fitness for surgery. The multidisciplinary team reviews all results and designs your personalised treatment plan.

For early-stage NSCLC, surgical resection (lobectomy or segmentectomy) is the primary treatment, often performed using minimally invasive VATS (video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery) or robotic-assisted techniques. If you are not fit for surgery, SBRT offers an excellent non-surgical alternative. For locally advanced disease, a combination of chemotherapy and radiation therapy (chemoradiation) is typically used. For metastatic disease with targetable mutations, oral targeted therapy is the first-line treatment, while immunotherapy is used for cancers with high PD-L1 expression and no targetable mutations.

Throughout your treatment, you will receive comprehensive supportive care tailored to lung cancer-specific needs. This includes management of chemotherapy side effects, radiation pneumonitis prevention, immune-related adverse event monitoring and pulmonary rehabilitation to optimise your breathing. Regular imaging every 8–12 weeks assesses your response, and your plan is adjusted accordingly.

## What Happens During the Procedure

The treatment experience varies depending on the modality. For surgical patients, VATS lobectomy is performed under general anaesthesia with one-lung ventilation. The surgeon makes 3–4 small incisions between your ribs, inserts a camera and instruments, and removes the affected lobe along with nearby lymph nodes. The procedure takes 2–3 hours with minimal blood loss and a hospital stay of 4–6 days.

For SBRT, treatment is delivered using a linear accelerator that precisely targets your tumour while sparing surrounding healthy lung tissue. Each session lasts 30–45 minutes, and you typically complete 3–5 sessions over 1–2 weeks. No anaesthesia is required, and you can return to your accommodation between sessions.

Chemotherapy is administered in day-care units over 2–4 hours per cycle, with cycles repeated every 3 weeks. Targeted therapy is taken orally at home once the initial assessment is complete. Immunotherapy is given as an intravenous infusion every 2–3 weeks, with each session lasting 30–60 minutes.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

**Will it hurt?** After VATS or robotic surgery, you will have some chest pain and discomfort around your incisions, but this is well managed with pain medication. You will typically be mobile within 24 hours and discharged in 4–6 days. A chest tube remains for 2–3 days to drain fluid and air. You will be encouraged to perform breathing exercises to expand your lungs and prevent complications. Full recovery takes 4–6 weeks, during which you should avoid heavy lifting and strenuous activity.

After SBRT, most patients experience minimal side effects, though fatigue and mild radiation pneumonitis may occur 4–8 weeks after treatment. If you are receiving chemotherapy or immunotherapy, you will be monitored for side effects between cycles, with blood counts checked before each treatment. Pulmonary rehabilitation is recommended for all patients, focusing on breathing exercises and techniques to manage breathlessness.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

Outcomes for lung cancer treatment in Bangalore's leading hospitals are comparable to international benchmarks. For early-stage NSCLC treated with surgery, five-year survival rates exceed 70–80%. SBRT for inoperable early-stage disease achieves local control rates of 85–90% with five-year survival of 50–60%. For locally advanced disease treated with chemoradiation, five-year survival ranges from 20–30%.

For metastatic disease, outcomes have improved significantly with targeted therapy and immunotherapy. Patients with EGFR-mutant lung cancer treated with osimertinib achieve median progression-free survival of 18–20 months. Immunotherapy in patients with high PD-L1 expression produces durable responses in 30–45% of patients, with some achieving long-term survival beyond 3–5 years. No centre can promise a cure, but these figures represent real hope.

## Follow-Up Care After Returning Home

After returning home, you will continue follow-up with your local oncologist while maintaining contact with the Bangalore treating team. For surgical patients, a follow-up CT scan is recommended at 3 months, then every 6 months for 2 years, and annually thereafter. If you are on targeted therapy or immunotherapy, you will continue medications locally with blood tests and imaging every 8–12 weeks.

## Alternatives to Consider

Surgery may be combined with neoadjuvant or adjuvant chemotherapy to improve outcomes. If you are unsuitable for surgery, radiofrequency ablation or microwave ablation offers a minimally invasive alternative for small tumours. Palliative care services are available for advanced cases, focusing on symptom control and quality of life. Clinical trials may be an option if you have exhausted standard therapies, providing access to investigational drugs. Your multidisciplinary team will discuss all options with you.
