## Overview

Lung cancer is one of the most common and serious types of cancer, requiring a comprehensive and personalised treatment approach. Treatment depends on the cancer type—non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) or small cell lung cancer (SCLC)—as well as the stage at diagnosis and the patient's overall pulmonary function. Modern lung cancer care integrates surgery, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy, often in combination, to achieve the best possible outcomes.

Indian oncology hospitals offer state-of-the-art lung cancer treatment through multidisciplinary thoracic oncology teams. These teams include thoracic surgeons, medical oncologists, radiation oncologists, pulmonologists, and pathologists who collaborate to design individualised treatment plans. Diagnostic capabilities include high-resolution CT, PET-CT, bronchoscopy with biopsy, EBUS (endobronchial ultrasound), and comprehensive molecular profiling for EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, and PD-L1, ensuring precise staging and treatment selection.

The cost of lung cancer treatment in India ranges from $6,000 to $25,000 for a complete course, compared to $60,000–$250,000 in the United States. This affordability, combined with access to advanced thoracic surgical techniques (including VATS and robotic surgery), cutting-edge radiation technology (SBRT, IMRT), and the full range of targeted and immunotherapy drugs, makes India a leading destination for international patients seeking lung cancer care.

## Procedure Details

Lung cancer treatment begins with a comprehensive diagnostic workup, including CT and PET-CT imaging, bronchoscopy or CT-guided biopsy for tissue diagnosis, and brain MRI to check for metastases. Molecular profiling is performed on biopsy tissue to identify targetable mutations (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF) and PD-L1 expression, which guide the choice of targeted therapy or immunotherapy. Pulmonary function tests assess the patient's fitness for surgery. The multidisciplinary team reviews all results and designs a personalised treatment plan.

For early-stage non-small cell lung cancer, surgical resection (lobectomy or segmentectomy) is the primary treatment, often performed using minimally invasive VATS (video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery) or robotic-assisted techniques. For patients not fit for surgery, SBRT offers an excellent non-surgical alternative. For locally advanced disease, a combination of chemotherapy and radiation therapy (chemoradiation) is typically used. For metastatic disease with targetable mutations, oral targeted therapy is the first-line treatment, while immunotherapy is used for cancers with high PD-L1 expression and no targetable mutations.

Throughout treatment, patients receive comprehensive supportive care tailored to lung cancer-specific needs. This includes management of chemotherapy side effects, radiation pneumonitis prevention and treatment, immune-related adverse event monitoring for immunotherapy patients, and pulmonary rehabilitation to optimise breathing capacity. Regular imaging (CT or PET-CT) every 8–12 weeks assesses treatment response, and the treatment plan is adjusted based on results. For patients on oral targeted therapy, treatment can be continued at home with periodic follow-up visits to Bangalore.
