## Overview

Breast cancer is the most common cancer among women worldwide, and its treatment requires a carefully coordinated, multimodal approach. Treatment typically involves a combination of surgery, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, targeted therapy, and hormonal therapy, tailored to the individual patient based on the cancer's stage, receptor status (ER, PR, HER2), and the patient's overall health. The goal is to achieve the best possible outcome while preserving quality of life and, where possible, breast appearance.

Indian oncology hospitals offer comprehensive breast cancer care through multidisciplinary tumour boards that include surgical oncologists, medical oncologists, radiation oncologists, pathologists, and reconstructive surgeons. These teams follow internationally recognised guidelines such as NCCN and ESMO to design personalised treatment plans. Diagnostic capabilities include digital mammography, breast MRI, core needle biopsy, immunohistochemistry, and FISH testing for HER2 status, ensuring accurate staging and receptor profiling.

The cost of breast cancer treatment in India ranges from $5,000 to $20,000 for a complete course, compared to $50,000–$200,000 in the United States. This affordability, combined with access to advanced diagnostic technology, modern surgical techniques including oncoplastic and robotic surgery, and the full range of systemic therapies, makes India a preferred destination for international patients seeking comprehensive breast cancer care.

## Procedure Details

Breast cancer treatment begins with a thorough diagnostic workup, including breast imaging (mammography, ultrasound, and sometimes MRI), core needle biopsy for histopathology, and receptor status testing (ER, PR, HER2). Staging may involve CT, PET-CT, or bone scans to check for distant spread. The multidisciplinary team reviews all results and designs a personalised treatment plan, which may include neoadjuvant therapy (treatment before surgery to shrink the tumour), surgery, adjuvant therapy (treatment after surgery), or a combination.

Surgery is a cornerstone of breast cancer treatment. Options include breast-conserving surgery (lumpectomy) with sentinel lymph node biopsy, or mastectomy with or without immediate breast reconstruction. For HER2-positive cancers, targeted therapy with trastuzumab is added to chemotherapy. For hormone receptor-positive cancers, hormonal therapy (tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitors) is prescribed for 5–10 years. Radiation therapy is typically recommended after breast-conserving surgery and sometimes after mastectomy depending on tumour characteristics.

Chemotherapy may be given before surgery (neoadjuvant) to shrink the tumour and assess treatment response, or after surgery (adjuvant) to eliminate any remaining cancer cells. The regimen typically consists of 4–8 cycles over 3–6 months. Throughout treatment, patients receive comprehensive supportive care including anti-nausea medications, growth factors, nutritional counselling, and psychological support. Regular monitoring through blood tests and imaging ensures treatment effectiveness and early detection of any side effects.
