## Overview

Hearing the words "you have breast cancer" is one of the most frightening moments you can face. You may be feeling overwhelmed, scared, and unsure about what comes next. That's completely natural, and you don't have to figure this out alone. Every year, thousands of women from Bangladesh, the Gulf countries, and across Africa travel to Bangalore for breast cancer treatment that is both world-class and affordable.

Breast cancer treatment is not a single procedure. It's a carefully planned combination of therapies — which may include surgery, chemotherapy, radiation, targeted therapy, and hormonal therapy — designed specifically for your cancer based on its stage and receptor status (ER, PR, and HER2). Your treatment team will tailor every decision to your body, your cancer, and your life. The goal is to give you the best possible chance of beating this cancer while preserving your quality of life and, wherever possible, the appearance of your breast.

Bangalore's top hospitals use the same drugs, surgical techniques, and internationally recognised treatment guidelines (NCCN and ESMO) as hospitals in the United States or Europe. The difference is the cost — treatment in India ranges from $5,000 to $20,000 for a complete course, compared to $50,000–$200,000 in the US. The savings come from India's lower cost base and the favourable exchange rate, not from lower quality.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

If you've received a confirmed breast cancer diagnosis through a core needle biopsy and histopathology, you're a candidate for treatment in Bangalore. Your treatment plan will be determined by your cancer's stage, receptor status (ER, PR, HER2), and your overall health. Both early-stage and locally advanced breast cancers can be treated effectively here.

You'll need to be well enough to tolerate the planned treatment. If you have other health conditions — such as heart disease, kidney problems, or diabetes — your team will assess these and may adjust your treatment plan or help stabilise your condition before starting cancer therapy.

This option is particularly valuable if you're facing prohibitive costs at home. If you don't have insurance, or if your insurance has high deductibles or doesn't cover the full treatment, the savings in India can be life-changing. You'll receive the same surgical techniques, chemotherapy drugs, targeted therapies, and radiation protocols — without compromising on quality or safety.

## Procedure Details

Your treatment journey begins with a thorough diagnostic workup — breast imaging (mammography, ultrasound, sometimes MRI), a core needle biopsy to confirm the diagnosis and test receptor status (ER, PR, HER2), and possibly a CT, PET-CT, or bone scan to check whether the cancer has spread.

Once your results are ready, a multidisciplinary team — including a surgical oncologist, medical oncologist, radiation oncologist, pathologist, and reconstructive surgeon — will review your case and agree on your personalised treatment plan. This might involve neoadjuvant therapy (treatment before surgery to shrink the tumour), surgery itself, adjuvant therapy (treatment after surgery), or a combination.

Surgery is often a key part of your treatment. You may have breast-conserving surgery (a lumpectomy) or a mastectomy. Your surgeon will also check your lymph nodes using a sentinel lymph node biopsy. If you're having a mastectomy, you can discuss breast reconstruction — done at the same time (immediate) or as a separate procedure later (delayed).

If your cancer is HER2-positive, you'll receive targeted therapy (such as trastuzumab) alongside chemotherapy. If hormone receptor-positive, you'll take hormonal therapy (tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitors) for 5–10 years, which you can continue at home. Chemotherapy typically involves 4–8 cycles over 3–6 months. Throughout all of this, your team will provide supportive care — anti-nausea medication, nutritional advice, and emotional support.

## What Happens During the Procedure

When you arrive at the hospital in Bangalore, your treatment begins with a comprehensive consultation. Your multidisciplinary team will review all your diagnostic records, may order additional tests, and will finalise your treatment plan. They'll explain the recommended sequence of treatments, how long each phase will take, what side effects to expect, and what outcomes you can anticipate. Nothing happens without your full understanding and consent.

If you're having surgery, you'll be admitted on the day of, or the day before, your procedure. A lumpectomy typically takes 1–2 hours, while a mastectomy with reconstruction may take 3–6 hours. You'll be under general anaesthesia. Your surgeon will perform a sentinel lymph node biopsy during the operation. After surgery, you'll typically stay in hospital for 2–5 days.

For chemotherapy, each cycle is given in a dedicated infusion suite, with oncology nurses and your medical oncologist supervising. The infusion can take anywhere from 30 minutes to several hours. For radiation therapy, you'll attend daily sessions Monday to Friday for 5–6 weeks, with each session lasting about 15–30 minutes. Modern techniques minimise exposure to surrounding healthy tissue.

Throughout every phase, your oncology team will monitor you closely through blood tests, imaging, and regular check-ups. If side effects arise, they'll be managed proactively.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

After surgery, you'll stay in hospital for 2–5 days. You may have surgical drains in place to prevent fluid build-up (seroma) — these are usually removed before you go home or at a follow-up visit. You'll be encouraged to start gentle arm movements to prevent stiffness, and a physiotherapist may give you exercises. Pain is managed with oral medication, and you'll receive clear wound care instructions. Will it hurt? You'll have some soreness and discomfort, especially in the first few days, but this is well managed with pain medication and gradually eases.

If you're receiving chemotherapy, side effects typically peak 3–7 days after each cycle and then gradually improve. You may experience fatigue, nausea, reduced appetite, changes in taste, and hair loss. Losing your hair is one of the most emotionally difficult parts of treatment for many women, and it's okay to grieve that. The good news is that hair loss from chemotherapy is almost always temporary; your hair will grow back, usually starting 2–3 months after your last cycle. Your team will provide a comprehensive side effect management plan. Watch for fever, unusual bleeding, or excessive tiredness between cycles.

After radiation therapy, you may notice your skin becoming red, dry, or peeling in the treated area — similar to a sunburn. Fatigue is common and tends to build up over the course of treatment. Most skin reactions settle within 2–4 weeks after you finish radiation.

Emotional support is just as important as physical care during recovery. Bangalore hospitals offer counselling services, support groups, and access to breast care nurses who specialise in helping women through exactly what you're experiencing.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

It's natural to want to know: "Am I going to be okay?" The honest answer is that breast cancer treatment outcomes in India are comparable to those in Western countries, because the same treatment protocols, drugs, and technologies are used. For early-stage breast cancer, five-year survival rates exceed 90% with appropriate treatment.

For locally advanced breast cancer, neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by surgery and radiation achieves good long-term control for the majority of patients. If your cancer is HER2-positive, targeted therapy with trastuzumab has significantly improved outcomes — reducing recurrence rates by approximately 50% when combined with chemotherapy. If your cancer is ER-positive, hormonal therapy continued for 5–10 years further reduces your risk of recurrence.

Long-term follow-up is essential. Your surveillance will typically include clinical examinations every 3–6 months for the first two years, annual mammography, and periodic imaging. Most side effects of treatment do resolve over time, though some — such as lymphoedema or joint pain from hormonal therapy — may need ongoing management.

## Alternatives to Consider

Breast cancer treatment is highly individualised. For some women, surgery alone may be sufficient — particularly for very early-stage cancers. Others may benefit from neoadjuvant therapy to shrink the tumour before surgery, which can sometimes make breast-conserving surgery possible instead of a full mastectomy.

If your cancer is HER2-positive, targeted therapy with trastuzumab, pertuzumab, or T-DM1 may be recommended alongside or instead of traditional chemotherapy. These targeted drugs attack specific proteins on your cancer cells and often cause fewer side effects. If your cancer is hormone receptor-positive and you're postmenopausal, hormonal therapy alone may be enough for certain low-risk cases.

If you have triple-negative breast cancer, immunotherapy may be an option, particularly in combination with chemotherapy for locally advanced or metastatic disease. Your multidisciplinary team in Bangalore will discuss all available options and recommend the most appropriate treatment based on your cancer's molecular profile, its stage, and your own health and preferences.
