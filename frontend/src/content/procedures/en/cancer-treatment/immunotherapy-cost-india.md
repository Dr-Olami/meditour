## Overview

Immunotherapy is an advanced cancer treatment that harnesses the body's own immune system to identify and destroy cancer cells. Unlike chemotherapy, which directly targets rapidly dividing cells, immunotherapy works by blocking proteins that shield cancer cells from immune detection—known as immune checkpoint inhibition—or by using engineered monoclonal antibodies to mark cancer cells for immune attack. This approach has revolutionised cancer care, offering durable responses in cancers that were previously difficult to treat.

In India, immunotherapy is administered in leading oncology centres equipped with modern infusion suites and staffed by medical oncologists with expertise in immunotherapy protocols. The drugs used—including checkpoint inhibitors such as pembrolizumab, nivolumab, and ipilimumab—are sourced from the same global manufacturers that supply Western hospitals, ensuring identical quality and efficacy. Treatment follows internationally recognised guidelines such as NCCN and ESMO.

The cost of immunotherapy in India ranges from $5,000 to $15,000 per course, representing savings of up to 90% compared to the United States and Europe. This affordability, combined with access to cutting-edge immunotherapy drugs and comprehensive immune-related adverse event management, makes India a compelling destination for international patients seeking this advanced treatment modality.

## Procedure Details

Immunotherapy treatment begins with comprehensive biomarker testing to determine eligibility. Tests may include PD-L1 expression analysis, microsatellite instability (MSI) testing, tumour mutational burden (TMB) assessment, and specific genetic markers depending on the cancer type. Once eligibility is confirmed, the medical oncologist designs a personalised treatment plan specifying the drug, dosage, schedule, and duration.

Immunotherapy drugs are administered intravenously in a dedicated infusion suite, with each session typically lasting 30–60 minutes. Depending on the drug, infusions are given every 2, 3, or 4 weeks. Before each session, patients undergo blood tests and a clinical assessment to check for immune-related adverse events, which can affect the skin, gastrointestinal tract, liver, lungs, or endocrine system. Most patients receive treatment on a day-care basis and return to their accommodation the same day.

A critical aspect of immunotherapy is the management of immune-related adverse events, which differ from traditional chemotherapy side effects. Patients are closely monitored for symptoms such as rash, diarrhoea, breathlessness, or thyroid dysfunction, and treatment may be paused or supplemented with corticosteroids if significant immune reactions occur. Response to immunotherapy is assessed through periodic imaging (CT or PET-CT) every 8–12 weeks, and treatment is continued as long as the patient is benefiting.
