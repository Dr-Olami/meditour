## Overview

If your oncologist has mentioned immunotherapy as a treatment option, you may be feeling a mix of hope and uncertainty. You have probably heard that immunotherapy is one of the most exciting breakthroughs in cancer treatment — but you may also be wondering what it will be like, whether it will work for you, and what side effects to expect. These are all completely natural questions, and this guide will help you understand what to expect when you choose Bangalore for your immunotherapy treatment.

Immunotherapy is fundamentally different from chemotherapy. Instead of directly attacking cancer cells, it works by unleashing your own immune system to recognise and destroy them. Cancer cells often hide from your immune system by putting up "shields" — proteins that switch off your immune response. Immunotherapy drugs called checkpoint inhibitors (such as pembrolizumab, nivolumab and ipilimumab) block these shields, allowing your immune cells to see and attack the cancer. This approach has revolutionised cancer care, offering durable responses in cancers that were previously very difficult to treat.

In Bangalore, you will receive the same drugs from the same global manufacturers that supply Western hospitals, following internationally recognised guidelines (NCCN and ESMO). The cost ranges from $5,000 to $15,000 per course — savings of up to 90% compared to the United States, where immunotherapy often costs over $100,000 per year. This affordability, combined with expert immune-related adverse event management, makes Bangalore a compelling choice.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Immunotherapy may be suitable for you if your tumour expresses specific biomarkers that predict a response to immune checkpoint inhibition. The most common biomarker is PD-L1, which is tested on your tumour tissue using immunohistochemistry. If you have high PD-L1 expression in cancers such as non-small cell lung cancer, melanoma or head and neck cancer, you may be eligible for first-line immunotherapy. Other biomarkers include microsatellite instability (MSI-H) and high tumour mutational burden (TMB-H).

You must have adequate organ function — liver, kidney, heart and lung — verified through blood tests. If you have an active autoimmune disease such as rheumatoid arthritis, lupus or inflammatory bowel disease, you may not be a suitable candidate, as checkpoint inhibitors can activate your immune system and worsen these conditions. However, each case is evaluated individually, and if your autoimmune disease is well-controlled, you may still be considered.

Immunotherapy is generally recommended if you have an ECOG performance status of 0–2, meaning you are fully active or have only mild limitations in your daily activities. It is used as first-line treatment for cancers with high biomarker expression, as second-line treatment after chemotherapy has failed, in combination with chemotherapy for certain advanced cancers, and as adjuvant therapy after surgery in melanoma and lung cancer to reduce recurrence risk.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Although immunotherapy does not involve surgery, thorough preparation is essential. You will be counselled extensively about potential immune-related side effects and given clear instructions on what symptoms to report immediately. Unlike chemotherapy side effects, which are often predictable and occur within days, immune-related adverse events can appear at any time — during or even after treatment ends — and can affect virtually any organ system.

You will receive written information and emergency contact details so you can reach the oncology team at any time. Khan Meditour ensures you understand the reporting process and helps arrange prompt medical attention if any concerns arise.

## Procedure Details

Your immunotherapy journey begins with a comprehensive consultation with a medical oncologist who specialises in immunotherapy. Your medical history, previous treatments and current symptoms will be reviewed, and your biomarker test results analysed to confirm that immunotherapy is the right choice for you. If biomarker testing has not yet been performed, it will be arranged on your existing biopsy tissue or through a fresh biopsy if needed.

Baseline tests are conducted before your first infusion, including a complete blood count, liver and kidney function tests, thyroid function tests and cardiac assessment. These establish a reference point against which any immune-related side effects can be compared during treatment. A CT or PET-CT scan is also performed to document the current extent of your disease. These baseline assessments typically take 2–3 days.

Your immunotherapy will be administered in cycles every 2–4 weeks, depending on the specific drug. Each infusion takes 30–60 minutes in a day-care suite. Between infusions, your blood counts and organ function will be monitored, and imaging studies (CT or PET-CT) performed every 8–12 weeks to evaluate your tumour response. Your oncologist will review these results and determine whether to continue treatment, pause it due to side effects, or adjust the approach.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of each infusion, you will arrive at the hospital's day-care infusion suite. A nurse will check your vital signs — blood pressure, heart rate, temperature and oxygen levels — and review any symptoms you have experienced since your last visit. Blood tests may be drawn, and the oncologist will conduct a brief clinical assessment to look for signs of immune-related adverse events such as skin rash, diarrhoea or breathlessness.

**Will the infusion hurt?** If all checks are satisfactory, the immunotherapy drug is administered through an intravenous line in your arm. Each infusion takes approximately 30–60 minutes. You may feel mild warmth or flushing, but most patients tolerate the infusion well and experience no discomfort. A nurse remains nearby to monitor for any immediate reactions.

After the infusion, you will be observed for 30–60 minutes to ensure there are no delayed reactions. Once cleared, you can return to your accommodation. Most patients are able to eat, walk and carry out normal activities on the same day, though some may experience mild fatigue. Your oncologist will provide a treatment calendar outlining all planned infusion dates.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

Since immunotherapy is delivered on an outpatient basis, there is no traditional recovery period. However, ongoing monitoring for immune-related adverse events is a critical part of your care. You should report any new symptoms immediately, no matter how minor they may seem — early intervention can prevent serious complications. Common side effects include fatigue, skin rash, mild diarrhoea and thyroid dysfunction, all of which can usually be managed effectively with prompt treatment.

More serious immune-related adverse events, while less common, can include severe colitis, pneumonitis, hepatitis or endocrinopathies such as adrenal insufficiency. These require prompt recognition and treatment with corticosteroids or other immunosuppressive agents. The oncology team in Bangalore is experienced in managing these events and has established protocols for their detection and treatment. You will receive a 24-hour contact number.

Response to immunotherapy is assessed through periodic imaging. Some patients experience a phenomenon known as pseudoprogression, where tumours initially appear larger on imaging due to immune cell infiltration before subsequently shrinking. Your oncologist is trained to distinguish this from true disease progression.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

Immunotherapy has transformed outcomes for several previously hard-to-treat cancers. In melanoma, checkpoint inhibitors have improved five-year survival rates from approximately 15% to over 40% for advanced disease. In non-small cell lung cancer with high PD-L1 expression, first-line immunotherapy achieves response rates of 30–45%, with a significant proportion of patients experiencing durable responses lasting years.

Not all patients respond to immunotherapy — approximately 20–40% of patients with qualifying biomarkers experience meaningful tumour shrinkage, while others may achieve stable disease. For those who do respond, the durability of response is a key advantage: some patients maintain remission long after treatment has stopped, suggesting the immune system has been permanently reprogrammed to control the cancer. Long-term follow-up includes periodic imaging and blood tests, typically every 3–6 months for the first two years and then annually.

## Alternatives to Consider

If biomarker testing shows that your tumour does not express PD-L1, MSI-H or high TMB, immunotherapy may not be the most effective option for you. In such cases, chemotherapy — either alone or in combination with targeted therapy — remains the standard treatment. Targeted therapy is another alternative if your tumour harbours specific driver mutations such as EGFR, ALK, BRAF or HER2. These oral medications can be highly effective and are often better tolerated.

For localised cancer that has not spread, surgery or radiation therapy may offer a better chance of cure than immunotherapy, which is primarily used for advanced or metastatic disease. Your multidisciplinary team in Bangalore will review all available options and recommend the approach most likely to benefit you.
