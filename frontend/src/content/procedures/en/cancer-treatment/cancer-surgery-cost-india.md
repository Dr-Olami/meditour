## Overview

Surgical oncology, or cancer surgery, is the physical removal of a tumour and surrounding tissue to treat cancer. It is one of the oldest and most effective cancer treatments, often providing the best chance of cure for solid tumours that have not spread beyond their primary site. Surgery may be used as a standalone treatment or in combination with chemotherapy, radiation therapy, or other modalities as part of a multidisciplinary treatment plan.

Indian oncology hospitals offer the full range of surgical oncology services, from traditional open surgery to advanced minimally invasive techniques including laparoscopic and robotic-assisted surgery. Surgical oncologists in Bangalore are highly skilled, with many trained at premier institutions in India and abroad. They work as part of multidisciplinary tumour boards alongside medical and radiation oncologists to ensure each patient receives the most appropriate and comprehensive treatment plan.

The cost of cancer surgery in India ranges from $4,000 to $15,000 depending on the type and complexity of the procedure, compared to $40,000–$120,000 in the United States. This dramatic cost difference, combined with world-class surgical expertise, modern operating theatres, and comprehensive perioperative care, makes India a leading destination for international patients seeking cancer surgery.

## Procedure Details

Cancer surgery begins with a thorough pre-operative evaluation, including imaging studies (CT, MRI, or PET-CT) to determine the tumour's size, location, and relationship to surrounding structures. A pre-anaesthetic assessment evaluates the patient's fitness for surgery, including cardiac and pulmonary function tests. The surgical oncologist then plans the procedure, considering the goal of surgery—whether curative, debulking, or palliative—and the most appropriate surgical approach.

During surgery, the tumour is removed along with a margin of healthy tissue to ensure complete excision. Lymph nodes in the region may also be removed (lymphadenectomy) to check for cancer spread. Depending on the cancer type and location, the procedure may be performed as open surgery, laparoscopic surgery, or robotic-assisted surgery. Minimally invasive techniques offer smaller incisions, less post-operative pain, and faster recovery. In some cases, reconstructive surgery is performed simultaneously to restore function or appearance.

After surgery, the patient is monitored in the recovery room and then transferred to a hospital room or ICU depending on the procedure's complexity. The surgical team manages pain control, wound care, and early mobilisation. The resected tissue is sent for pathological examination, which provides critical information about tumour type, grade, margin status, and lymph node involvement. These results guide decisions about any additional treatment needed, such as chemotherapy or radiation therapy.
