## Overview

Being told that your cancer has a specific genetic mutation can feel frightening, but it also opens a door to one of the most exciting advances in modern oncology—targeted therapy. Targeted therapy is a precision cancer treatment that uses drugs designed to specifically identify and attack cancer cells based on their unique molecular characteristics. Unlike conventional chemotherapy, which affects all rapidly dividing cells, targeted therapy zeroes in on specific proteins, genes, or signalling pathways that cancer cells rely on for growth and survival. This approach minimises damage to healthy cells and often results in fewer and more manageable side effects.

In India, targeted therapy is delivered in advanced oncology centres with access to comprehensive molecular profiling laboratories. The targeted agents used—including tyrosine kinase inhibitors, monoclonal antibodies, and proteasome inhibitors—are sourced from the same global pharmaceutical companies that supply Western hospitals. Treatment is guided by molecular test results and follows internationally recognised protocols from NCCN, ESMO, and other leading oncology guidelines.

The cost of targeted therapy in India ranges from $3,000 to $12,000 per course, representing savings of up to 90% compared to the United States and Europe. This affordability is particularly significant for targeted therapies, which are among the most expensive cancer treatments globally. Indian hospitals offer these cutting-edge drugs at dramatically reduced prices, making precision oncology accessible to international patients from Bangladesh, the Gulf, Africa, and beyond who might otherwise be unable to afford these life-changing medicines.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Targeted therapy is suitable for patients whose cancer has been confirmed through biopsy and whose tumour has undergone molecular profiling that identifies a targetable mutation or protein. Not every cancer has a known target, which is why genetic testing is the essential first step. If your tumour carries a mutation such as EGFR, ALK, HER2, BRAF, or KRAS, targeted therapy may be a powerful option for you.

You will need adequate organ function—liver, kidney, and heart—confirmed through blood tests, and a performance status of 0–2 on the ECOG scale, meaning you are reasonably active and able to care for yourself. You should have no contraindications to the specific targeted agent being considered, and you must be willing to undergo regular monitoring and adhere to oral medication regimens where applicable.

Patients with lung, breast, colorectal, melanoma, and certain blood cancers are among the most common candidates. For patients travelling from Bangladesh, the Gulf, and Africa, the ability to start treatment in Bangalore and continue oral therapy at home makes targeted therapy both practical and affordable. Your oncologist will discuss whether your specific cancer profile makes you a good candidate.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Preparation for targeted therapy begins with a thorough consultation with your medical oncologist. You will need to bring your biopsy reports, pathology slides, and any previous imaging or treatment records. If molecular profiling has not yet been done, it will be arranged in Bangalore—this typically takes 1–2 weeks and is performed on your biopsy tissue or a blood sample.

You will undergo blood tests to assess liver and kidney function, a heart evaluation (such as an ECG or echocardiogram) if the planned drug carries cardiac risks, and a baseline CT or PET-CT scan to measure your tumour before treatment begins. Your oncologist will review the specific drug, its schedule, and the side effects you should expect, and will give you clear instructions on what to do if side effects arise.

If you are travelling from abroad, we recommend arriving at least one week before your planned treatment start date so all testing can be completed without rushing. Our team will coordinate every appointment and keep you informed at each step.

## Procedure Details

Targeted therapy begins with molecular profiling of the tumour, which involves testing biopsy tissue or blood samples for specific genetic mutations, gene amplifications, or protein expressions. Common targets include EGFR mutations in lung cancer, HER2 amplification in breast and gastric cancer, BRAF mutations in melanoma, and BCR-ABL in chronic myeloid leukaemia. The results of these tests determine which targeted agent is appropriate for you, and this personalised approach is what makes targeted therapy so effective.

Once a target is identified, the medical oncologist prescribes the appropriate targeted therapy. Many targeted therapies are oral medications taken daily at home, while others are administered as intravenous infusions in a hospital or clinic setting. For oral therapies, you receive your initial supply in Bangalore and can continue treatment at home, with periodic follow-up visits for reassessment. For IV therapies, you attend infusion sessions every 1–3 weeks depending on the drug and protocol.

Monitoring during targeted therapy is essential. You will undergo regular blood tests to check for side effects such as liver enzyme elevation, cardiac toxicity, or blood count changes. Imaging studies (CT or PET-CT) are performed every 8–12 weeks to assess tumour response. Side effects are generally milder than chemotherapy but can include skin rashes, diarrhoea, fatigue, and hypertension, which are managed with supportive medications and dose adjustments as needed. Your oncologist will explain exactly what to watch for and when to call for help.

## What Happens During the Procedure

If your targeted therapy is an oral medication, you will simply take tablets or capsules daily, usually at home. Before you leave Bangalore, your oncologist will confirm the correct dose, watch for any initial side effects over the first 1–2 weeks, and ensure you tolerate the drug well. You will be given a clear schedule and a supply of medication to take home.

If your targeted therapy is intravenous, you will attend day-care infusion sessions at the hospital. Each session lasts between 30 minutes and a few hours depending on the drug. You will sit in a comfortable chair in the chemotherapy day-care unit, and a nurse will monitor you throughout. You can read, use your phone, or have a family member sit with you. Most patients feel well enough to return to their accommodation the same evening.

During treatment, you will have regular blood tests and imaging scans every 8–12 weeks to check how your cancer is responding. Your oncologist will review the results with you and adjust the treatment if needed. Many patients are relieved to find that targeted therapy is far less disruptive to daily life than they expected.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

Recovery from targeted therapy is different from surgery or chemotherapy—because many targeted therapies are ongoing, "recovery" really means managing side effects while continuing treatment. Most side effects are mild to moderate and can be controlled. Skin rashes, particularly with EGFR inhibitors, are common and are managed with topical creams and sometimes oral antibiotics. Diarrhoea is treated with antidiarrhoeal medication and dietary adjustments.

You will remain in Bangalore for 1–2 weeks after starting treatment so the team can assess your initial tolerance and optimise your dosage. If you are on oral therapy, you will then return home with a supply of medication and clear instructions. Your oncologist will arrange remote follow-up and periodic return visits for reassessment. If side effects become troublesome at home, you can contact the team for guidance at any time.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

Targeted therapy has transformed outcomes for many cancers. For EGFR-mutated lung cancer, targeted therapy achieves response rates of 70–80% with median progression-free survival of 10–18 months. For HER2-positive breast cancer, targeted therapy has dramatically improved survival rates. In chronic myeloid leukaemia, tyrosine kinase inhibitors have turned a once-fatal disease into a manageable condition with near-normal life expectancy for most patients.

Response depends on the specific mutation, cancer type, and stage. Some patients remain on targeted therapy for years with stable disease and good quality of life. Resistance can develop over time, and your oncologist will monitor for this and discuss alternative targeted agents or combinations if needed. The key advantage is that targeted therapy often allows you to maintain a near-normal lifestyle while keeping the cancer under control.

## Alternatives to Consider

Targeted therapy is one option within a broader treatment landscape. Depending on your cancer type and stage, alternatives may include chemotherapy, immunotherapy, radiation therapy, or surgery. In many cases, targeted therapy is combined with other treatments for the best result. Your oncologist will explain which options suit your specific molecular profile and help you weigh the benefits, side effects, and costs of each approach so you can make the decision that feels right for you and your family.
