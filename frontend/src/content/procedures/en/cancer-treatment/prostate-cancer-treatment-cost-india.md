## Overview

Hearing that you have prostate cancer can leave you with a swirl of emotions — fear, confusion, and perhaps embarrassment about discussing symptoms that feel deeply personal. You may be worried about what treatment will mean for your urinary function, your sexual function, and your daily life. These concerns are completely natural, and you should feel comfortable discussing them openly with your medical team. The good news is that prostate cancer is one of the most treatable cancers, especially when caught early, and Bangalore's uro-oncology teams are highly experienced in helping men navigate this journey.

Prostate cancer treatment requires a nuanced approach that balances cancer control with preservation of your urinary and sexual function. Treatment options range from active surveillance for low-risk disease to surgery, radiation therapy, hormone therapy and systemic therapy for advanced cases. The choice depends on your cancer's risk category (based on PSA level, Gleason score and clinical stage), your age and overall health, and your personal preferences regarding outcomes and side effects.

Bangalore's multidisciplinary uro-oncology teams follow internationally recognised guidelines (NCCN and EAU) to design your personalised treatment plan. Advanced diagnostic capabilities include multiparametric prostate MRI, PSMA PET-CT, genomic testing and precision biopsy techniques. The cost ranges from $5,000 to $18,000 — savings of up to 90% compared to the United States — with access to robotic surgery, advanced radiation techniques and the full range of hormonal and systemic therapies.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Prostate cancer treatment in Bangalore is suitable for you whether you have localised low-risk cancer or advanced metastatic disease. You should have a confirmed prostate cancer diagnosis based on biopsy and histopathology, with documented PSA levels, Gleason score and clinical staging.

If you have localised low-risk prostate cancer, active surveillance may be recommended — avoiding the side effects of immediate treatment while monitoring for progression. If you have intermediate or high-risk localised disease, curative treatment with robotic prostatectomy, radiation therapy or brachytherapy will be discussed. Men with a life expectancy of at least 10 years are generally considered appropriate for curative-intent treatment.

For locally advanced or metastatic disease, systemic therapy with hormone treatment combined with newer agents offers excellent disease control and prolonged survival. You should discuss potential side effects — particularly regarding urinary and sexual function — openly with your treating team. Realistic expectations and shared decision-making are central to achieving satisfactory outcomes.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Before beginning treatment, you will undergo a comprehensive pre-treatment assessment including a detailed medical history, physical examination and evaluation of comorbidities. Cardiac evaluation with ECG and echocardiography is performed for surgical patients.

If you are considering robotic prostatectomy, pre-operative counselling will cover the surgical procedure, expected hospital stay and potential side effects including urinary incontinence and erectile dysfunction. Pelvic floor exercises will be taught before surgery to strengthen the muscles that support urinary continence, accelerating your recovery. You will be advised to stop blood-thinning medications several days before surgery.

For radiation therapy patients, a planning CT scan and MRI are performed to map your prostate and surrounding organs precisely. If you are undergoing brachytherapy, a pre-implant ultrasound will determine prostate size and seed placement strategy.

## Procedure Details

Your treatment begins with a comprehensive diagnostic evaluation including PSA testing, digital rectal examination, multiparametric prostate MRI and prostate biopsy with Gleason scoring. For staging, PSMA PET-CT or conventional CT and bone scans may be used. The multidisciplinary team assigns a risk category (low, intermediate or high) based on these results and discusses treatment options with you, considering cancer control, potential side effects and quality of life.

For localised prostate cancer, curative treatment options include robotic-assisted radical prostatectomy, external beam radiation therapy (IMRT/IGRT/VMAT) and brachytherapy. Robotic prostatectomy offers nerve-sparing precision that improves recovery of urinary continence and erectile function. Radiation therapy may be combined with short-term hormone therapy for intermediate and high-risk cases. For low-risk disease, active surveillance with regular PSA monitoring and periodic biopsy may be recommended to avoid overtreatment.

For advanced or metastatic prostate cancer, treatment typically involves androgen deprivation therapy (hormone therapy) as the foundation, combined with newer agents such as abiraterone, enzalutamide or docetaxel chemotherapy. Throughout your treatment, you will receive comprehensive supportive care including management of hormone therapy side effects, bone health monitoring and pelvic floor rehabilitation.

## What Happens During the Procedure

For robotic-assisted radical prostatectomy, the procedure is performed under general anaesthesia. The surgeon makes 5–6 small incisions in your abdomen, inserts robotic instruments and a high-definition camera, and removes your prostate gland along with seminal vesicles and nearby lymph nodes if indicated. Nerve-sparing techniques are used where appropriate to preserve erectile function. The procedure takes 2–3 hours with minimal blood loss and a hospital stay of 2–3 days.

For external beam radiation therapy, you will attend daily treatment sessions (Monday to Friday) over 5–8 weeks. Each session lasts 15–20 minutes, with you lying still while the linear accelerator delivers precisely targeted radiation. No anaesthesia is required, and you can continue normal activities between sessions.

Brachytherapy involves a single procedure under spinal or general anaesthesia, where radioactive seeds are implanted into your prostate through needles guided by transrectal ultrasound. The procedure takes 1–2 hours, and you are typically discharged the same day or after an overnight stay.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

**Will it hurt?** After robotic prostatectomy, you will have some discomfort around your incision sites, but this is well managed with pain medication. A urinary catheter remains in place for 7–10 days to allow healing of the bladder-urethra connection. You will be encouraged to walk within 24 hours and can resume light activities within 1–2 weeks.

Pelvic floor exercises continue post-operatively to restore urinary continence, with most patients achieving good control within 4–8 weeks. Erectile function recovery is gradual and may take 6–18 months, particularly when nerve-sparing was performed. After radiation therapy, you may experience fatigue, urinary frequency or bowel changes during and shortly after treatment — these are usually temporary and resolve within a few weeks.

If you are on hormone therapy, you will be monitored for side effects including hot flashes, fatigue and bone density loss. Calcium and vitamin D supplementation, along with weight-bearing exercise, helps maintain your bone health.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

Prostate cancer treatment outcomes in Bangalore's leading hospitals are comparable to international standards. For localised prostate cancer treated with surgery or radiation, 10-year cancer-specific survival exceeds 90–95%. Robotic prostatectomy achieves excellent cancer control with positive margin rates below 10% in experienced hands. Brachytherapy for low-risk disease achieves 10-year biochemical disease-free survival of 85–90%.

For advanced prostate cancer, combined hormone therapy with abiraterone or enzalutamide has transformed outcomes, with median overall survival exceeding 5–6 years for metastatic hormone-sensitive disease. Regular PSA monitoring every 3–6 months detects recurrence early, allowing timely intervention.

## Follow-Up Care After Returning Home

After returning home, you will continue follow-up with your local urologist or oncologist while maintaining contact with the Bangalore treating team. PSA testing is recommended every 3–6 months for the first two years, then every 6–12 months thereafter. A rising PSA may indicate recurrence, prompting further evaluation.

## Alternatives to Consider

Focal therapy using high-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) or cryotherapy targets only the cancerous portion of your prostate, preserving surrounding tissue and reducing side effects. Clinical trials may offer access to novel therapies if you have exhausted standard options. For elderly patients with significant comorbidities and low-risk disease, watchful waiting with deferred hormone therapy is a reasonable strategy that avoids treatment burden while maintaining disease control. Your uro-oncology team will discuss all options with you.
