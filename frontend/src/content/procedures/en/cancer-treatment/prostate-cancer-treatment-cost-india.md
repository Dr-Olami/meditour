## Overview

Prostate cancer is one of the most common cancers in men, and its treatment requires a nuanced approach that balances cancer control with preservation of urinary and sexual function. Treatment options range from active surveillance for low-risk disease to surgery, radiation therapy, hormone therapy, and systemic therapy for advanced cases. The choice depends on the cancer's risk category (based on PSA level, Gleason score, and clinical stage), the patient's age and overall health, and personal preferences regarding treatment outcomes and potential side effects.

Indian oncology hospitals offer comprehensive prostate cancer care through multidisciplinary uro-oncology teams comprising uro-oncologists, radiation oncologists, medical oncologists, and pathologists. These teams follow internationally recognised guidelines such as NCCN and EAU to design personalised treatment plans. Advanced diagnostic capabilities include multiparametric prostate MRI, PSMA PET-CT, genomic testing, and precision biopsy techniques, ensuring accurate risk stratification and treatment selection.

The cost of prostate cancer treatment in India ranges from $5,000 to $18,000 for a complete course, compared to $50,000–$180,000 in the United States. This affordability, combined with access to robotic surgery, advanced radiation techniques (IMRT, IGRT, brachytherapy), and the full range of hormonal and systemic therapies, makes India a preferred destination for international patients seeking high-quality prostate cancer care.

## Procedure Details

Prostate cancer treatment begins with a comprehensive diagnostic evaluation, including PSA testing, digital rectal examination, multiparametric prostate MRI, and prostate biopsy with Gleason scoring. For staging, PSMA PET-CT or conventional CT and bone scans may be used. The multidisciplinary team assigns a risk category (low, intermediate, or high) based on these results and discusses treatment options with the patient, considering cancer control, potential side effects, and quality of life.

For localised prostate cancer, curative treatment options include robotic-assisted radical prostatectomy, external beam radiation therapy (IMRT/IGRT/VMAT), and brachytherapy (internal radiation with radioactive seed implants). Robotic prostatectomy, available in Bangalore hospitals, offers nerve-sparing precision that improves recovery of urinary continence and erectile function. Radiation therapy may be combined with short-term hormone therapy for intermediate and high-risk cases. For low-risk disease, active surveillance with regular PSA monitoring and periodic biopsy may be recommended to avoid overtreatment.

For advanced or metastatic prostate cancer, treatment typically involves androgen deprivation therapy (hormone therapy) as the foundation, combined with newer agents such as abiraterone, enzalutamide, or docetaxel chemotherapy depending on the disease burden and patient fitness. Throughout treatment, patients receive comprehensive supportive care including management of hormone therapy side effects, bone health monitoring, pelvic floor rehabilitation for post-surgical continence recovery, and psychological support. Regular PSA monitoring guides treatment response and detects any recurrence early.
