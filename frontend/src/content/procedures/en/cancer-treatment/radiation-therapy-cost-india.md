## Overview

Being diagnosed with cancer is overwhelming, and you probably have many questions about your treatment options. If your oncologist has recommended radiation therapy, you may be feeling anxious about what lies ahead. Radiation therapy, or radiotherapy, is one of the most common and effective cancer treatments available today. It uses high-energy radiation beams to destroy cancer cells and shrink tumours, and it can be used on its own or alongside surgery, chemotherapy, or immunotherapy.

Radiation therapy can be given with curative intent, as additional (adjuvant) treatment after surgery to destroy remaining cancer cells, or for palliative purposes to relieve symptoms such as pain, bleeding, or obstruction. In many cases, it offers a real chance of controlling or eliminating the disease while preserving nearby healthy organs.

Indian oncology hospitals are equipped with advanced radiation therapy technology, including modern linear accelerators (LINAC) capable of Intensity-Modulated Radiation Therapy (IMRT), Image-Guided Radiation Therapy (IGRT), Volumetric Modulated Arc Therapy (VMAT), and Stereotactic Body Radiation Therapy (SBRT). These technologies enable highly precise tumour targeting while minimising damage to surrounding healthy tissue, leading to better outcomes and fewer side effects.

The cost of a complete radiation therapy course in India ranges from $3,000 to $8,000, which is up to 90% less than in Western countries. This affordability, combined with cutting-edge technology and experienced radiation oncologists, makes India a preferred destination for international cancer patients from Bangladesh, the Gulf, Africa, and beyond who seek high-quality radiotherapy without the crippling price tag.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Radiation therapy is recommended for patients with a confirmed cancer diagnosis where the tumour type and location are suitable for radiation. It is used across a wide range of cancers, including breast, prostate, lung, head and neck, cervical, brain, and rectal cancers. You may be a candidate if your oncologist has identified a defined tumour volume on imaging and established realistic treatment goals.

You will need adequate overall health to tolerate daily treatments over several weeks. Radiation therapy is generally not suitable during pregnancy, and certain pre-existing conditions may influence whether radiation is the right choice for you. If you have already undergone surgery, radiation may be recommended as adjuvant therapy to reduce the risk of recurrence. If your cancer is advanced, radiation can still play an important palliative role—shrinking tumours that are causing pain, bleeding, or pressure on nearby organs.

Patients from Bangladesh, the Gulf countries, and African nations frequently travel to Bangalore for radiation therapy because the quality matches Western standards at a fraction of the cost. Whether you are seeking curative treatment or symptom relief, your radiation oncologist will discuss whether this approach fits your diagnosis, stage, and personal circumstances.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Before your radiation therapy begins, you will undergo a thorough preparation process. This starts with a detailed consultation with your radiation oncologist, who will review your biopsy reports, imaging scans, and previous treatments. You may need additional imaging such as a PET-CT or MRI to precisely locate the tumour and map nearby organs.

The simulation session is a key step—you will be positioned in the exact posture you will use during treatment, and a CT scan will be taken in that position. Small permanent ink marks or tiny tattoos may be placed on your skin to ensure the radiation beam targets the same spot every session. Some patients worry about these marks; they are very small and serve an essential purpose.

You will also have blood tests and a general health assessment to confirm you are fit for the full course. Your oncologist will discuss potential side effects specific to your treatment area and give you practical guidance on skin care, nutrition, and managing fatigue. If you are travelling from abroad, we recommend arriving at least one week before your treatment start date to complete all preparation comfortably.

## Procedure Details

Radiation therapy begins with a planning phase called simulation, where you will undergo a CT scan (sometimes combined with PET or MRI) in the exact treatment position. The radiation oncologist carefully defines the tumour target and the critical organs to avoid, and medical physicists create a customised treatment plan that optimises radiation dose distribution. This planning process typically takes 1–2 weeks before treatment begins, and it is one of the most important steps—getting it right means the radiation hits the cancer and spares healthy tissue.

Once the plan is finalised, you will receive daily radiation sessions, usually five days a week (Monday to Friday) over 5–8 weeks. Each session is painless and lasts approximately 15–30 minutes, with most of the time spent on precise positioning. The actual radiation delivery takes only a few minutes. You are monitored weekly by the radiation oncologist to assess treatment response and manage any side effects.

Advanced techniques available in Bangalore hospitals allow for shorter, more intensive courses in selected cases. SBRT can deliver highly focused radiation in 3–5 sessions for small tumours, while brachytherapy (internal radiation) is used for cancers of the prostate, cervix, and breast. The choice of technique depends on the cancer type, location, stage, and your overall health. Your oncologist will explain which approach is best for your specific situation and why.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On each treatment day, you will go to the radiation oncology department as an outpatient—you are not admitted to hospital and can stay at your nearby accommodation. You will lie on the treatment table in the same position as during simulation, and the radiation therapist will align you carefully using the skin marks and imaging guidance. The machine, a linear accelerator, will move around you but will not touch you.

The actual radiation delivery lasts only a few minutes, though the full session including positioning takes 15–30 minutes. You will be alone in the treatment room during delivery, but the therapists watch you continuously through cameras and can hear you via an intercom. You can breathe normally and should remain as still as possible.

A common question patients ask is whether radiation hurts—the answer is no. You will not feel, see, or hear the radiation beams. What you may notice is a faint buzzing sound from the machine. Weekly reviews with your oncologist will track your progress and address any side effects early. Most patients can walk out of the department and resume light activities the same day, though fatigue may build up over the weeks.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

Although radiation therapy is an outpatient treatment, the side effects can accumulate over the course and continue for a short while after completion. Fatigue is the most common complaint—many patients describe it as a heaviness that builds gradually. Skin reactions in the treated area, such as redness, dryness, or peeling, are also common and usually resolve within 2–4 weeks after treatment ends.

Your oncologist will provide specific skin care instructions, recommend gentle moisturisers, and advise avoiding sun exposure and harsh soaps on the treated area. Depending on the site treated, you may experience other effects such as difficulty swallowing, changes in bowel habits, or a dry mouth. These are managed with supportive medications and dietary adjustments.

You will remain in Bangalore for 1–2 weeks after your final session so the team can assess your recovery and address any acute side effects before you travel home. Most patients feel significantly better within 2–6 weeks after completion. We will arrange a final review appointment and provide a detailed discharge summary for your oncologist back home.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

Radiation therapy has well-established success rates that vary depending on the cancer type, stage, and whether it is used alone or in combination with other treatments. For early-stage breast, prostate, and cervical cancers, radiation therapy achieves local control rates of 85–95% at five years. For head and neck cancers, combined modality treatment including radiation offers cure rates of 60–80% in locally advanced disease.

Even when a cure is not possible, radiation is highly effective at relieving symptoms—bone pain from metastases responds in over 70% of cases. Long-term side effects are possible, including tissue fibrosis or, rarely, secondary cancers years later, but modern techniques like IMRT and VMAT have significantly reduced these risks by sparing healthy tissue. Your oncologist will discuss the balance of benefits and risks specific to your case so you can make an informed decision.

## Follow-Up Care After Returning Home

After you return home, follow-up imaging is typically scheduled at 3-month intervals for the first year, then less frequently. We will send your complete treatment records, including radiation dose details and planning images, to your local oncologist so they can continue your care seamlessly. If any concerns arise, your Bangalore oncologist is available for teleconsultation.

## Alternatives to Consider

Radiation therapy is often one part of a broader treatment plan. Depending on your diagnosis, alternatives or complementary options may include surgery to remove the tumour, chemotherapy for systemic control, targeted therapy for cancers with specific genetic mutations, or immunotherapy. Sometimes a combination offers the best outcome. Your oncologist will explain which options suit your cancer type and stage, and help you weigh the benefits, side effects, and costs of each path.
