## Overview

Radiation therapy, or radiotherapy, is a cancer treatment that uses high-energy radiation beams to destroy cancer cells and shrink tumours. It is one of the most common cancer treatments, used either alone or in combination with surgery, chemotherapy, or immunotherapy. Radiation therapy can be delivered with curative intent, as adjuvant treatment after surgery, or for palliative purposes to relieve symptoms such as pain or obstruction.

Indian oncology hospitals are equipped with advanced radiation therapy technology, including modern linear accelerators (LINAC) capable of Intensity-Modulated Radiation Therapy (IMRT), Image-Guided Radiation Therapy (IGRT), Volumetric Modulated Arc Therapy (VMAT), and Stereotactic Body Radiation Therapy (SBRT). These technologies enable highly precise tumour targeting while minimising damage to surrounding healthy tissue, leading to better outcomes and fewer side effects.

The cost of a complete radiation therapy course in India ranges from $3,000 to $8,000, which is up to 90% less than in Western countries. This affordability, combined with cutting-edge technology and experienced radiation oncologists, makes India a preferred destination for international cancer patients seeking high-quality radiotherapy.

## Procedure Details

Radiation therapy begins with a planning phase called simulation, where the patient undergoes a CT scan (sometimes combined with PET or MRI) in the treatment position. The radiation oncologist defines the tumour target and critical organs to avoid, and medical physicists create a customised treatment plan that optimises radiation dose distribution. This planning process typically takes 1–2 weeks before treatment begins.

Once the plan is finalised, the patient receives daily radiation sessions, usually five days a week (Monday to Friday) over 5–8 weeks. Each session is painless and lasts approximately 15–30 minutes, with most of the time spent on precise positioning. The actual radiation delivery takes only a few minutes. Patients are monitored weekly by the radiation oncologist to assess treatment response and manage any side effects.

Advanced techniques available in Bangalore hospitals allow for shorter, more intensive courses in selected cases. SBRT can deliver highly focused radiation in 3–5 sessions for small tumours, while brachytherapy (internal radiation) is used for cancers of the prostate, cervix, and breast. The choice of technique depends on the cancer type, location, stage, and the patient's overall health.
