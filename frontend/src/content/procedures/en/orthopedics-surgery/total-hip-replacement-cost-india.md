## Overview

Total Hip Replacement (THR), or total hip arthroplasty, is a surgical procedure that replaces the damaged ball-and-socket hip joint with an artificial implant consisting of a metal or ceramic femoral head and a metal or polyethylene acetabular cup. It is one of the most reliable operations in orthopaedics, consistently relieving pain and restoring mobility in patients with end-stage hip disease.

Bangalore's leading hospitals perform total hip replacement using the same FDA-approved implant systems used in the United States and Europe, including ceramic-on-polyethylene, metal-on-polyethylene, and ceramic-on-ceramic bearing surfaces. Surgeons employ both anterior and posterior surgical approaches, with the anterior approach offering faster recovery and fewer post-operative precautions in suitable candidates.

International patients benefit from Bangalore's combination of surgical excellence, English-fluent medical teams, and costs that are 80-85% lower than in Western countries. Dedicated international patient services handle everything from visa assistance to post-operative rehabilitation planning, making the entire experience seamless.

## Procedure Details

The procedure is performed under spinal or general anaesthesia and lasts 60-90 minutes. Through an incision over the side or front of the hip, the surgeon dislocates the joint, removes the arthritic femoral head, and reams the acetabulum (socket) to fit the new cup component. The acetabular cup—metal with a plastic or ceramic liner—is pressed or cemented into place.

The femur is then prepared with a reamer, and a metal stem is inserted into the canal, either cemented or press-fit for biologic fixation. A ceramic or metal head is attached to the stem, and the joint is reduced. Stability, leg length, and range of motion are tested before layered wound closure. The surgical approach (anterior vs posterior) influences post-operative precautions and early mobility.

Rehabilitation begins on the day of surgery or the following morning. Patients walk with a walker, practice stairs, and learn hip precautions before discharge. Physiotherapy continues for 6-12 weeks to restore strength and gait. Most patients return to driving by 4-6 weeks and to low-impact sports by 3 months.
