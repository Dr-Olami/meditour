## Overview

If you are reading this, you probably know the feeling all too well — that deep, grinding ache in your hip that greets you every morning, the wince when you try to put on your socks, the way you now think twice before walking to the corner shop. Living with a deteriorating hip joint reshapes your daily life in ways large and small, and it is completely understandable if you feel frustrated, anxious, or simply exhausted by it. Total Hip Replacement (THR) — sometimes called total hip arthroplasty — is one of the most dependable operations in modern orthopaedics, and for many people it marks the moment they get their life back.

In straightforward terms, the procedure replaces your damaged ball-and-socket hip joint with an artificial implant: a metal or ceramic femoral head paired with a metal or polyethylene acetabular cup. The goal is simple but life-changing — to take away the bone-on-bone pain and give you back the mobility you have been missing.

Bangalore's leading hospitals perform this surgery using the very same FDA-approved implant systems you would find in the United States or Europe, including ceramic-on-polyethylene, metal-on-polyethylene, and ceramic-on-ceramic bearing surfaces. Your surgical team will be fluent in English, and you will have a dedicated international coordinator guiding you through every step — from your visa invitation letter to your rehabilitation plan — so that you feel supported rather than overwhelmed.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Total hip replacement is typically recommended when your hip joint disease has reached an end stage and non-surgical treatments are no longer giving you adequate relief. The most common reason is osteoarthritis — the condition where the cartilage cushioning your hip has worn away, leaving bone grinding against bone. Other reasons include rheumatoid arthritis, avascular necrosis (loss of blood supply to the femoral head), hip dysplasia, and post-traumatic arthritis following a previous fracture.

You may be a suitable candidate if you experience persistent hip or groin pain that limits everyday activities — walking, climbing stairs, putting on your shoes — and you have not responded adequately to pain medication, physiotherapy, or steroid injections. The best candidates have adequate bone stock, are medically fit for anaesthesia, and are genuinely motivated to commit to post-operative rehabilitation, which is just as important as the surgery itself.

If you are very obese, have an active hip infection, or have severe medical conditions that make anaesthesia unsafe, your surgeon may advise against replacement at this time. If you are younger with focal cartilage damage, hip preservation procedures such as arthroscopy or periacetabular osteotomy might delay the need for a replacement. Your Bangalore surgeon will review your X-rays, medical history, and functional status and give you an honest assessment of whether hip replacement is the right step for you right now.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Once your surgery date is confirmed, the pre-operative phase begins. You will undergo a comprehensive assessment including blood tests, an ECG, a chest X-ray, and an anaesthesia consultation. A dedicated pelvic X-ray with a templating marker is used to plan your implant size and leg length precisely. Your surgeon will discuss your implant choice, bearing surface, and surgical approach with you, and you will sign an informed consent form — so please ask every question you have.

Stop blood-thinning medications (aspirin, clopidogrel, warfarin) as advised, typically 5 to 7 days before surgery. If you smoke, do your best to stop at least two weeks beforehand — it genuinely reduces both anaesthetic risk and wound-healing complications. Arrange for a companion to travel with you, because you will need practical assistance during the first week after discharge, and having someone familiar nearby makes a real difference to your recovery.

Bring any previous hip X-rays, MRI scans, or surgical records for your medical team to review. Your international coordinator will give you a personalised checklist covering medication adjustments, fasting instructions, and what to pack for both your hospital stay and your recovery period in Bangalore.

## Procedure Details

The operation itself is performed under spinal or general anaesthesia and typically lasts 60 to 90 minutes. Through an incision over the side or front of your hip, your surgeon dislocates the joint, removes the arthritic femoral head, and carefully reshapes the acetabulum (the socket) to receive the new cup component. That cup — metal with a plastic or ceramic liner — is either press-fit or cemented into position.

Next, your femur is prepared with a reamer, and a metal stem is inserted into the canal, again either cemented or press-fit for biologic fixation. A ceramic or metal ball is attached to the stem, and the joint is gently reduced back into place. Before closing, your surgeon tests stability, leg length, and range of motion through a full movement cycle. The surgical approach — anterior versus posterior — influences your post-operative precautions and how quickly you can mobilise, and your surgeon will recommend the best option for your anatomy.

Rehabilitation begins almost immediately — often the same day or the following morning. You will walk with a walker, practise stairs, and learn the hip precautions you need to protect your new joint. Physiotherapy then continues for 6 to 12 weeks to rebuild strength and restore a natural gait. Most people return to driving by 4 to 6 weeks and to low-impact sports by around 3 months.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of your surgery, you will be admitted, reviewed by the anaesthetist, and given spinal or general anaesthesia. The surgical site is prepared with antiseptic and draped sterile. For a posterior approach, the incision is made over the side of your hip; for an anterior approach, it is at the front.

Your surgeon carefully divides the muscles to expose the hip joint, dislocates it, and removes the arthritic femoral head with a saw. The acetabulum is reamed to create a clean socket, and the metal cup with its liner is press-fit or cemented into position. The femoral canal is then reamed, and a metal stem is inserted — either cemented or press-fit for biologic fixation. A ceramic or metal ball is attached to the stem, and the joint is reduced back into place.

Your surgeon checks stability through a full range of motion, confirms your leg length, and takes an intra-operative X-ray. The wound is closed in layers with absorbable sutures, and a waterproof dressing is applied. The entire procedure takes 60 to 90 minutes. You will spend roughly one hour in the recovery room before being transferred to your room, where your companion can be with you.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

Your recovery begins on the day of surgery. A physiotherapist will help you stand and take a few steps with a walker within 24 hours — and yes, it is completely normal to feel nervous about this, but you will be closely supervised. Pain is managed with oral analgesics, and you will receive daily blood-thinning injections to prevent clots. You will learn your hip precautions — avoiding excessive bending, crossing your legs, or inward rotation — and these are essential to protect your new joint from dislocation during the early healing phase.

During your Bangalore stay, daily physiotherapy sessions focus on safe transfers, gait training, stair climbing, and strengthening exercises. Your wound will be reviewed, and an X-ray taken before discharge to confirm your implant position. The sutures are absorbable, so there is no removal appointment to worry about. Most people are discharged from hospital on day 3 to 5 and continue outpatient physiotherapy for the remainder of their stay.

Before you depart, you will receive a discharge summary, a rehabilitation protocol, and a fitness-to-fly certificate. Once home, keep up your physiotherapy, progress from walker to cane to unaided walking, and gradually resume your daily activities. Driving is typically safe at 4 to 6 weeks — slightly later if it is your right hip — and low-impact sports such as swimming and cycling can resume by around 3 months.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

Total hip replacement is one of the most successful operations in all of medicine. Modern implants report survivorship exceeding 90 to 95% at 15 to 20 years, which means the vast majority of people never require revision surgery. Pain relief is usually immediate and dramatic, and most people report significant improvement within the first few weeks.

Functional outcomes are equally encouraging. The majority of people walk unaided by 6 to 12 weeks, return to work, and resume low-impact recreational activities. High-impact sports such as running or contact sports are generally discouraged because they can shorten the life of your implant. Factors that affect longevity include the implant material, bearing surface, surgical technique, your weight, and your activity level — so the choices you make after surgery matter just as much as the surgery itself.

Your Bangalore surgeon will discuss realistic expectations based on your age, activity level, and implant choice. With appropriate care, a well-performed hip replacement can give you decades of pain-free mobility and a dramatically improved quality of life — though no surgeon can guarantee a specific outcome, and it is important you understand that.

Share this with your local physician and physiotherapist so they understand exactly what was done. A virtual follow-up is scheduled at 6 weeks and 3 months to review your progress and your X-rays.

If you experience increasing hip pain, swelling, redness, or a feeling of instability, contact your coordinator promptly — do not wait. Your one-year medical visa allows a return visit if an in-person review is needed. Routine long-term follow-up with annual X-rays is recommended to monitor your implant position and detect any wear early.

## Alternatives to Consider

Before committing to hip replacement, it is worth exploring conservative options with your surgeon. These include anti-inflammatory medication, physiotherapy to strengthen the muscles around your hip, weight loss to reduce joint loading, and intra-articular steroid or hyaluronic acid injections for temporary pain relief.

If you are younger with early-stage arthritis or focal cartilage damage, hip arthroscopy or hip preservation surgery may delay the need for a full replacement. Your Bangalore surgeon will give you an honest assessment of whether non-surgical management, preservation surgery, or total hip replacement is most appropriate for your specific condition and lifestyle — and they will not push you toward surgery if a simpler option could help you first.
