## Overview

Arthroscopy is a minimally invasive surgical technique that allows orthopaedic surgeons to visualise, diagnose, and treat problems inside a joint without making a large open incision. Using an arthroscope—a thin tube containing a high-definition camera and light source—inserted through a keyhole incision of just a few millimetres, the surgeon can inspect the joint interior and perform repairs using specialised miniature instruments introduced through additional small portals.

Arthroscopy is commonly performed on the knee (meniscal repair, ACL reconstruction, cartilage debridement), shoulder (rotator cuff repair, labral repair, impingement decompression), hip (femoroacetabular impingement correction, labral repair), and ankle. Compared to open surgery, arthroscopy offers significantly less post-operative pain, smaller scars, lower infection risk, and faster recovery, making it the preferred approach for a wide range of joint conditions.

In Bangalore, arthroscopy is performed at JCI- and NABH-accredited hospitals by fellowship-trained sports medicine and arthroscopy surgeons using state-of-the-art high-definition camera systems and radiofrequency ablation devices. The surgical expertise and technology are on par with leading Western centres, while the total cost is typically 80-85% lower than in the United States.

## Procedure Details

The surgery is performed under spinal, regional, or general anaesthesia depending on the joint and procedure. The surgeon makes two to four small incisions (portals) around the joint. The arthroscope is inserted through one portal, filling the joint with sterile saline fluid to expand the space and provide a clear view. Surgical instruments such as shavers, graspers, and radiofrequency probes are introduced through the other portals.

Common procedures include meniscal trimming or repair, removal of loose bodies, cartilage debridement or microfracture, ACL or PCL reconstruction, rotator cuff repair, and subacromial decompression. For ligament reconstructions, a tendon graft (often from the hamstring or patellar tendon) is harvested and fixed in place using bioabsorbable or metallic screws. The entire procedure typically takes 30-90 minutes depending on complexity.

Post-operatively, patients begin mobilisation on the same day or the next morning. A hinged brace or sling may be used depending on the joint and procedure. Most patients are discharged within 24 hours with a structured physiotherapy programme. Recovery time varies from 2 weeks for simple debridement to 8 weeks for ligament reconstruction, with full return to sports typically at 6-9 months for ACL reconstruction.
