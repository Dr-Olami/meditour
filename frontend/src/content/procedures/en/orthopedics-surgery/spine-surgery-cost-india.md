## Overview

Spine Surgery encompasses a wide range of procedures designed to treat conditions affecting the vertebral column, intervertebral discs, and spinal nerves. From minimally invasive microdiscectomy for a herniated disc to complex spinal fusion for degenerative instability or scoliosis correction, spine surgery aims to relieve pain, preserve or restore neurological function, and stabilise the spinal column.

In Bangalore, spine surgery is performed at JCI- and NABH-accredited hospitals by fellowship-trained spine surgeons—many trained at leading institutions in the US, UK, and Europe—using advanced intraoperative imaging (O-arm, C-arm), neuro-monitoring (intraoperative neuromonitoring to protect spinal nerves), and navigation systems. Both open and minimally invasive techniques are available, allowing the surgical team to tailor the approach to each patient's anatomy and pathology.

For international patients, Bangalore offers a compelling medical tourism destination: English-speaking medical teams, dedicated international patient desks, streamlined visa processing, and a temperate climate conducive to post-operative recovery. The total package, including implants, hospital stay, and initial physiotherapy, typically costs 85-90% less than in the United States.

## Procedure Details

The specific technique depends on the underlying condition. For a herniated disc, microdiscectomy involves a small incision (2-3 cm), removal of a portion of the lamina, and extraction of the herniated disc fragment compressing the nerve root—all performed under microscope or endoscope magnification. For spinal stenosis, laminectomy widens the spinal canal by removing the lamina and thickened ligaments.

For degenerative instability or spondylolisthesis, spinal fusion is performed using pedicle screws and rods to stabilise the affected vertebrae, with bone graft (autograft or allograft) placed between the vertebrae to achieve bony union. Interbody cages (PLIF, TLIF) may be used to restore disc height and foraminal patency. For scoliosis or complex deformity, multi-level fusion with corrective osteotomies is performed. All procedures are conducted under intraoperative neuromonitoring to minimise the risk of nerve injury.

Post-operatively, patients begin mobilisation within 24-48 hours with a brace and assistance from a physiotherapist. Pain is managed with a multimodal analgesia protocol. Most patients are discharged within 3-7 days with a structured home exercise programme. Full recovery varies from 3 months for simple decompression to 12 months for complex fusion, with fusion maturation confirmed by follow-up imaging.
