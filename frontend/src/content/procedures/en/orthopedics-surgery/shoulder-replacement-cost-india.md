## Overview

Shoulder Replacement, also known as shoulder arthroplasty, is a surgical procedure that replaces the damaged surfaces of the ball (humeral head) and socket (glenoid) of the shoulder joint with metal and high-grade plastic components. It is highly effective for relieving pain and restoring arm function in patients with end-stage shoulder arthritis or complex fractures that cannot be treated with joint-preserving methods.

In Bangalore, shoulder replacement is performed at JCI- and NABH-accredited hospitals by fellowship-trained shoulder and upper-limb surgeons using the same FDA-approved implants available in the United States and Europe. Both anatomic (standard) and reverse shoulder replacement techniques are offered, depending on the integrity of the rotator cuff and the underlying pathology. Advanced operation theatres with laminar airflow and computer-assisted planning ensure precise implant positioning and long-term durability.

For international patients, Bangalore offers an excellent medical tourism destination: English-speaking medical teams, dedicated international patient desks, streamlined visa processing, and a pleasant climate conducive to post-operative recovery. The total package, including implant, hospital stay, and initial physiotherapy, typically costs 80-85% less than in the United States.

## Procedure Details

The surgery is performed under general anaesthesia (often with a regional nerve block for post-operative pain control) and takes approximately 90-120 minutes. The surgeon makes an incision over the front of the shoulder, separates the deltoid and pectoralis muscles, and accesses the joint. The damaged cartilage and bone are removed from the humeral head and glenoid.

In an anatomic replacement, a metal ball on a stem is inserted into the humerus and a plastic socket is cemented or press-fit into the glenoid. In a reverse replacement, the geometry is inverted—a metal socket is fixed to the glenoid and a plastic ball is attached to the humeral stem—allowing the deltoid muscle to compensate for a deficient rotator cuff. After implantation, the joint is tested for stability and range of motion before the wound is closed in layers.

Post-operatively, the arm is immobilised in a sling. Passive range-of-motion exercises begin on day one under the guidance of a physiotherapist. Active movements are introduced gradually over the following weeks. Most patients are discharged within 2-4 days with a structured home exercise programme. Full recovery, including return to normal activities, typically takes 3-6 months.
