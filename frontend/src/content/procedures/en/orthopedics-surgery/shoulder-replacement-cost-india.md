## Overview

If you are living with chronic shoulder pain that makes it difficult to sleep, reach overhead, wash your hair, or even dress yourself, you know how exhausting it can be. Night pain is particularly distressing — many people find themselves unable to sleep on the affected side, waking repeatedly through the night. If conservative treatments like physiotherapy, medications and injections have stopped helping, shoulder replacement surgery could give you your life back. It is one of the most effective orthopaedic procedures for relieving pain and restoring arm function.

Shoulder replacement (also called shoulder arthroplasty) replaces the damaged surfaces of the ball (humeral head) and socket (glenoid) of your shoulder joint with metal and high-grade plastic components. When the cartilage that normally cushions these surfaces has worn away entirely, you are left with bone-on-bone friction that causes severe pain and stiffness. The surgery eliminates that friction and restores smooth joint movement.

In Bangalore, shoulder replacement is performed at JCI-accredited hospitals by fellowship-trained shoulder surgeons using the same FDA-approved implants available in the United States and Europe. Both anatomic (standard) and reverse shoulder replacement techniques are offered, depending on the integrity of your rotator cuff. The total package costs 80–85% less than in the United States, and patients who face waiting lists of 12–18 months at home can often be scheduled within 1–2 weeks.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Shoulder replacement may be recommended if you have exhausted conservative treatment options and continue to experience significant shoulder pain and functional limitation. The most common indication is end-stage osteoarthritis, where the protective cartilage has worn away entirely. You may also benefit if you have rheumatoid arthritis, avascular necrosis of the humeral head, or post-traumatic arthritis following a previous shoulder injury.

You may be a suitable candidate if you experience persistent shoulder pain that interferes with sleep, limits your ability to reach overhead, wash or dress yourself, or prevents you from participating in activities you once enjoyed. If you have sustained a complex proximal humerus fracture — often from a fall — you may also be a candidate, particularly for reverse shoulder replacement when the fracture cannot be reliably fixed with plates and screws.

You must be in reasonably good general health to tolerate anaesthesia and participate in post-operative rehabilitation. Conditions such as severe heart disease, uncontrolled diabetes, active infections or significant obesity may need to be addressed before surgery.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Once your surgery date is confirmed, you will receive a detailed pre-operative checklist. This typically includes blood tests, an ECG and dedicated imaging of your affected shoulder — X-rays and an MRI are standard, and a CT scan may be requested for 3D templating of implant size. If you have existing medical conditions, you may need additional clearance.

In the weeks leading up to surgery, focus on optimising your physical condition. If you smoke, it is strongly advised to stop at least four weeks before surgery, as smoking impairs wound healing and increases the risk of infection. Blood-thinning medications such as aspirin, clopidogrel or NSAIDs may need to be stopped 5–7 days before surgery. Herbal supplements such as garlic, ginkgo biloba, fish oil and vitamin E can also increase bleeding and should be discontinued.

## Procedure Details

Your surgery is performed under general anaesthesia, often combined with a regional nerve block that provides targeted pain relief to your shoulder and arm for 12–18 hours afterwards. The surgeon makes an incision over the front of your shoulder, separates the deltoid and pectoralis muscles, and accesses the joint. The damaged cartilage and bone are removed from the humeral head and glenoid.

In an anatomic replacement, a metal ball on a stem is inserted into your humerus and a plastic socket is cemented or press-fit into your glenoid. In a reverse replacement, the geometry is inverted — a metal socket is fixed to your glenoid and a plastic ball is attached to your humeral stem — allowing your deltoid muscle to compensate for a deficient rotator cuff. This design is particularly beneficial if you are elderly with rotator cuff arthropathy or have a complex proximal humerus fracture. After implantation, the joint is tested for stability and range of motion before the wound is closed in layers.

Post-operatively, your arm is immobilised in a sling. Passive range-of-motion exercises begin on day one under the guidance of a physiotherapist. Active movements are introduced gradually over the following weeks. Most patients are discharged within 2–4 days with a structured home exercise programme. Full recovery typically takes 3–6 months.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the morning of your surgery, you will be admitted and taken to the pre-operative holding area. The anaesthesiologist will review your medical history, place an intravenous line and administer the chosen anaesthetic. General anaesthesia is standard, often combined with an interscalene nerve block for post-operative pain relief.

Once you are anaesthetised, the surgical team positions you in a semi-upright (beach-chair) position. The surgeon makes an incision over the front of your shoulder and carefully exposes the joint. The damaged cartilage and bone are removed using precision cutting guides, and the prosthetic components are implanted. After implantation, the surgeon tests the joint through a full range of motion, checking for stability and balanced soft-tissue tension. The wound is closed in layers with absorbable sutures. The entire procedure typically takes 90–120 minutes.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

**Will it hurt?** Pain management is a priority. A multimodal analgesia protocol combines the interscalene nerve block with oral medications, keeping pain well-controlled while minimising side effects. You will have some discomfort, but it is very manageable and gradually subsides over the first week.

Your arm will be immobilised in a sling, and passive range-of-motion exercises begin on the first post-operative day. Pendulum exercises and gentle assisted movements are introduced progressively. Over the next 2–4 days, you will learn how to care for your sling, perform basic daily activities with one hand, and execute a structured home exercise programme.

After discharge, you will continue supervised physiotherapy for 1–2 weeks in Bangalore. A final wound review and X-ray are performed before you receive your medical fitness-to-fly certificate, typically 10–14 days after surgery. The sling is usually discontinued by week 4–6, and full recovery with restored strength and mobility takes 3–6 months.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

Shoulder replacement is a highly successful procedure, with patient satisfaction rates of 90–95% in large registry studies. The vast majority of patients experience dramatic pain relief and significant improvement in shoulder function and quality of life. Most can dress independently, reach overhead and perform daily activities without assistance within 3–6 months.

Modern shoulder implants have a proven survival rate of 90–95% at 10 years and 85–90% at 15 years. Implant longevity depends on your activity level, body weight, implant alignment and the integrity of surrounding soft tissues. You should avoid heavy lifting, repetitive overhead activities and contact sports, which can accelerate implant wear. Recreational activities such as swimming, walking, golf and light gardening are encouraged and fully compatible with a shoulder replacement.

## Alternatives to Consider

Before committing to shoulder replacement, you may explore conservative and less invasive alternatives, particularly if your symptoms are moderate. These include activity modification, physiotherapy to strengthen the rotator cuff and scapular stabilisers, and analgesic or anti-inflammatory medications. Intra-articular injections of corticosteroids can provide temporary relief, while hyaluronic acid injections may lubricate the joint. PRP injections are increasingly used for mild to moderate tendon and cartilage conditions.

For certain conditions, arthroscopic procedures may be appropriate — keyhole surgery can address rotator cuff tears, labral tears, impingement syndrome and loose bodies without replacing the joint. However, once arthritis is advanced with complete cartilage loss and bone-on-bone contact, shoulder replacement remains the most reliable and durable solution. Your surgeon will recommend the best approach based on your MRI findings, clinical examination, age and activity level.
