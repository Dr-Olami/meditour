## Overview

If you're reading this, you've probably been living with knee pain for months or even years. Simple things like walking to the shops, climbing stairs, or sleeping through the night have become difficult. You may have tried painkillers, physiotherapy, and injections, only to find the relief was temporary. You're likely wondering if knee replacement surgery is the right choice—and whether travelling to India for it is safe and worth it.

Total knee replacement is one of the most successful operations in all of medicine. It works by replacing the worn-out surfaces of your knee joint—the bottom of your thigh bone, the top of your shin bone, and the back of your kneecap—with metal and high-grade plastic components. When the cartilage that normally cushions your knee wears away (most often due to osteoarthritis, but also rheumatoid arthritis or a previous injury), the bones rub directly against each other. That bone-on-bone contact is what causes the pain, stiffness, and swelling you've been experiencing. The new implant acts as a smooth bearing surface, eliminating that friction so you can walk, climb, and rest comfortably again.

In Bangalore, this surgery is performed at JCI- and NABH-accredited hospitals by surgeons trained at leading institutions in the US, UK, and Germany. They use the same FDA-approved implants from manufacturers like Zimmer Biomet, Stryker, and DePuy that you'd receive in a Western hospital. The total package—including the implant, your hospital stay, and initial physiotherapy—typically costs 70-80% less than in the United States, making high-quality knee replacement accessible if you're facing long waiting lists or prohibitive costs at home.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

This surgery is for you if you've exhausted conservative treatments and your knee pain is still interfering with your life. The most common reason is end-stage osteoarthritis, where the protective cartilage in your knee has worn away entirely, causing bone-on-bone friction with every step. You may also benefit if you have rheumatoid arthritis, post-traumatic arthritis following a previous knee injury, or avascular necrosis.

You're likely a good candidate if your knee pain stops you from sleeping through the night, limits your walking to a few blocks, makes stair-climbing a struggle, or keeps you from activities you once enjoyed. If you've noticed your legs becoming bowed or knock-kneed over time, the implant can also correct that alignment and straighten your leg.

You do need to be in reasonably good general health to tolerate anaesthesia and take part in rehabilitation afterwards. If you have conditions like severe heart disease, uncontrolled diabetes, active infections, or significant obesity, your surgeon may ask you to address these before surgery. Don't worry—your Bangalore surgical team will assess everything thoroughly and give you an honest opinion about whether this is the right step for you.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Once your surgery date is confirmed, you'll receive a detailed pre-operative checklist. This typically includes blood tests, an ECG and echocardiogram to check your heart, a chest X-ray, and dedicated knee X-rays or CT scans to plan your implant size and alignment. If you have existing conditions like diabetes, high blood pressure, or heart disease, you may need additional clearance from your own doctor before travelling.

In the weeks leading up to surgery, try to get yourself in the best possible shape. Losing even a few kilograms can reduce stress on your new joint and make rehabilitation easier. Gentle strengthening exercises for your thigh and calf muscles can speed up recovery. If you smoke, please try to stop at least four weeks before surgery—smoking impairs wound healing and increases your risk of infection and blood clots.

You'll need to review certain medications with your surgeon. Blood-thinning medications such as aspirin, clopidogrel, warfarin, or NSAIDs may need to be stopped 5-7 days before surgery to reduce bleeding risk. Herbal supplements can also increase bleeding and should be discontinued. Always consult your surgeon before stopping any prescribed medication. On the day before surgery, you'll be asked to fast for at least 8 hours and to shower with an antiseptic solution.

## Procedure Details

Your surgery will be performed under spinal or general anaesthesia and takes about 60-90 minutes. Your surgeon makes an incision of 15-20 centimetres over the front of your knee, gently moves your kneecap aside, and removes the damaged cartilage along with a small amount of bone from your thigh and shin bones. Precision cuts are made using either mechanical guides or, increasingly, computer navigation and robotic-assisted systems that improve alignment accuracy to within one degree—this reduces the risk of uneven wear and early implant failure.

Three components are then fitted: a metal piece for your thigh bone, a metal baseplate with a plastic insert for your shin bone, and a plastic button for your kneecap. These are fixed to your bone using bone cement (the most common method, which gives immediate stability) or a cementless surface that allows your bone to grow into the implant over several weeks. Once everything is in place, your surgeon moves your knee through its full range of motion to check that it's stable and balanced. The wound is closed in layers with absorbable sutures, a small drain is placed to prevent fluid build-up, and a sterile compression dressing is applied.

You'll be encouraged to start moving on the same day or the next morning with a walker. Most people achieve independent walking within 3-5 days and go home with a detailed exercise programme. Full recovery typically takes 6-12 weeks, though you'll notice a dramatic reduction in pain almost immediately.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the morning of your surgery, you'll be admitted and taken to the pre-operative holding area. Your anaesthesiologist will place an intravenous line and give you your anaesthetic—typically a spinal block that numbs you from the waist down, or a general anaesthetic. Many surgeons also use a nerve block around your knee for extended pain relief for 12-18 hours after surgery.

Once you're anaesthetised, the surgical team prepares your knee with an antiseptic solution and drapes your leg in sterile sheets. Your surgeon makes the incision, exposes the joint, and uses precision cutting guides to remove the damaged cartilage and bone. The metal components are fixed to the prepared bone surfaces, a plastic insert is locked into the tibial baseplate, and your surgeon tests the knee through a full range of motion. The wound is closed in layers with absorbable sutures, a drain is placed, and a sterile dressing is applied. The entire procedure takes 60-90 minutes, and you'll be back in your private room within 1-2 hours.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

Will it hurt? That's one of the most common questions, and the honest answer is: you'll have some discomfort, but it's well managed. Your team uses a multimodal pain relief approach—combining the nerve block, oral medications like paracetamol and NSAIDs, and mild opioids as needed. This keeps pain controlled while minimising side effects, so you can actively participate in your rehabilitation.

You'll be encouraged to start moving on the day of surgery or the following morning. A physiotherapist will help you stand and walk with a walker, and a continuous passive motion (CPM) machine may gently bend and straighten your knee while you rest. Over the next 3-5 days, you'll progress to walking longer distances, climbing stairs, and achieving at least 90 degrees of knee bend. The nursing team monitors your wound and gives daily blood-thinning injections to prevent clots. Most people go home on day 3-5, once they can walk independently with a walker and manage pain with oral medications.

After discharge, you'll continue supervised physiotherapy at your accommodation or an outpatient clinic in Bangalore for 1-2 weeks. The focus shifts to achieving full knee extension, increasing your bend beyond 100 degrees, strengthening your muscles, and normalising your walking pattern. A final wound review and X-ray are performed before you receive your medical fitness-to-fly certificate, typically 10-14 days after surgery.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

Total knee replacement is one of the most successful operations in all of medicine, with satisfaction rates exceeding 90% in large studies. The vast majority of people experience dramatic pain relief and significant improvement in mobility, function, and quality of life. Most can walk without a limp, climb stairs, get in and out of cars, and resume daily activities without assistance within 6-12 weeks.

Modern knee implants have a proven survival rate of 90-95% at 15 years and 80-85% at 20 years, meaning the majority of people will never need revision surgery. Implant longevity depends on your activity level, body weight, and implant alignment. You'll be advised to avoid high-impact activities like running and jumping, which can accelerate wear. Low-impact activities like walking, swimming, cycling, and golf are encouraged and fully compatible with your knee replacement.

It's important to know that a knee replacement feels different from a natural knee—some people notice slight warmth, mild stiffness, or occasional clicking, which are all normal. Full recovery of strength and endurance can take 6-12 months. Sticking with your physiotherapy programme and maintaining a healthy weight are the best ways to ensure long-term success.

## Alternatives to Consider

Before committing to total knee replacement, it's worth exploring whether less invasive options might work for you, especially if your symptoms are moderate rather than severe. These include modifying your activities, losing weight, physiotherapy to strengthen the muscles supporting your knee, and pain-relieving or anti-inflammatory medications. Injections of corticosteroids can temporarily reduce inflammation, while hyaluronic acid injections may lubricate the joint and ease pain for several months.

If your arthritis is limited to just one part of your knee (typically the inner side), a partial (unicompartmental) knee replacement may be an option. This preserves the undamaged portions of your knee, meaning a smaller incision, faster recovery, and more natural knee movement. However, it's only suitable for a specific pattern of arthritis with intact ligaments—your surgeon will tell you if you qualify.

Arthroscopic procedures like meniscal trimming or cartilage clean-up can address specific problems within the knee, but they aren't effective for end-stage arthritis. Once arthritis is widespread and advanced, total knee replacement remains the most reliable and durable solution for lasting pain relief and restored mobility. Your Bangalore surgeon will give you an honest assessment of all your options so you can make the decision that's right for you.
