## Overview

Total Knee Replacement (TKR), also known as total knee arthroplasty, is a surgical procedure that replaces the damaged surfaces of the thigh bone (femur), shin bone (tibia), and kneecap (patella) with metal and high-grade plastic components. It is one of the most successful and commonly performed orthopaedic procedures worldwide, offering dramatic pain relief and functional restoration for patients with end-stage knee arthritis.

In Bangalore, total knee replacement is performed at JCI- and NABH-accredited hospitals by fellowship-trained joint replacement surgeons using the same FDA-approved implants available in the United States and Europe. The combination of world-class surgical expertise, advanced operation theatres with laminar airflow, and computer-assisted or robotic-assisted techniques ensures outcomes comparable to leading Western centres—at a fraction of the cost.

For international patients, Bangalore offers a compelling medical tourism destination: English-speaking medical teams, dedicated international patient desks, streamlined visa processing, and a temperate climate conducive to post-operative recovery. The total package, including implant, hospital stay, and initial physiotherapy, typically costs 70-80% less than in the United States.

## Procedure Details

The surgery is performed under spinal or general anaesthesia and takes approximately 60-90 minutes. The surgeon makes an incision over the front of the knee, moves the kneecap aside, and removes the damaged cartilage and a small amount of bone from the femur and tibia. Precision cuts are made using jigs or computer navigation to ensure correct implant alignment and leg axis.

The femoral component (metal), tibial component (metal baseplate with a plastic insert), and patellar component (plastic button) are then fixed to the bone using bone cement (cemented technique) or a biologic bonding surface (cementless technique). The choice depends on bone quality, patient age, and surgeon preference. After implantation, the knee is taken through a full range of motion to verify stability and tracking before the wound is closed in layers.

Post-operatively, patients begin mobilisation on the same day or the next morning with a walker. Continuous passive motion and supervised physiotherapy continue throughout the hospital stay. Most patients achieve independent walking with a walker within 3-5 days and are discharged with a detailed home exercise programme. Full recovery, including return to low-impact activities, typically takes 6-12 weeks.
