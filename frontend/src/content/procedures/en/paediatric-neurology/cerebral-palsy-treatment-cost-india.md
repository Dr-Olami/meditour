## Overview

A cerebral palsy diagnosis for your child brings many questions about the future. Cerebral palsy is the most common motor disability of childhood, affecting roughly 2-3 per 1,000 live births worldwide. It is caused by damage to the developing brain, typically before or around birth, and affects muscle tone, movement, and posture. The brain injury itself is permanent, but with the right treatment, children with cerebral palsy can make remarkable progress in mobility, independence, and quality of life.

Cerebral palsy treatment is not a single surgery or one-time intervention — it is a comprehensive, multidisciplinary approach that evolves with your child. In Bangalore, our partner hospitals bring together paediatric neurologists, orthopaedic surgeons, neurosurgeons, physiotherapists, occupational therapists, speech and language therapists, developmental specialists, and rehabilitation nurses into a single coordinated team. Your child receives every aspect of care under one roof, with each specialist contributing to a unified plan tailored to their type of cerebral palsy, abilities, and your family's goals.

International families from Bangladesh, the Gulf, Africa, and beyond choose Bangalore for the combination of internationally trained specialists, child-friendly rehabilitation facilities, and costs up to 80% lower than Western countries. Centres are equipped with advanced gait analysis laboratories, robotic-assisted therapy devices, and dedicated paediatric therapy spaces. ## Who Is This Procedure For?

Cerebral palsy treatment is for children and adolescents diagnosed with any type — spastic, dyskinetic, ataxic, or mixed — who need a structured, multidisciplinary management programme. If your child has spasticity limiting movement, muscle contractures causing pain or deformity, difficulty with speech or swallowing, or developmental delays needing targeted intervention, a comprehensive programme can help.

## Pre-operative Preparation

Before any intervention, your child undergoes a thorough multidisciplinary assessment: a detailed neurological examination, review of medical history and previous imaging, and functional assessment by the physiotherapy and occupational therapy teams. If surgery is being considered, additional tests may include gait analysis, hip X-rays to check for subluxation or dislocation, and an MRI of the brain and spine to plan the surgical approach.

For selective dorsal rhizotomy candidates, a careful selection process assesses your child's selective motor control, underlying strength, and spasticity distribution to determine whether SDR will benefit them. This is a decision made together with you, explained in plain language.

If botulinum toxin injections are planned, the team identifies the specific muscles contributing to spasticity and plans injection sites. For orthopaedic surgery, the surgeon explains which contractures or deformities need correction and how surgery will improve function. Throughout preparation, you can ask questions, understand expected outcomes, and participate in shared decision-making.

## Procedure Details

Cerebral palsy treatment encompasses several complementary modalities, personalised based on your child's type, severity, age, and functional goals.

**Physiotherapy** is the cornerstone. Specialised physiotherapists use stretching, strengthening, gait training, balance exercises, and functional task practice to improve mobility and prevent contractures. Sessions are engaging and age-appropriate, often incorporating play-based therapy for younger children.

**Occupational therapy** develops daily living skills — dressing, feeding, writing, playing — and assesses your child for assistive devices and home adaptations to maximise independence.

**Speech and language therapy** addresses communication challenges and feeding or swallowing difficulties common in cerebral palsy, particularly dyskinetic or mixed types. Therapists work on articulation, language development, and alternative communication methods where needed.

**Medications** manage spasticity. Oral antispasticity drugs (baclofen, diazepam, tizanidine) reduce muscle tone systemically. For focal spasticity, botulinum toxin injections provide targeted relief lasting 3-6 months. In severe, generalised spasticity, a baclofen pump may be surgically implanted to deliver medication directly into the spinal fluid.

**Selective dorsal rhizotomy (SDR)** is a neurosurgical procedure for spastic diplegia. The neurosurgeon exposes the nerve rootlets in the lower spinal cord and selectively cuts those contributing to abnormal spasticity, preserving those carrying normal signals. This can permanently reduce leg spasticity and improve gait, but requires intensive post-operative rehabilitation.

**Orthopaedic surgery** addresses contractures, joint deformities, and hip instability. Procedures may include tendon lengthening, joint reconstructions, and hip surgeries. These are often performed as single-event multilevel surgery, correcting multiple deformities in one session to minimise anaesthetics and recovery periods.

## What Happens During the Procedure

For therapy-based programmes, your child attends daily sessions with the physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and speech therapy teams. Each session targets specific functional goals using hands-on techniques, exercises, and play-based activities. Progress is monitored continuously and the programme adjusted as your child responds.

For botulinum toxin injections, the procedure is performed under mild sedation or local anaesthetic. Using ultrasound guidance, the specialist injects precise doses into targeted muscles. The procedure takes 30-60 minutes, and your child can usually go home the same day. Full effect develops over 1-2 weeks, with therapy intensified during this period.

For selective dorsal rhizotomy, your child is given general anaesthesia. The neurosurgeon makes a small incision in the lower back, exposes the conus medullaris and cauda equina, and uses intraoperative electrophysiological monitoring to identify which nerve rootlets carry abnormal signals. These are selectively divided while preserving normal function. The procedure takes 3-4 hours, followed by 1-2 days in the paediatric ICU.

For orthopaedic surgery, specific procedures depend on your child's deformities. Tendon lengthening, joint capsule releases, and bone osteotomies may be performed on multiple levels in a single session. Intraoperative imaging confirms corrections, and casts or splints are applied. Surgery typically takes 2-4 hours, followed by ward-based recovery and early mobilisation.

## Post-operative Care & Recovery

After surgery, your child is closely monitored in the paediatric ICU or ward. Pain management is a priority, and early mobilisation begins as soon as it is safe, with physiotherapists guiding gentle movements to prevent stiffness and promote healing.

For SDR patients, intensive rehabilitation begins within days. Your child works with physiotherapists daily to rebuild strength, learn new movement patterns, and progress from lying to sitting to standing to walking. This continues throughout your stay and transitions to a home programme before you leave.

For orthopaedic surgery patients, casts are typically maintained for 4-6 weeks, with therapy focusing on maintaining range in uncasted joints. Once casts are removed, progressive weight-bearing and gait training begin.

The multidisciplinary team meets regularly to review progress and adjust the programme. You are involved in every decision and taught the techniques needed to continue therapy at home.

## Long-term Outcomes & Success Rates

With comprehensive, early, and consistent intervention, children with cerebral palsy can achieve significant improvements in mobility, function, and quality of life. SDR with appropriate rehabilitation shows lasting spasticity reduction, improved gait, and increased independence in 80-90% of well-selected cases. Orthopaedic surgery successfully corrects contractures and deformities in the vast majority of patients.

However, realistic expectations matter. Cerebral palsy is a lifelong condition, and treatment does not remove the underlying brain injury. The goal is to maximise your child's potential, and every child's trajectory is different. Your team will be honest about what treatment can and cannot achieve for your child's specific type and severity.

Long-term success depends heavily on continued therapy at home, regular follow-up, and ongoing developmental support. Children who receive consistent therapy and family support show the best outcomes over time.

Tele-consultation follow-up with your Bangalore team is available at 3 months, 6 months, and annually thereafter. This allows the team to monitor progress, adjust the home programme, and plan future interventions such as repeat botulinum toxin injections or follow-up surgery as your child grows.

If your child develops new symptoms — increasing pain, hip problems, or regression in mobility — contact your local paediatrician and arrange a tele-consultation with your Bangalore team. Early intervention prevents minor issues from becoming major problems.

## Alternatives to Consider

Not every child with cerebral palsy needs surgery. For many, a well-structured therapy and medication programme provides meaningful improvement without surgical risks and recovery. If spasticity is well managed with botulinum toxin injections and oral medications, and function is progressing satisfactorily with therapy alone, surgery may not be necessary.

Stem cell therapy is sometimes promoted for cerebral palsy, but evidence for its effectiveness remains limited and it is not a standard recommended treatment. Your Bangalore team will provide an honest, evidence-based assessment of any alternative therapies you are considering.

For families unable to travel for an extended period, a shorter initial assessment visit can be arranged with a detailed home programme and tele-consultation follow-up — allowing you to benefit from expert assessment without committing to a long stay upfront.
