## Overview

If you or your child has been diagnosed with severe to profound sensorineural hearing loss and conventional hearing aids no longer provide meaningful benefit, you are likely exploring every available option. Being told that hearing aids are no longer enough can feel overwhelming, especially when the path forward involves surgery and a device you may not fully understand. The encouraging news is that cochlear implants have transformed the lives of hundreds of thousands of people worldwide, and the technology available in India is identical to what you would find in the most advanced Western hospitals.

A cochlear implant is an electronic medical device that bypasses the damaged hair cells in your inner ear (cochlea) and directly stimulates the auditory nerve. Unlike a hearing aid, which simply amplifies sound, a cochlear implant converts sound into electrical signals that your brain interprets as hearing. The system has two parts: an internal component that is surgically implanted under the skin behind your ear, and an external sound processor that you wear like a behind-the-ear device. Together, they restore a sense of hearing to people who would otherwise live in silence.

Cochlear implantation is suitable for both children and adults. In children born with profound hearing loss, early implantation—ideally before the age of two—gives the best outcomes because the brain's auditory pathways are most adaptable during early development. In adults who lost their hearing after learning to speak (post-lingual deafness), cochlear implants can restore the ability to converse, use the telephone, and enjoy music. Bangalore's ENT surgeons work alongside audiologists, speech therapists, and rehabilitation specialists to provide a complete care pathway from evaluation through long-term follow-up.

For international patients, Bangalore offers a compelling combination: world-class surgical expertise, the latest implant technology from global manufacturers, and costs that are up to 70% lower than in Western countries. Dedicated international patient teams coordinate every step of your journey, from visa assistance to post-activation rehabilitation planning.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Cochlear implantation may be right for you or your child if you have severe to profound sensorineural hearing loss in both ears and receive limited or no benefit from appropriately fitted conventional hearing aids. In adults, this typically means speech understanding scores of 50% or less in the ear to be implanted and 60% or less in the better ear, using standard sentence tests in a quiet environment. If you find yourself increasingly isolated in conversations, relying heavily on lip-reading, or avoiding social situations because you cannot follow what is being said, a cochlear implant could significantly improve your quality of life.

For children, the criteria are different and the timing is critical. Congenital hearing loss is now routinely detected through newborn hearing screening programmes. If a child is diagnosed with profound hearing loss and does not develop adequate speech and language with hearing aids by around 12 to 18 months of age, cochlear implantation is strongly recommended. The earlier a child receives an implant, the better their chances of developing natural speech, attending mainstream schools, and integrating fully with hearing peers. Children who are implanted before age two consistently achieve the best language outcomes.

You must also have a functioning auditory nerve for the implant to work. This is confirmed through imaging (CT and MRI of the temporal bone) and electrophysiological testing. Certain conditions, such as cochlear ossification (bone growth inside the cochlea) following meningitis or the absence of the auditory nerve, may require special surgical techniques or make implantation less straightforward. Your ENT surgeon will assess all of these factors during the evaluation process.

Equally important is your commitment to the rehabilitation process. A cochlear implant is not a cure that works instantly on the day of surgery. After the device is activated, you or your child will need months of auditory training and speech therapy to learn how to interpret the new signals. Without this commitment, the outcomes will be disappointing. Your team in Bangalore will help you understand what to expect and connect you with rehabilitation resources, both locally and in your home country.

## Pre-operative Preparation

Before you travel to Bangalore, share all your relevant medical records with your treating ENT surgeon for advance review. This includes audiograms, hearing aid trial reports, speech perception test results, any prior imaging (CT or MRI of the temporal bone), and a complete medical history. If you are travelling for a child, include newborn hearing screening results, developmental assessments, and records from any speech therapy already undertaken. Be sure to disclose all medications and supplements, and inform the team of any prior ear infections or surgeries.

Once you arrive in Bangalore, you will undergo a comprehensive pre-operative evaluation. This includes a detailed audiological assessment to confirm the degree of hearing loss and the benefit (or lack thereof) from hearing aids. Imaging studies—typically a high-resolution CT scan and an MRI of the temporal bone—are performed or reviewed to evaluate the anatomy of your cochlea, confirm the presence and integrity of the auditory nerve, and rule out conditions such as cochlear ossification or inner ear malformations that could affect the surgical approach.

You will also have blood tests, an electrocardiogram, and a general medical fitness assessment. For children, a paediatric evaluation ensures they are fit for general anaesthesia. Vaccinations against pneumococcus and meningitis are strongly recommended before cochlear implant surgery, as implant recipients have a slightly elevated risk of meningitis. Your surgeon will explain the planned procedure, the specific implant device to be used, the potential risks, and the expected timeline to device activation, and will ask for your informed consent. You will be asked to fast for 6 to 8 hours before surgery.

## Procedure Details

Your cochlear implant surgery takes place in a fully equipped operating theatre under general anaesthesia. The procedure typically takes 2 to 3 hours, though complex cases may take longer. Bangalore's ENT surgical teams use advanced microsurgical techniques and intraoperative monitoring to maximise safety and precision. The internal component of the implant—the receiver-stimulator package and the electrode array—is placed entirely under the skin, leaving no visible hardware after healing.

The surgery begins with an incision behind the ear, and a small area of mastoid bone is removed to create a pathway to the inner ear. A delicate opening is made into the cochlea (cochleostomy or via the round window), and the thin, flexible electrode array is carefully threaded into the spiral of the cochlea. The receiver-stimulator is placed in a shallow bed created in the skull bone behind the ear, and the incision is closed with dissolvable sutures. Intraoperative electrical testing confirms that the implant is functioning correctly and that the auditory nerve is responding to stimulation before the surgery is completed.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of your surgery, you will be taken to the operating theatre and given a general anaesthetic so you are completely asleep and feel no pain. The surgical team will shave a small area of hair behind the ear to be implanted, clean the skin with antiseptic, and drape the area to maintain a sterile field. Facial nerve monitoring equipment is used throughout the procedure to protect the facial nerve, which runs close to the surgical path.

Your surgeon makes an incision behind the ear and performs a mastoidectomy—removing a small amount of spongy bone to access the middle ear and cochlea. Using an operating microscope or endoscope, the surgeon identifies the round window or creates a cochleostomy, a tiny opening into the cochlea. The electrode array is then gently inserted into the scala tympani of the cochlea. Great care is taken to insert the array fully without damaging the delicate intracochlear structures, as preserving residual hearing can improve outcomes with modern implants.

The receiver-stimulator is positioned in a recess created in the temporal bone and secured in place. Intraoperative impedance testing and, in some cases, electrically evoked compound action potential (ECAP) measurements are performed to confirm the device is working and the auditory nerve is responding. Once everything is confirmed, the incision is closed in layers with dissolvable sutures, and a sterile bandage is applied around the head. You will wake up in the recovery room and be transferred to your hospital room once you are fully alert.

## Post-operative Care & Recovery

After surgery, you will be monitored in your hospital room for one to two days. The head bandage remains in place overnight to reduce swelling and protect the surgical site. You may experience some pain, which is managed with prescribed medication, and mild dizziness or nausea is not uncommon in the first 24 hours. Most patients are up and walking by the day after surgery. Before discharge, your surgeon will check the incision, review your recovery, and provide detailed wound care instructions.

Once you return to your accommodation, you will continue to heal over the next two to three weeks. The incision behind the ear will gradually close and any swelling or bruising will subside. You should avoid strenuous activity, heavy lifting, and contact sports during this period. Keep the incision site clean and dry, and follow your surgeon's instructions regarding bathing. Mild popping sounds or sensations in the ear are normal as healing progresses.

The external sound processor is not fitted immediately. Activation is typically scheduled for three to four weeks after surgery, once the incision has fully healed and any swelling has resolved. This waiting period can feel long, especially for parents of young children, but it is essential to allow the internal implant to settle properly before the external components are attached.

## Long-term Outcomes & Success Rates

Cochlear implantation has consistently high success rates, with over 95% of surgeries completed without major complications in experienced centres. Bangalore's ENT teams achieve outcomes consistent with international benchmarks, thanks to high surgical volumes and access to the latest implant technology from manufacturers such as Cochlear, MED-EL, and Advanced Bionics. Most recipients experience a dramatic improvement in hearing compared to their pre-implant state with hearing aids.

For adults with post-lingual hearing loss, the majority can understand conversation without lip-reading within a few months of activation, and many can use telephones and enjoy music. The pace of improvement depends on how long you were deaf, the age at which you lost your hearing, and your commitment to rehabilitation. For children implanted early, the outcomes are remarkable: most develop spoken language on par with their hearing peers and attend mainstream schools. Studies consistently show that early-implanted children achieve significantly better language, academic, and social outcomes than those implanted later.

It is important to understand that hearing through a cochlear implant is not the same as natural hearing, especially in the early weeks after activation. Sounds may seem mechanical or high-pitched at first. However, with consistent use of the device and regular auditory rehabilitation, the brain adapts and sounds become increasingly natural over time. Long-term success depends on wearing the device consistently, attending mapping sessions, and engaging actively in speech therapy.

Regular mapping sessions—typically every few months in the first year and annually thereafter—ensure the device is programmed optimally as your brain adapts and your hearing needs change. For children, intensive auditory-verbal therapy is essential and should continue for several years. If you experience any sudden loss of sound, pain, swelling, or signs of infection at the implant site, contact your audiologist or ENT surgeon immediately. ## Alternatives to Consider

If your hearing loss is moderate to severe but you still derive meaningful benefit from conventional hearing aids, upgrading to advanced digital hearing aids or bone-anchored hearing devices may be a reasonable first step before considering cochlear implantation. Modern hearing aids with directional microphones, noise reduction algorithms, and Bluetooth connectivity can significantly improve communication in many environments. Your audiologist can help you determine whether a more powerful hearing aid trial is worthwhile before proceeding to implant evaluation.

For people with single-sided deafness, where one ear has profound hearing loss but the other ear functions normally, a cochlear implant may be appropriate, but alternatives such as a CROS (contralateral routing of signals) hearing aid or a bone-anchored hearing system may also be considered. For individuals with auditory neuropathy spectrum disorder, where the auditory nerve function is atypical, outcomes with cochlear implants are more variable and a thorough evaluation is essential. Your ENT team in Bangalore will discuss all options and help you choose the path that best suits your specific hearing profile and lifestyle.
