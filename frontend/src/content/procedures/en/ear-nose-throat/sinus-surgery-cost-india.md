## Overview

If you have been living with chronic sinusitis, you know how exhausting it can be. The constant facial pressure, the blocked nose that never seems to clear, the headaches that radiate behind your eyes, the loss of your sense of smell, and the endless rounds of antibiotics that provide only temporary relief — all of these take a real toll on your quality of life. When medical treatment has been tried thoroughly and your symptoms persist, functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS) offers a proven, minimally invasive solution that addresses the underlying anatomical and inflammatory causes of your sinus disease.

FESS is a modern surgical technique that uses thin, rigid endoscopes equipped with high-definition cameras and specialised micro-instruments to open the natural drainage pathways of your sinuses. Unlike traditional sinus surgery, which required external incisions on the face, FESS is performed entirely through your nostrils — there are no external scars. The surgeon works within the natural openings of your sinuses, removing polyps, clearing infected tissue, and widening blocked passages to restore normal mucus drainage and airflow. By preserving as much healthy tissue as possible, FESS promotes faster healing and better long-term outcomes.

Chronic rhinosinusitis affects millions of people worldwide and is one of the most common reasons for ENT surgery. In many cases, it is driven by a combination of anatomical factors (such as a deviated septum or narrowed sinus openings) and inflammatory factors (such as allergies, nasal polyps, or immune dysfunction). FESS addresses the anatomical component, while ongoing medical management handles the inflammatory component. For international patients, Bangalore offers world-class ENT centres with the latest endoscopic technology, image-guided surgery systems, and experienced rhinologists — all at costs up to 80% lower than in Western countries.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Functional endoscopic sinus surgery may be right for you if you have chronic rhinosinusitis that has not responded to at least 12 weeks of appropriate medical treatment. This typically includes a course of broad-spectrum antibiotics, topical or oral corticosteroids, saline irrigation, and treatment of any underlying allergies. If you continue to experience facial pain, nasal obstruction, thick discharge, reduced sense of smell, or recurrent infections despite this treatment, your ENT surgeon will likely recommend surgery.

You may also be a candidate for FESS if you have nasal polyps — soft, painless, non-cancerous growths that develop in the lining of your sinuses or nasal passages. Polyps are common in people with chronic inflammation, asthma, aspirin sensitivity, or cystic fibrosis. When polyps are large enough to block your nasal passages or sinus openings, they cause the symptoms of chronic sinusitis. FESS removes the polyps and widens the sinus openings to reduce the likelihood of recurrence.

Other indications for FESS include recurrent acute sinusitis (four or more episodes per year), fungal sinusitis, sinus mucocele (a cyst-like swelling filled with mucus), and anatomical obstruction such as a concha bullosa (an air-filled middle turbinate) or Haller cells that narrow the sinus drainage pathways. If you have a deviated nasal septum contributing to your symptoms, a septoplasty can be performed at the same time as FESS to correct the alignment of your nasal septum and improve airflow.

It is important to understand that FESS is not a standalone cure. It is part of a comprehensive management plan that includes ongoing medical therapy. If you have allergies, asthma, or immune conditions, these must be managed in parallel with surgery to achieve the best long-term results. Your ENT team in Bangalore will coordinate with your local physician to ensure continuity of care after you return home.

## Pre-operative Preparation

Before you travel to Bangalore, share your relevant medical records with your treating ENT surgeon for advance review. This includes any prior CT scans of your sinuses, reports from nasal endoscopy, allergy test results, a list of medications you have tried, and your complete medical history. Be sure to disclose all medications, particularly blood-thinning medications, nasal steroid sprays, and any supplements, as some may need to be adjusted before surgery. If you have asthma, ensure it is well-controlled before travelling.

Once you arrive in Bangalore, you will undergo a comprehensive pre-operative evaluation. This begins with a detailed consultation with your ENT surgeon, who will take a full history and examine your nasal cavity using a rigid nasal endoscope. This in-office procedure allows the surgeon to directly visualise the inside of your nose and sinuses, assess the extent of polyps or inflammation, and identify any anatomical abnormalities. A high-resolution CT scan of your paranasal sinuses is performed (or reviewed if you bring one from home) to map the exact anatomy of your sinuses and plan the surgical approach.

You will also have blood tests and a general medical fitness assessment. If you are having general anaesthesia, you will be asked to fast for 6 to 8 hours before surgery. Your surgeon will explain the planned procedure, the specific sinuses to be opened, the potential risks, and the expected recovery timeline, and will ask for your informed consent. Many centres use computer-assisted image-guided surgery systems that link your CT scan to the endoscopic instruments in real time, providing the surgeon with precise navigational accuracy during the procedure.

## Procedure Details

Your sinus surgery takes place in a fully equipped operating theatre under general anaesthesia. The procedure typically takes 1 to 3 hours, depending on the extent of disease and the number of sinuses involved. Bangalore's ENT surgical teams use high-definition endoscopic systems with 4K cameras, microdebriders (powered instruments that simultaneously cut and suction tissue), and image-guided navigation to maximise precision and safety.

The surgery is performed entirely through your nostrils — there are no external incisions. The surgeon inserts the endoscope into your nasal cavity, which provides a magnified, illuminated view of the sinus anatomy on a high-definition monitor. Using micro-instruments and the microdebrider, the surgeon systematically opens the blocked sinus ostia (natural openings), removes nasal polyps, clears infected mucus and diseased mucosa, and widens the drainage pathways. If a deviated septum is contributing to obstruction, a septoplasty is performed at the same time. The goal is to restore natural sinus ventilation and drainage while preserving as much healthy tissue as possible.

## What Happens During the Procedure

On the day of your surgery, you will be taken to the operating theatre and given a general anaesthetic so you are completely asleep and feel no pain. The surgical team will apply a decongestant and local anaesthetic to the inside of your nose to reduce bleeding and improve visibility. The image-guidance system is calibrated to match your CT scan with your actual anatomy, providing real-time navigation throughout the procedure.

Your surgeon begins by inspecting the nasal cavity with the endoscope, identifying the key anatomical landmarks and the extent of disease. Using the microdebrider and other fine instruments, the surgeon carefully removes polyps and opens the natural sinus openings one by one — typically starting with the anterior sinuses (maxillary, anterior ethmoid, and frontal recess) and progressing to the posterior sinuses (posterior ethmoid and sphenoid) as needed. The image-guidance system confirms the position of instruments relative to critical structures such as the orbit (eye socket) and the skull base, minimising the risk of injury.

Throughout the procedure, bleeding is controlled with specialised bipolar forceps and topical haemostatic agents. Once all the targeted sinuses have been opened and cleared, the surgeon inspects the entire cavity to ensure adequate drainage and haemostasis. Dissolvable nasal packing or splints may be placed to support healing and reduce bleeding. You will wake up in the recovery room and be transferred to your hospital room once you are fully alert.

## Post-operative Care & Recovery

After surgery, you will be monitored for a few hours before discharge. Most FESS procedures are day-care or overnight stays. You may have nasal packing or splints in place, which are typically removed within 1 to 2 days. Some bleeding from the nose is normal for the first 24 to 48 hours — you will be given a drip pad to absorb any discharge. Mild discomfort, nasal congestion, and a feeling of pressure in your face are common and are managed with prescribed pain medication.

Once you return to your accommodation, you will begin a saline irrigation protocol — rinsing your nasal passages with a saline solution several times a day to keep the sinus cavities clean and promote healing. Your surgeon will prescribe a topical steroid spray to reduce inflammation and prevent polyp recurrence. You should avoid strenuous activity, heavy lifting, bending forward, and blowing your nose for the first week. Keep your head elevated while sleeping to reduce swelling.

A follow-up endoscopic cleaning is performed in Bangalore before you fly home. This involves the surgeon using the endoscope to remove crusts, blood clots, and dried mucus from the sinus cavities — a critical step in ensuring the sinus openings remain patent. This procedure is typically done under local anaesthesia and is well tolerated. Your surgeon will review the healing progress and provide detailed instructions for continued care at home.

## Long-term Outcomes & Success Rates

FESS has a well-documented success rate of 80 to 90% for chronic rhinosinusitis when combined with appropriate post-operative medical management. Bangalore's ENT teams achieve outcomes consistent with international benchmarks, thanks to high surgical volumes, advanced endoscopic technology, and image-guided navigation. Most patients experience a dramatic improvement in nasal breathing, reduction in facial pain and pressure, restoration of smell, and a significant decrease in sinus infections.

The long-term success of FESS depends heavily on post-operative care. The sinus mucosa takes 4 to 6 weeks to fully heal, and during this time regular saline irrigation and topical steroid use are essential. For patients with nasal polyps, particularly those with asthma or aspirin sensitivity (Samter's triad), the recurrence rate is higher and long-term medical management is critical. Your surgeon may recommend biologic therapies (such as dupilumab) for severe, recurrent nasal polyposis that does not respond to conventional treatment.

It is important to have realistic expectations. FESS does not cure the underlying inflammatory tendency that caused your sinus disease — it removes the anatomical obstruction and creates the conditions for medical management to work. With proper ongoing care, the vast majority of patients enjoy years of symptom relief and a markedly improved quality of life. Regular follow-up with your local ENT specialist, including periodic nasal endoscopy, helps detect and treat any recurrence early.

## Alternatives to Consider

If your chronic sinusitis has not been treated with a full course of appropriate medical therapy, this should be attempted before considering surgery. A typical medical regimen includes 3 to 6 weeks of broad-spectrum antibiotics, a course of oral corticosteroids (for polyp disease), daily saline irrigation, and a topical intranasal steroid spray. For patients with allergic rhinitis, allergen avoidance and antihistamines are important. Many patients with mild to moderate disease achieve significant symptom relief with medical management alone and never need surgery.

For patients with nasal polyps who have not responded to oral steroids, biologic therapies such as dupilumab, mepolizumab, or omalizumab offer a non-surgical alternative. These targeted injectable medications reduce the inflammatory pathway responsible for polyp growth and have shown excellent results in clinical trials. However, they are expensive, require ongoing injections, and are not suitable for everyone. Your ENT team in Bangalore can advise whether biologic therapy is appropriate for your specific case.

If your primary problem is a deviated septum rather than sinus disease, a septoplasty alone — without FESS — may be sufficient to relieve your nasal obstruction. For patients with turbinate hypertrophy (enlarged nasal turbinates causing congestion), turbinate reduction procedures can improve airflow without extensive sinus surgery. Your surgeon will recommend the most appropriate procedure based on your CT scan, nasal endoscopy, and clinical symptoms.
