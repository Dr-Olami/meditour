## Overview

Multiple myeloma is a cancer of plasma cells in the bone marrow that leads to bone destruction, anaemia, kidney dysfunction and recurrent infections. Hearing the word "incurable" is frightening, and it is natural to feel overwhelmed when you or a loved one receives this diagnosis. The encouraging news is that modern treatment combinations have transformed myeloma into a highly manageable disease, with many patients living for years, even decades, with a good quality of life.

Treatment typically involves induction therapy with novel agents (proteasome inhibitors, immunomodulatory drugs and monoclonal antibodies), followed by an autologous stem cell transplant for eligible patients and long-term maintenance therapy. The goal is not a cure in the traditional sense, but a deep and lasting remission that lets you return to your normal life.

India has become a leading destination for myeloma care, with JCI-accredited hospitals in Bangalore offering the full spectrum of treatment from induction to transplant and maintenance. The country performs hundreds of myeloma transplants each year, supported by experienced haematology teams, advanced transplant units and access to all major novel agents.

International patients choose Bangalore for its high success rates, internationally trained haematologists and cost savings of 70–90% compared to Western pricing. The combination of clinical excellence, English-speaking staff and dedicated international patient services makes India an attractive option when you are facing long waiting lists or prohibitive costs at home.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

This treatment is for patients with confirmed symptomatic multiple myeloma — meaning the disease is causing problems such as bone pain, anaemia, raised calcium levels or kidney impairment, not just abnormal blood tests. If your doctor has recommended systemic therapy, you are a candidate for the induction phase. Whether you go on to a transplant depends on your age, fitness and how well the induction therapy works.

Transplant-eligible patients need adequate stem cell yield and organ function. Your cardiac, renal and hepatic function must be strong enough to tolerate the high-dose chemotherapy used in conditioning. Most programmes require a performance status of 0–2 on the ECOG scale, meaning you are reasonably active and able to care for yourself. Active uncontrolled infection at the time of admission will delay treatment until it is resolved.

Many patients worry that their age alone will disqualify them. In reality, the decision is based on overall fitness rather than a number. If you are older but active and without major comorbidities, you may still be eligible. Be open with your team about your medical history and your concerns, so they can plan your treatment safely and honestly.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Before you travel, your local haematologist should share your complete medical records with the Bangalore team, including pathology reports, imaging, previous treatment summaries and your current medication list. The hospital will review these and may request additional tests such as a PET-CT, bone marrow biopsy, echocardiogram and lung function tests, some of which you may be able to complete at home to save time.

Stop any medications your treating team advises, particularly blood thinners and immunosuppressive drugs, and ensure your vaccinations are up to date before the transplant, because your immune system will be weakened for several weeks afterwards. Arrange for a family member or close friend to accompany you, as you will need practical and emotional support during the hospital stay and the recovery weeks that follow. Financially, confirm the full package cost in writing, including what is covered and what might be billed separately, so there are no surprises later.

## Procedure Details

Multiple myeloma treatment follows a phased approach, and your doctor will tailor each phase to your specific disease and fitness. Induction therapy typically combines a proteasome inhibitor (bortezomib), an immunomodulatory drug (lenalidomide) and a steroid (dexamethasone) over 4–6 cycles to achieve a deep response. These are given mostly as outpatient injections and tablets, so you may not need to stay in hospital during this phase.

For transplant-eligible patients, stem cells are then mobilised and collected by apheresis, a process similar to dialysis that takes a few hours, and the cells are cryopreserved. You are then admitted for high-dose melphalan conditioning followed by reinfusion of your own stem cells. Engraftment, when your blood counts begin to recover, typically occurs within 10–14 days, during which you stay in a specialised transplant room with strict infection control measures.

After discharge, maintenance therapy with lenalidomide is continued long-term to prolong remission. For relapsed or refractory disease, additional options include daratumumab, carfilzomib, pomalidomide, bispecific antibodies and CAR-T cell therapy. The overall inpatient stay for a transplant spans 2–4 weeks, followed by several weeks of close outpatient monitoring and lifelong maintenance.

## What Happens During the Procedure

The transplant experience unfolds in stages. First, the stem cell collection: you sit in a reclining chair for a few hours while a machine draws blood, separates out your stem cells, and returns the rest to you. Most patients find this uncomfortable but not painful, similar to a long dialysis session.

Next comes a short rest period while your cells are frozen. When you are admitted for the transplant, you receive high-dose melphalan chemotherapy over one or two days. This is strong chemotherapy that wipes out your bone marrow to make way for the new cells, and it can cause nausea, mouth sores and fatigue. Your team will give you medications to manage these side effects.

A day or two later, your own stem cells are reinfused through an intravenous line, a process that takes only minutes and is painless. The critical period is the next 10–14 days, when your blood counts are very low and you are vulnerable to infection and bleeding. You will stay in a HEPA-filtered room with strict hygiene measures, receive blood product support as needed, and be monitored daily. Once your counts begin to rise, you start to feel better quickly.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

After discharge, you will stay in Bangalore for two to three weeks of outpatient monitoring. Your team will check your blood counts regularly, watch for infections, and start you on maintenance therapy with lenalidomide, which you will continue long-term. You will feel tired for the first few weeks, and it is important to rest, eat well, and avoid crowds and anyone who is unwell.

Many patients worry about pain. The reinfusion itself is not painful, and the discomfort comes mainly from the chemotherapy side effects — mouth sores, nausea and fatigue — all of which are managed with medication. You will need someone with you during this period, both for practical support and to recognise any warning signs early, such as fever or unusual bleeding.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

Leading Bangalore centres report progression-free survival rates of 60–80% at three years for transplant-eligible patients receiving induction, autologous transplant and maintenance, comparable to international benchmarks. Many patients return to work and normal activities within a few months of the transplant.

It is important to have realistic expectations. Myeloma is not considered curable, and most patients will relapse at some point, often years later. The good news is that each relapse brings new treatment options, and survival has improved dramatically over the past decade. Maintenance therapy helps keep the disease in check, and your haematology team will monitor you closely with regular blood tests and imaging to catch any changes early.

## Follow-Up Care After Returning Home

Once you are cleared to fly home, your Bangalore team will provide a detailed discharge summary and a follow-up plan for your local haematologist. You will need monthly blood tests for the first six months, continued maintenance therapy, and regular checks for disease response. Tele-consultations with your Bangalore doctor can be arranged if your local team has questions. Keep all your records together and share them promptly with your home physician.

## Alternatives to Consider

An autologous transplant is not the only path. Depending on your age, fitness and disease characteristics, alternatives include continued drug therapy without a transplant, a second autologous transplant, an allogeneic transplant using a donor's cells, or newer options such as bispecific antibodies and CAR-T cell therapy for relapsed disease. Some patients do well on combination drug regimens alone for years. Discuss all options openly with your haematologist, and consider a second opinion if you are unsure. The right choice depends on your disease, your fitness and your personal priorities.
