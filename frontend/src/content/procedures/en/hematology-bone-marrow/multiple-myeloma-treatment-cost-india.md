## Overview

Multiple myeloma is a cancer of plasma cells in the bone marrow that leads to bone destruction, anaemia, kidney dysfunction and recurrent infections. While it remains incurable, modern treatment combinations have transformed it into a highly manageable disease with prolonged survival. Treatment typically involves induction therapy with novel agents (proteasome inhibitors, immunomodulatory drugs and monoclonal antibodies), followed by autologous stem cell transplant for eligible patients and long-term maintenance therapy.

India has become a leading destination for myeloma care, with JCI-accredited hospitals in Bangalore offering the full spectrum of treatment from induction to transplant and maintenance. The country performs hundreds of myeloma transplants each year, supported by experienced haematology teams, advanced transplant units and access to all major novel agents.

International patients choose Bangalore for its high success rates, internationally trained haematologists and cost savings of 70–90% compared to Western pricing. The combination of clinical excellence, English-speaking staff and dedicated international patient services makes India an attractive option for patients facing long waiting lists or prohibitive costs at home.

## Procedure Details

Multiple myeloma treatment follows a phased approach. Induction therapy typically combines a proteasome inhibitor (bortezomib), an immunomodulatory drug (lenalidomide) and a steroid (dexamethasone) over 4–6 cycles to achieve a deep response. For transplant-eligible patients, stem cells are then mobilised and collected by apheresis and cryopreserved.

The patient is admitted for high-dose melphalan conditioning followed by reinfusion of their own stem cells. Engraftment typically occurs within 10–14 days, during which the patient stays in a specialised transplant room with strict infection control measures. After discharge, maintenance therapy with lenalidomide is continued long-term to prolong remission.

For relapsed or refractory disease, additional options include daratumumab, carfilzomib, pomalidomide, bispecific antibodies and CAR-T cell therapy. The overall inpatient stay for a transplant spans 2–4 weeks, followed by several weeks of close outpatient monitoring and lifelong maintenance.

## Travel & Visa

International patients travelling for multiple myeloma treatment require a Medical Visa (e-Medical or regular Medical Visa), which is granted to the patient and up to two attendants. The visa is typically valid for up to one year with multiple entries, allowing for pre-transplant assessment, the procedure itself and follow-up visits.
