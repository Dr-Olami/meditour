## Overview

If you or someone you love has just been told the word "leukaemia," you are probably feeling overwhelmed, frightened and full of questions. That is completely natural. A leukaemia diagnosis turns your life upside down overnight, and the road ahead can look daunting. This guide is here to walk you through what treatment in Bangalore actually involves, so you can make decisions with clarity rather than fear.

Leukaemia is a cancer of your blood-forming tissues, including your bone marrow and lymphatic system. It is broadly divided into acute and chronic forms, and each type needs its own tailored strategy. Your treatment will typically combine induction chemotherapy to push the disease into remission, consolidation therapy to deepen that response, and maintenance therapy or a stem cell transplant to keep the disease under control long term.

Bangalore's leading cancer hospitals follow internationally recognised protocols from the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) and the European LeukemiaNet. You will receive the same chemotherapy regimens, targeted therapies (such as tyrosine kinase inhibitors for CML) and immunotherapies available in the West, but at a significantly lower cost. You will also benefit from multidisciplinary tumour boards, modern chemotherapy day-care units, round-the-clock transfusion support and English-speaking care teams who understand what international families go through.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Leukaemia treatment in India is designed for you if you have received a confirmed diagnosis of acute or chronic leukaemia and you are seeking affordable, high-quality care. Suitable candidates include those with AML, ALL, CML or CLL who have adequate organ function to tolerate chemotherapy or targeted agents and a performance status of ECOG 0–3.

If you have high-risk cytogenetics or relapsed disease and you need a stem cell transplant, you are also well served here. Bangalore centres perform both autologous and allogeneic transplants with experienced donor registries and HLA typing services. If you face long waiting lists, limited access to novel agents or prohibitive costs in your home country, India offers a practical alternative.

It is important that you have realistic expectations about the multi-cycle, long-duration nature of this pathway. Leukaemia treatment is not a single event but a series of carefully timed interventions over many months. You should be prepared for extended stays, repeated blood tests and the possibility of inpatient admission for complications. A supportive attendant is strongly recommended, particularly during induction, when your risk of infection and bleeding is highest.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Before your treatment begins, you will undergo a comprehensive baseline assessment. This includes a repeat bone marrow biopsy with cytogenetics and molecular profiling, a full blood panel, coagulation studies, viral serology, cardiac evaluation with echocardiography or ECG, and a PET-CT or CT chest to rule out hidden infection. Dental and infectious disease screening is also performed so that any sources of infection can be treated before chemotherapy lowers your immunity.

You will be counselled about fertility preservation, side effects and the expected timeline. A central venous catheter is typically inserted at the start of your treatment to allow repeated blood draws and infusions without repeated needle sticks, which saves you discomfort and protects your veins. ## Procedure Details

Your treatment journey begins with a precise diagnostic workup. Your haematologist will arrange a bone marrow biopsy, flow cytometry, cytogenetics and molecular testing to pin down the exact subtype and risk category of your leukaemia. This stratification is critical because it determines which induction regimen is right for you. Induction is usually given in hospital over four to six weeks, with intensive monitoring for falling blood counts and infections.

Once you achieve remission, you will move on to consolidation cycles. These are shorter inpatient stays or day-care infusions spaced several weeks apart. If your disease is high-risk or has relapsed, your plan may include a stem cell transplant, CAR-T cell therapy or newer agents such as bispecific antibodies and FLT3 inhibitors.

The full treatment pathway can span four to twelve months, depending on your subtype, how you respond to therapy and whether a transplant is indicated. Throughout every phase, supportive care is woven into your plan. That means blood product transfusions, antibiotics, anti-sickness medication and nutritional support to keep you safe and as comfortable as possible.

## What Happens During the Procedure

Your induction chemotherapy is administered in a private hospital room with continuous monitoring. The specific drugs depend on your subtype. If you have AML, you typically receive a 7+3 regimen of cytarabine and an anthracycline. If you have ALL, you will have a multi-drug induction over four to six weeks. If you have CML or CLL, you usually begin oral targeted therapy (imatinib or ibrutinib) as an outpatient with regular monitoring.

During the nadir period, when your blood counts drop to their lowest, you will be monitored daily for fever, bleeding and signs of infection. Neutropenic precautions include a protected room, filtered air, prophylactic antibiotics and antifungals. Blood and platelet transfusions are given as needed to maintain safe counts. Once your counts recover and a day-14 or day-28 marrow confirms remission, you are discharged to await the next consolidation cycle. Each cycle follows a similar pattern of infusion, nadir monitoring and recovery, spaced three to four weeks apart.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

After each cycle, you will remain in Bangalore for two to four weeks of outpatient monitoring. Your blood counts are checked twice weekly, and transfusions or antibiotics are administered in the day-care unit as required. Side effects such as mouth sores, nausea and fatigue are managed with supportive medications, and your nutrition is optimised with the help of a dietitian who understands what cancer treatment does to your appetite.

Once your counts stabilise and you are clinically well, you may return home between cycles or continue maintenance therapy locally. If you are a transplant patient, your recovery period is longer, with strict infection precautions for three to six months. You will need to avoid crowds, practise meticulous hand hygiene and report any fever immediately. Gradual resumption of activity is encouraged, and most people return to near-normal routines within six to twelve months of completing therapy.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

Outcomes for leukaemia treatment in Bangalore are comparable to international benchmarks, though no centre can promise a cure or a guaranteed result. If you have acute myeloid leukaemia and achieve complete remission after induction, your 5-year survival is around 35–45% overall, rising to 50–60% if you undergo allogeneic transplant in first remission. If you have adult acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, modern protocols offer a 5-year survival of 40–50%. If you have CML and take tyrosine kinase inhibitors, you can expect a near-normal life expectancy with 10-year survival exceeding 85%.

Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia, though not curable, is highly manageable, with 10-year survival rates of 60–80% depending on your stage and genetics. Relapse remains a risk, particularly in high-risk subtypes, and is managed with salvage therapy, transplant or novel immunotherapies. Long-term surveillance with periodic bone marrow exams and molecular monitoring ensures early detection of any recurrence.

## Follow-Up Care After Returning Home

After you return home, you will continue maintenance or surveillance under a local haematologist. If you have CML, this means regular PCR monitoring of your BCR-ABL transcripts. If you have AML or ALL, your bone marrow biopsies are performed every three to six months for the first two years, then annually. Your Bangalore team remains available for tele-consultation and reviews all results shared by your local physician.

You are advised to return to Bangalore annually for a comprehensive reassessment, or sooner if relapse is suspected. Vaccinations are re-administered once your immune recovery is confirmed, typically six to twelve months post-transplant. This shared-care model ensures continuity while minimising your travel burden.

## Alternatives to Consider

You may also consider a stem cell transplant as a definitive option for high-risk or relapsed leukaemia, particularly an allogeneic transplant when a matched donor is available. CAR-T cell therapy is emerging as an alternative for relapsed B-ALL, though it is available at select centres and at a higher cost than standard chemotherapy.

For chronic forms such as CML, lifelong oral targeted therapy may suffice without the need for a transplant. In some cases, you may choose to receive induction in your home country and travel to India only for the transplant component. Up to two attendants may accompany you on attendant visas, and having a trusted family member or friend by your side makes an enormous difference during the hardest weeks.

Because leukaemia therapy spans several months, you may alternate between stays in Bangalore for active treatment and periods at home between cycles.
