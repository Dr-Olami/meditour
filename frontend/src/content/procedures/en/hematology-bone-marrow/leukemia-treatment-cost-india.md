## Overview

Leukemia is a cancer of the blood-forming tissues, including bone marrow and the lymphatic system. It is broadly classified into acute and chronic forms, each requiring a tailored treatment strategy. Treatment typically combines induction chemotherapy to achieve remission, consolidation therapy to deepen the response, and maintenance or stem cell transplant to secure long-term cure.

India's leading cancer hospitals in Bangalore follow internationally recognised protocols such as those from the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) and the European LeukemiaNet. Patients receive the same chemotherapy regimens, targeted therapies (such as tyrosine kinase inhibitors for CML) and immunotherapies available in the West, but at significantly lower cost.

International patients choose Bangalore for its multidisciplinary tumour boards, modern chemotherapy day-care units, 24/7 transfusion support and English-speaking care teams. The city's hospitals also offer seamless coordination between medical oncology, haematology and bone marrow transplant services for patients who need escalation of care.

## Procedure Details

Leukemia treatment begins with a precise diagnostic workup including bone marrow biopsy, flow cytometry, cytogenetics and molecular testing to determine the subtype and risk category. This stratification guides the choice of induction regimen, which is administered in hospital over 4–6 weeks with intensive monitoring for cytopenias and infections.

Once remission is achieved, patients move to consolidation cycles — shorter inpatient stays or day-care infusions spaced several weeks apart. For high-risk or relapsed disease, the treatment plan may incorporate a stem cell transplant, CAR-T cell therapy or novel agents such as bispecific antibodies and FLT3 inhibitors.

The full treatment pathway can span 4–12 months, with the exact duration determined by the leukaemia subtype, response to therapy and whether transplant is indicated. Throughout, supportive care including blood product transfusions, antibiotics, antiemetics and nutritional support is integral to maintaining patient safety and quality of life.

## Travel & Visa

Patients travelling for leukemia treatment require a Medical Visa, which can be issued for up to one year with multiple entries to accommodate the long, multi-cycle nature of the treatment. Up to two attendants may accompany the patient on attendant visas.

Because leukemia therapy spans several months, many patients alternate between stays in Bangalore for active treatment and periods at home between cycles.
