## Overview

If your child or someone you love has thalassemia, you already know the rhythm it imposes on life — the regular hospital visits for blood transfusions, the daily chelation medications, the fatigue, the worry about what the future holds. You may be wondering whether there is a way to break free from this cycle. There is. A bone marrow transplant can offer a cure, and Bangalore has become one of the world's leading centres for this procedure.

Thalassemia is an inherited blood disorder in which your body produces abnormal haemoglobin, leading to chronic anaemia. It ranges from mild (thalassemia minor, which may not need treatment) to severe and transfusion-dependent (thalassemia major). Without proper management, thalassemia major causes growth failure, bone deformities, organ damage from iron overload and a significantly reduced lifespan. Treatment ranges from lifelong regular blood transfusions combined with iron chelation therapy to a curative bone marrow or stem cell transplant.

Bangalore's JCI-accredited hospitals offer both comprehensive transfusion-and-chelation programmes and curative transplant options. These centres perform hundreds of thalassemia transplants each year, supported by experienced paediatric and adult haematology teams, advanced HLA typing laboratories and dedicated transplant units. You will find internationally trained haematologists, English-speaking staff and dedicated international patient services — all at 70–90% lower cost than Western pricing.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Thalassemia treatment in India is suitable for you or your child across the severity spectrum. Children and adults with confirmed thalassemia major or transfusion-dependent intermedia are the primary candidates for curative transplant evaluation. If you require regular transfusions every 2–4 weeks and have a compatible HLA-matched sibling donor, you are an ideal candidate — particularly if you are young and have received consistent iron chelation therapy.

If you do not have a matched sibling, unrelated donor registries or haploidentical (half-matched) transplant may be considered, though these carry higher risks of graft failure and graft-versus-host disease. If you have severe iron-overload-related cardiac or hepatic damage, you may not tolerate conditioning chemotherapy and may be better managed with optimised transfusion and chelation programmes. A comprehensive pre-transplant evaluation in Bangalore will determine the safest and most effective pathway for you.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Preparation begins in your home country. You should gather recent medical records including haemoglobin electrophoresis, transfusion history, serum ferritin levels, cardiac T2* MRI for iron assessment and liver function tests. These will be shared with the Bangalore transplant team for a remote opinion before you travel.

Once you arrive, a full in-person workup is conducted over 5–7 days, covering cardiac evaluation with echocardiography, endocrine assessment, infectious disease screening and detailed HLA typing of you and your potential donors. Your donor will also undergo a complete medical assessment to confirm fitness for stem cell collection. You and your family will be counselled on the conditioning regimen, expected side effects and the transplant timeline. A central venous catheter will be planned for access. Psychological support is provided, as the transplant process is demanding for both you and your family — particularly if the patient is a young child.

## Procedure Details

Your treatment will be tailored to the severity of your condition. If you have thalassemia minor, you may need no treatment beyond folic acid supplementation and routine monitoring. If you or your child has thalassemia major or transfusion-dependent intermedia, the mainstay is regular red cell transfusions every 2–4 weeks to keep haoglobin above 9–10 g/dL, combined with iron chelation therapy (deferasirox, deferiprone or deferoxamine) to prevent iron overload in the heart, liver and endocrine glands. Without effective chelation, transfusional iron accumulation causes cardiomyopathy, liver fibrosis and diabetes — the leading causes of mortality in thalassemia major.

If you are seeking a cure, an allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplant is the only established curative option. The procedure begins with HLA typing of siblings or unrelated donors, followed by conditioning chemotherapy to suppress your marrow. Healthy donor stem cells are then infused intravenously and engraftment typically occurs within 10–21 days. The best outcomes are seen in children with an HLA-matched sibling donor who have received adequate iron chelation and have no severe organ damage. The inpatient stay spans 4–6 weeks in a HEPA-filtered transplant unit, followed by several weeks of close outpatient monitoring.

## What Happens During the Procedure

If you are having a curative transplant, you will be admitted to a HEPA-filtered transplant unit and begin conditioning chemotherapy, which typically lasts 5–7 days. This chemotherapy suppresses your own bone marrow to create space for donor cells and reduce the risk of graft rejection. The regimen is selected based on your age, iron overload status and organ function, with reduced-intensity protocols preferred if you have significant organ damage.

Once conditioning is complete, the donor stem cells are infused intravenously through your central line. The infusion itself takes 30–60 minutes and is similar to a blood transfusion. Over the following 10–21 days, the donor cells migrate to your bone marrow and begin producing healthy haemoglobin and red blood cells. During this engraftment period, you will be closely monitored for infections, bleeding and graft-versus-host disease. Your blood counts will be checked daily and supportive transfusions given as needed. Once engraftment is confirmed and you are clinically stable, you will transition to outpatient monitoring.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

After discharge from the transplant unit, you will remain in Bangalore for 2–4 weeks of outpatient follow-up. Your blood counts will be monitored twice weekly, and immunosuppressive medications adjusted based on your engraftment and any signs of graft-versus-host disease. Infection prophylaxis with antiviral, antifungal and antibacterial medications continues for several months. Iron chelation is typically discontinued once engraftment is stable and transfusions are no longer needed.

Physical activity is gradually reintroduced, starting with short walks and building up over weeks. Nutrition is guided by a dietitian to support your immune recovery. Most patients feel significantly better within 4–6 weeks as normal haemoglobin production resumes and transfusion dependence ends — for many families, this is an emotional milestone. Your treating team will provide a detailed discharge summary, medication schedule and follow-up plan before you are cleared to fly home, typically around week 6–10.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

Bangalore transplant centres report disease-free survival rates of 80–90% in well-chelated paediatric patients with an HLA-matched sibling donor, consistent with international benchmarks. A successful transplant eliminates transfusion dependence and halts iron accumulation, allowing normal growth and development in children. Most patients no longer require transfusions or chelation after engraftment stabilises, and organ function often improves as iron load gradually decreases.

Long-term considerations include surveillance for graft-versus-host disease and monitoring for endocrine effects such as growth delay, hypothyroidism or fertility issues related to conditioning chemotherapy. Regular follow-up with a local haematologist is essential for the first year. Adult transplant recipients and those with pre-existing organ damage may have somewhat lower survival rates, but outcomes remain strong for well-selected candidates. Gene therapy trials offer future promise but are not yet standard care.

## Follow-Up Care After Returning Home

After you return home, you should establish care with a local haematologist experienced in post-transplant management. Weekly blood counts are recommended for the first month, gradually reducing to monthly and then quarterly checks. Immunosuppressive medications are tapered under guidance from your Bangalore team, which remains available for tele-consultation. Vaccinations are restarted on a modified schedule beginning around six months post-transplant.

You should report any fever, rash, jaundice or persistent fatigue immediately, as these may indicate infection or graft-versus-host disease. Annual check-ups assessing organ function, iron status and donor chimerism help confirm long-term cure. Your Bangalore transplant coordinator will maintain contact for at least one year to support a smooth transition to home-country care.

## Alternatives to Consider

If you are not a candidate for transplant, or while you await a donor, optimised medical management remains the standard of care. Regular transfusion programmes combined with effective iron chelation (deferasirox, deferiprone or deferoxamine) can maintain good quality of life and prevent organ damage when started early and adhered to consistently. Luspatercept, a newer agent that improves red blood cell maturation, may reduce your transfusion burden.

Gene therapy is an emerging alternative that uses your own genetically modified stem cells, eliminating the need for a donor, but it is currently available only through clinical trials and at very high cost in Western centres. You should discuss all options with your Bangalore haematology team, who can provide a balanced recommendation based on your disease severity, donor availability, age and organ function.
