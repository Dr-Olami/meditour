## Overview

Thalassemia is an inherited blood disorder in which the body produces abnormal haemoglobin, leading to chronic anaemia that ranges from mild (thalassemia minor) to severe and transfusion-dependent (thalassemia major). Treatment ranges from lifelong regular blood transfusions combined with iron chelation therapy to a curative bone marrow or stem cell transplant. Without proper management, thalassemia major leads to growth failure, organ damage from iron overload and a significantly reduced lifespan.

India has become a leading destination for thalassemia care, with JCI-accredited hospitals in Bangalore offering both comprehensive transfusion-and-chelation programmes and curative transplant options. The country performs hundreds of thalassemia transplants each year, supported by experienced paediatric and adult haematology teams, advanced HLA typing laboratories and dedicated transplant units.

International patients choose Bangalore for its high success rates, internationally trained haematologists and cost savings of 70–90% compared to Western pricing. The combination of clinical excellence, English-speaking staff and dedicated international patient services makes India an attractive option for families facing long waiting lists or prohibitive costs at home.

## Procedure Details

Thalassemia treatment is tailored to disease severity. For thalassemia minor, no treatment is usually required. For thalassemia major and transfusion-dependent intermedia, the mainstay is regular red cell transfusions every 2–4 weeks to maintain haemoglobin above 9–10 g/dL, combined with iron chelation therapy (deferasirox, deferiprone or deferoxamine) to prevent iron overload in the heart, liver and endocrine glands.

For patients seeking a cure, an allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplant is the only established curative option. The procedure begins with HLA typing of siblings or unrelated donors, followed by conditioning chemotherapy to suppress the patient's marrow. Healthy donor stem cells are then infused intravenously and engraftment typically occurs within 10–21 days. The best outcomes are seen in paediatric patients with an HLA-matched sibling donor who have received adequate iron chelation.

The overall inpatient stay for a transplant spans 4–6 weeks in a HEPA-filtered transplant unit, followed by several weeks of close outpatient monitoring. Gene therapy approaches are emerging but remain experimental and are not yet widely offered as a standard package.

## Travel & Visa

International patients travelling for thalassemia treatment require a Medical Visa (e-Medical or regular Medical Visa), which is granted to the patient and up to two attendants. The visa is typically valid for up to one year with multiple entries, allowing for pre-transplant assessment, the procedure itself and follow-up visits.
