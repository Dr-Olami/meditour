## Overview

CAR-T cell therapy (chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy) is an advanced form of cellular immunotherapy in which a patient's own T cells are genetically engineered to recognise and attack cancer cells. It is used to treat relapsed or refractory B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, certain non-Hodgkin lymphomas (diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma) and multiple myeloma. For patients who have exhausted standard therapies, CAR-T offers a chance at durable remission where none previously existed.

India has rapidly developed CAR-T cell therapy capability, with Bangalore hospitals offering both commercially approved products and locally manufactured investigational CAR-T constructs at a fraction of the cost charged in the United States or Europe. The country is emerging as a hub for affordable cellular therapy, supported by experienced haematology-oncology teams, GMP-certified cell processing facilities and dedicated immune effector cell units.

International patients choose Bangalore for its access to cutting-edge CAR-T therapy, internationally trained specialists and cost savings of 70–90% compared to Western pricing. The combination of clinical excellence, English-speaking staff and dedicated international patient services makes India an attractive option for patients facing prohibitive costs at home.

## Procedure Details

CAR-T cell therapy begins with leukapheresis, where the patient's T cells are collected from the bloodstream over 3–4 hours. These cells are then sent to a GMP-certified laboratory where they are genetically engineered to express a chimeric antigen receptor targeting a specific cancer antigen (CD19 for leukaemia and lymphoma, BCMA for myeloma). The engineered cells are expanded over 2–4 weeks and cryopreserved.

Once the CAR-T cells are ready, the patient is admitted for lymphodepleting chemotherapy (typically fludarabine and cyclophosphamide) over 3–5 days to create an immune environment that supports CAR-T cell expansion. The engineered cells are then infused intravenously as a single dose. Over the following 1–2 weeks the CAR-T cells multiply and attack cancer cells, which can trigger cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS).

The overall inpatient stay spans 2–4 weeks with close monitoring in a specialised immune effector cell unit, followed by several weeks of outpatient surveillance. Management of CRS and ICANS with tocilizumab and corticosteroids is a core component of the programme.

## Travel & Visa

International patients travelling for CAR-T cell therapy require a Medical Visa (e-Medical or regular Medical Visa), which is granted to the patient and up to two attendants. The visa is typically valid for up to one year with multiple entries, allowing for leukapheresis, cell manufacturing time, the infusion itself and follow-up visits.
