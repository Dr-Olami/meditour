## Overview

An allogeneic stem cell transplant is a procedure in which blood-forming stem cells from a donor — either an HLA-matched sibling, a matched unrelated donor or a haploidentical family member — are infused into a patient after conditioning chemotherapy. It is used to treat acute leukaemia, myelodysplastic syndromes, aplastic anaemia, inherited bone marrow disorders and certain lymphomas. The donor cells rebuild a healthy blood and immune system and exert a graft-versus-leukaemia effect that helps eradicate residual cancer cells.

India has emerged as a leading destination for allogeneic transplants, with JCI-accredited hospitals in Bangalore offering comprehensive transplant programmes at a fraction of the cost charged in the United States, Europe or the Middle East. The country performs thousands of allogeneic transplants each year across matched-sibling, unrelated and haploidentical modalities.

International patients choose Bangalore for its experienced transplant physicians, internationally trained haematologists, advanced HEPA-filtered transplant units and dedicated international patient services. The combination of clinical excellence, English-speaking staff and significant cost savings makes India an attractive option for patients facing long waiting times or prohibitive costs at home.

## Procedure Details

An allogeneic transplant begins with a thorough pre-transplant evaluation, including blood tests, imaging, cardiac and pulmonary assessment, and HLA typing to identify the best donor. Once a donor is confirmed, conditioning chemotherapy (and sometimes total body irradiation) is administered over several days to destroy diseased marrow and suppress the immune system.

Donor stem cells are then infused intravenously, much like a blood transfusion. These cells migrate to the bone marrow cavities where they begin producing new blood cells — a process called engraftment that typically takes 10–21 days. During this vulnerable period the patient stays in a specialised transplant room with strict infection control measures, receiving supportive care including antibiotics, blood transfusions and immunosuppressive drugs to prevent graft-versus-host disease.

The overall procedure spans 4–6 weeks of inpatient care, followed by several weeks of close outpatient monitoring. The exact protocol, donor source and conditioning intensity are tailored to each patient's diagnosis and clinical status.

## Travel & Visa

International patients travelling for an allogeneic stem cell transplant require a Medical Visa (e-Medical or regular Medical Visa), which is granted to the patient and up to two attendants. The visa is typically valid for up to one year with multiple entries, allowing for pre-transplant assessment, the procedure itself and follow-up visits.
