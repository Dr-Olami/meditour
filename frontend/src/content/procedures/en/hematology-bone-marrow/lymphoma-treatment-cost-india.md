## Overview

Lymphoma is a cancer of the lymphatic system that arises from lymphocytes, the body's infection-fighting white blood cells. It is divided into two main categories — Hodgkin lymphoma and non-Hodgkin lymphoma — each with distinct subtypes, prognoses and treatment pathways. Most lymphomas are highly treatable, and many are curable with modern combination therapy.

Bangalore's cancer hospitals offer the full spectrum of lymphoma care, from first-line regimens such as ABVD and R-CHOP to advanced immunotherapies, radiotherapy and autologous stem cell transplant for relapsed disease. Treatment is delivered by multidisciplinary teams including haemato-oncologists, radiation oncologists and nuclear medicine specialists.

International patients choose Bangalore for its protocol-driven care, modern day-care chemotherapy units and significant cost savings. The same monoclonal antibodies and chemotherapy agents used in Western centres are available at a fraction of the price, making high-quality lymphoma treatment accessible to patients from across Africa, the Middle East and South Asia.

## Procedure Details

Lymphoma treatment begins with an accurate diagnosis through excision biopsy, immunohistochemistry, flow cytometry and PET-CT staging. The subtype and stage determine the treatment plan, which may involve 4–8 cycles of chemotherapy with or without immunotherapy (rituximab, brentuximab or polatuzumab) and consolidative radiotherapy for localised disease.

Most cycles are delivered as day-care infusions every 2–3 weeks, with each visit lasting a few hours to a full day. Inpatient admission is reserved for complex regimens, severe cytopenias or infection management. Inter-cycle monitoring includes blood counts, infection surveillance and interim PET-CT to assess response.

For relapsed or refractory lymphoma, the pathway may escalate to salvage chemotherapy followed by autologous stem cell transplant or, increasingly, to CAR-T cell therapy. The overall treatment duration ranges from 3–9 months, with surveillance continuing for several years after completion to detect any recurrence early.

## Travel & Visa

Patients travelling for lymphoma treatment require a Medical Visa, typically valid for up to one year with multiple entries to accommodate the multi-cycle nature of therapy. Up to two attendants may accompany the patient.

Because lymphoma treatment spans several months with cycles spaced 2–3 weeks apart, many patients choose to stay in Bangalore for the full duration or alternate between India and home.
