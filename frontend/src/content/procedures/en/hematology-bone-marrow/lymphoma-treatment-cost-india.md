## Overview

Hearing the word "lymphoma" for the first time can leave you reeling. You may be sitting with a biopsy report you barely understand, trying to make sense of what comes next. That fear and uncertainty are completely natural — but you should know that lymphoma is one of the most treatable cancers in modern medicine, and many forms are curable. This guide will walk you through what treatment in Bangalore actually involves, so you can plan your next steps with clarity.

Lymphoma is a cancer of your lymphatic system — the network of nodes and vessels that helps your body fight infection. It begins in your white blood cells called lymphocytes. There are two broad categories: Hodgkin lymphoma and non-Hodgkin lymphoma, each with its own subtypes and treatment pathways. Your haematologist will identify which type you have and design a plan specifically for you.

Bangalore's cancer hospitals offer the full spectrum of lymphoma care, from first-line regimens such as ABVD and R-CHOP to advanced immunotherapies, radiotherapy and autologous stem cell transplant for relapsed disease. You will be treated by multidisciplinary teams including haemato-oncologists, radiation oncologists and nuclear medicine specialists. The same monoclonal antibodies and chemotherapy agents used in Western centres are available at a fraction of the price, which is why so many patients from Africa, the Middle East and South Asia choose Bangalore for their treatment.

## Who Is This Procedure For?

Lymphoma treatment in India is designed for you if you have a confirmed diagnosis of Hodgkin or non-Hodgkin lymphoma and you need systemic therapy. You should have adequate organ function to tolerate chemotherapy or immunotherapy, a performance status of ECOG 0–3, and no active uncontrolled infection at the start of treatment.

Whether you are newly diagnosed and seeking affordable first-line therapy or you have relapsed disease requiring salvage chemotherapy or autologous transplant, Bangalore centres are well equipped to help. All major subtypes are treated here, including diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma and classical Hodgkin lymphoma.

If you face limited access to immunotherapy agents, long waiting lists for radiotherapy or costs that are prohibitive in your home country, India offers a practical solution. It is important that you understand the multi-cycle nature of this pathway and are prepared for repeated visits, interim imaging and the possibility of inpatient admission for complications. A companion is strongly recommended, particularly during the early cycles when infusion reactions and low blood counts are most likely.

## Pre-Operative Preparation

Before your treatment begins, you will undergo a thorough staging workup. This includes a review of your original biopsy slides by the Bangalore pathology team, a PET-CT scan from neck to pelvis, a bone marrow biopsy for certain subtypes, and a full blood panel including lactate dehydrogenase and beta-2 microglobulin. If you are going to receive an anthracycline-based regimen, a cardiac assessment with echocardiography will be performed. Hepatitis B, C and HIV screening is carried out before rituximab.

You will be counselled about potential side effects, fertility preservation and the expected treatment timeline. A portacath or PICC line will be inserted to facilitate repeated infusions — this saves you from repeated needle sticks and protects your veins over the many cycles ahead. Khan Meditour schedules all your pre-treatment assessments so that staging is completed within 5–7 days, allowing your first cycle to begin without delay. You should also complete dental checks and vaccinations before starting therapy.

## Procedure Details

Your treatment journey begins with an accurate diagnosis. Your team will arrange an excision biopsy, immunohistochemistry, flow cytometry and a PET-CT scan to stage your disease. This staging is critical — it tells your doctor how far the lymphoma has spread and determines which treatment plan is right for you. You can expect this workup to take about a week.

Your treatment plan may involve 4–8 cycles of chemotherapy, with or without immunotherapy (rituximab, brentuximab or polatuzumab) and consolidative radiotherapy for localised disease. Most cycles are delivered as day-care infusions every 2–3 weeks, with each visit lasting a few hours to a full day. You will not need to stay in hospital overnight for most cycles — you can return to your accommodation afterwards. Inpatient admission is reserved for complex regimens, severe blood count drops or infection management.

Between cycles, your blood counts will be checked and an interim PET-CT after 2–3 cycles will assess how well you are responding. If your lymphoma is relapsed or refractory, your plan may escalate to salvage chemotherapy followed by an autologous stem cell transplant or, increasingly, CAR-T cell therapy. The overall treatment duration ranges from 3–9 months, with surveillance continuing for several years afterwards.

## What Happens During the Procedure

Most lymphoma chemotherapy is delivered in a comfortable day-care unit. If you have Hodgkin lymphoma, the standard regimen is ABVD (doxorubicin, bleomycin, vinblastine, dacarbazine) given every two weeks for 2–6 cycles, or BEACOPP for advanced disease. If you have non-Hodgkin lymphoma, you will typically receive R-CHOP (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, prednisolone) every three weeks for 6–8 cycles.

**Will the infusion hurt?** The first dose of rituximab is given slowly with pre-medication to prevent infusion reactions. You may feel some chills or flushing, but these are usually manageable. Subsequent doses are much better tolerated. Each cycle takes a few hours, after which you return to your accommodation. Your blood counts will be checked midway between cycles, and an interim PET-CT after 2–3 cycles will show how well you are responding. If your counts are adequate, the next cycle proceeds on schedule. Consolidative radiotherapy, where indicated, is delivered daily over 2–4 weeks after chemotherapy is completed.

## Post-Operative Care & Recovery

Between cycles, you will remain in Bangalore for 2–3 weeks of outpatient monitoring. Your blood counts will be checked twice weekly, and growth factor injections may be given to support your recovery if your neutrophil count drops. Side effects such as fatigue, nausea and neuropathy will be managed with supportive medications, and a dietitian will help you maintain your weight and strength.

After completing all cycles, a final PET-CT confirms your response. If you achieve a complete metabolic response, you will enter surveillance. If there is residual disease, you may need radiotherapy or a change of regimen. For transplant patients, recovery takes longer, with infection precautions maintained for 3–6 months. Most people return to normal activities within 3–6 months of completing standard chemotherapy, though fatigue may persist for up to a year — be patient with yourself.

## Long-Term Outcomes & Success Rates

Outcomes for lymphoma treatment in Bangalore are comparable to international standards, though no centre can promise a cure or a guaranteed result. Hodgkin lymphoma has an overall 5-year survival of 85–95% for early-stage disease and 70–80% for advanced stage — among the highest cure rates in all of oncology. Non-Hodgkin lymphoma outcomes vary by subtype: diffuse large B-cell lymphoma achieves a 5-year survival of 60–70% with R-CHOP, while follicular lymphoma has a median survival exceeding 15 years with modern management.

Relapse occurs in 20–30% of people with aggressive subtypes and is managed with salvage therapy and autologous transplant, which achieves long-term remission in 40–50% of eligible patients. Long-term surveillance with periodic PET-CT and clinical examination ensures early detection of any recurrence. You will also be monitored for late effects including secondary malignancies, cardiac toxicity and infertility, with appropriate screening and support.

## Follow-Up Care After Returning Home

After you return home, you will continue surveillance under a local haematologist or oncologist. Follow-up typically involves clinical examination and blood tests every 3 months for the first two years, then every 6 months for three years, and annually thereafter. PET-CT scans are performed at 6 and 12 months, then only if clinically indicated.

Your Bangalore team remains available for tele-consultation and will review any imaging and blood results shared by your local physician. You are advised to return annually for a comprehensive review, or sooner if new symptoms arise. This shared-care model ensures continuity while minimising your need for frequent international travel.

## Alternatives to Consider

If your lymphoma is relapsed or refractory, an autologous stem cell transplant is the standard salvage approach and is available at all major Bangalore transplant centres. CAR-T cell therapy is an emerging option for certain B-cell lymphomas, offered at select hospitals, though at a higher cost than conventional salvage therapy.

For early-stage Hodgkin lymphoma, radiotherapy alone or combined with limited chemotherapy may be sufficient. You may also choose to receive initial cycles in your home country and travel to India only for the transplant component if relapse occurs.
