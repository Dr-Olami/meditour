## Overview

An autologous stem cell transplant is a procedure in which a patient's own blood-forming stem cells are collected, frozen and reinfused after high-dose chemotherapy. It is primarily used to treat multiple myeloma, relapsed or high-risk Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and selected solid tumours such as neuroblastoma. The high-dose conditioning destroys cancer cells, and the stored stem cells rescue the bone marrow from otherwise lethal toxicity.

India has emerged as a leading destination for autologous transplants, with JCI-accredited hospitals in Bangalore offering comprehensive transplant programmes at a fraction of the cost charged in the United States, Europe or the Middle East. The country performs thousands of autologous transplants each year with outcomes comparable to international benchmarks.

International patients choose Bangalore for its experienced transplant physicians, internationally trained haematologists, advanced HEPA-filtered transplant units and dedicated international patient services. The combination of clinical excellence, English-speaking staff and significant cost savings makes India an attractive option for patients facing long waiting times or prohibitive costs at home.

## Procedure Details

An autologous transplant begins with stem cell mobilisation, where the patient receives chemotherapy and/or growth factors (G-CSF) to push stem cells from the marrow into the bloodstream. These cells are then collected by apheresis over 1–3 days and cryopreserved. A minimum yield of 2×10⁶ CD34+ cells/kg is typically required.

Once collection is complete, the patient is admitted for conditioning — high-dose chemotherapy (such as BEAM for lymphoma or melphalan for myeloma) administered over several days. The frozen stem cells are then thawed and reinfused intravenously, much like a blood transfusion. Engraftment typically occurs within 10–14 days, during which the patient stays in a specialised transplant room with strict infection control measures.

The overall procedure spans 2–4 weeks of inpatient care, followed by several weeks of close outpatient monitoring. Because the patient receives their own cells, there is no risk of graft-versus-host disease, making autologous transplants safer and faster to recover from than allogeneic procedures.

## Travel & Visa

International patients travelling for an autologous stem cell transplant require a Medical Visa (e-Medical or regular Medical Visa), which is granted to the patient and up to two attendants. The visa is typically valid for up to one year with multiple entries, allowing for pre-transplant assessment, the procedure itself and follow-up visits.
