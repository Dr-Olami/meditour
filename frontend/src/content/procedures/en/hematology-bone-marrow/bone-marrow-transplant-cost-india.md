## Overview

A bone marrow transplant (BMT) is a specialised procedure that replaces damaged or diseased bone marrow with healthy blood-forming stem cells. It is used to treat a range of life-threatening haematological conditions including leukaemia, lymphoma, multiple myeloma, aplastic anaemia, thalassemia and sickle cell disease. The transplant allows patients to receive high-dose chemotherapy or radiation and then rebuild a healthy blood and immune system.

India has emerged as a leading destination for bone marrow transplants, with JCI-accredited hospitals in Bangalore offering comprehensive transplant programmes at a fraction of the cost charged in the United States, Europe or the Middle East. The country performs thousands of transplants each year across autologous, allogeneic and haploidentical modalities.

International patients choose Bangalore for its experienced transplant physicians, internationally trained haematologists, advanced HEPA-filtered transplant units and dedicated international patient services. The combination of clinical excellence, English-speaking staff and significant cost savings makes India an attractive option for patients facing long waiting times or prohibitive costs at home.

## Procedure Details

A bone marrow transplant begins with a thorough pre-transplant evaluation, including blood tests, imaging, cardiac and pulmonary assessment, and donor matching (HLA typing) for allogeneic procedures. Once the patient is cleared, conditioning chemotherapy (and sometimes total body irradiation) is administered over several days to destroy diseased marrow and suppress the immune system.

Healthy stem cells are then infused intravenously, much like a blood transfusion. These cells migrate to the bone marrow cavities where they begin producing new blood cells — a process called engraftment that typically takes 10–21 days. During this vulnerable period the patient stays in a specialised transplant room with strict infection control measures, receiving supportive care including antibiotics, blood transfusions and nutritional support.

The overall procedure spans 4–8 weeks of inpatient care, followed by several weeks of close outpatient monitoring. The exact protocol, donor source (related, unrelated or haploidentical) and conditioning intensity are tailored to each patient's diagnosis and clinical status.

## Travel & Visa

International patients travelling for a bone marrow transplant require a Medical Visa (e-Medical or regular Medical Visa), which is granted to the patient and up to two attendants. The visa is typically valid for up to one year with multiple entries, allowing for pre-transplant assessment, the procedure itself and follow-up visits.

Patients should arrive in Bangalore at least one week before the scheduled transplant date to complete pre-transplant workup and conditioning planning.
