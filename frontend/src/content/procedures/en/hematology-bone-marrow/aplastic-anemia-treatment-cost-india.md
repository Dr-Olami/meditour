## Overview

Aplastic anaemia is a rare and serious condition in which the bone marrow fails to produce enough red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets, leading to fatigue, infections and bleeding. It may be acquired (often immune-mediated) or inherited. Treatment ranges from supportive care (transfusions and antibiotics) to immunosuppressive therapy with antithymocyte globulin (ATG) and cyclosporine, and ultimately a curative allogeneic stem cell transplant for eligible patients.

India has become a leading destination for aplastic anaemia care, with JCI-accredited hospitals in Bangalore offering both immunosuppressive therapy and curative transplant options. The country performs a significant number of aplastic anaemia transplants each year, supported by experienced haematology teams, advanced HLA typing laboratories and dedicated transplant units.

## Procedure Details

Aplastic anaemia treatment is tailored to severity and donor availability. For non-severe disease, supportive care with transfusions and growth factors may suffice. For severe or very severe aplastic anaemia, the two main definitive treatments are immunosuppressive therapy and allogeneic stem cell transplant.

Immunosuppressive therapy combines horse or rabbit ATG with cyclosporine over several days of inpatient care, followed by months of outpatient monitoring. Response is typically assessed at 3–6 months. For younger patients (under 40) with an HLA-matched sibling donor, an upfront allogeneic transplant is preferred and curative. The procedure begins with conditioning chemotherapy (typically cyclophosphamide-based), followed by infusion of donor stem cells and engraftment within 10–21 days.

The overall inpatient stay for a transplant spans 4–6 weeks in a HEPA-filtered transplant unit, followed by several weeks of close outpatient monitoring. Patients receiving immunosuppressive therapy alone typically stay 2–4 weeks in hospital with ongoing outpatient follow-up.

The visa is typically valid for up to one year with multiple entries, allowing for pre-transplant assessment, the procedure itself and follow-up visits.
