## Overview

Sickle cell disease is an inherited haemoglobin disorder in which abnormal red blood cells become rigid and sickle-shaped, causing chronic haemolytic anaemia, vaso-occlusive pain crises and progressive organ damage. Treatment ranges from supportive care (hydration, pain management, blood transfusions and hydroxyurea) to disease-modifying therapies and a curative bone marrow or stem cell transplant. Without intervention, severe sickle cell disease leads to stroke, pulmonary hypertension and reduced life expectancy.

India has become a leading destination for sickle cell care, with JCI-accredited hospitals in Bangalore offering both comprehensive medical management and curative transplant options. The country performs a growing number of sickle cell transplants each year, supported by experienced paediatric and adult haematology teams, advanced HLA typing laboratories and dedicated transplant units.

International patients choose Bangalore for its high success rates, internationally trained haematologists and cost savings of 70–90% compared to Western pricing. The combination of clinical excellence, English-speaking staff and dedicated international patient services makes India an attractive option for families facing long waiting lists or prohibitive costs at home.

## Procedure Details

Sickle cell disease treatment is tailored to severity. For mild disease, supportive care with folic acid supplementation, hydration and pain management during crises is the mainstay. Disease-modifying therapy with hydroxyurea reduces crisis frequency and transfusion needs, while regular transfusion programmes are used for patients with stroke risk or severe complications. Newer agents such as crizanlizumab and voxelotor may be added in selected cases.

For patients seeking a cure, an allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplant is the only established curative option. The procedure begins with HLA typing of siblings or unrelated donors, followed by reduced-intensity or myeloablative conditioning chemotherapy. Healthy donor stem cells are then infused intravenously and engraftment typically occurs within 10–21 days. The best outcomes are seen in paediatric patients with an HLA-matched sibling donor and no prior stroke.

The overall inpatient stay for a transplant spans 4–6 weeks in a HEPA-filtered transplant unit, followed by several weeks of close outpatient monitoring. Gene therapy approaches such as lovo-cel and exa-cel are emerging globally but remain experimental and are not yet widely offered as a standard package.

International patients travelling for sickle cell disease treatment require a Medical Visa (e-Medical or regular Medical Visa), which is granted to the patient and up to two attendants. The visa is typically valid for up to one year with multiple entries, allowing for pre-transplant assessment, the procedure itself and follow-up visits.
