## Overview

SPARSH Hospital, Hennur Road is a 300-bed quaternary care facility and the **newest hospital on Khan Meditour's panel**, inaugurated on May 18, 2025 by Karnataka's Health Minister. It is the latest addition to the **SPARSH Group of Hospitals** — an established Karnataka network with over 1,600 beds across 8 hospitals, recognised for its orthopaedics, trauma and emergency-care legacy. Located on Hennur Main Road in North-East Bangalore, the hospital is strategically positioned to serve a rapidly growing corridor that previously lacked access to high-end quaternary care.

What makes SPARSH Hennur distinctive is its **technology-first design and the SPARSH Group's clinical legacy**. The hospital was built from the ground up with AI-powered diagnostics, robotic-assisted surgery, 3D printing and real-time analytics — technologies that older hospitals have had to retrofit. It is the **first hospital in India to earn IFEM Gold Level Certification for Emergency Care**, reflecting the SPARSH Group's strength in trauma and rapid-response medicine. With 50+ specialities, 50+ ICU beds, a dedicated BMT unit, PET-CT, LINAC and a state-of-the-art cath lab, it offers the newest infrastructure on Khan Meditour's panel, particularly strong for orthopaedics, oncology, cardiac sciences and neurosciences.

The hospital is **NABH accredited** (200 sanctioned beds per NABH certification; 300 beds per the hospital's launch announcement), ensuring adherence to national standards in patient safety, clinical governance and quality improvement. As a quaternary care centre, SPARSH Hennur offers advanced treatments across 50+ speciality departments, making it one of the most comprehensive healthcare facilities in North Bangalore. SPARSH Group is the first in India to earn **IFEM Gold Level Certification** for Emergency Care.

With over 2,500 direct and indirect employment opportunities generated (per the 2025 launch announcement), the hospital also contributes to academic medicine and research in the region. SPARSH Group plans to scale to nine hospitals with over 1,700 beds by the end of 2025.
